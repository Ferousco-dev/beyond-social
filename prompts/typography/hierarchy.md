Hierarchy in type comes from contrast between levels, not from many levels. Three
tiers, display, heading, body, plus a small caption, cover almost everything. Make
adjacent levels clearly different in size or weight so the jump is obvious.

Pairing: one family used well beats two used carelessly. If pairing, contrast
roles, a characterful display face with a neutral, highly legible body face, and
keep them tonally compatible. Align type to the baseline grid; set tighter
letter-spacing on large display sizes and normal spacing on body.

Why: readers navigate by scanning headings; weak contrast between levels forces
full reading. Overusing distinct styles fragments the page and undermines the
sense of one voice.

Example: a bold 38px display, medium 20px section headings, regular 16px body,
one accent for links. Counter-example: five heading sizes two pixels apart, two
display fonts fighting, and justified body text with rivers.
