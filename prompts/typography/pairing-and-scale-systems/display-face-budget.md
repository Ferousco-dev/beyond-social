A product should budget exactly one characterful display face, at most, and
spend it on the single moment that needs personality; every additional
"characterful" font invited into the system competes with the first and
dilutes the hierarchy.

- One display face for hero headlines and top-level marketing moments.
- One neutral, highly legible workhorse sans for everything else: UI, body
  copy, secondary headings, forms.
- Emphasis inside the workhorse face comes from weight and size, not from a
  third font.
- Treat any request for "just one more font for this section" as a hierarchy
  problem to solve with the existing two faces, not a font problem to solve by
  adding a third.

Why: personality in type comes from scarcity. A distinctive display face reads
as a deliberate brand choice only if it's rare in the product; once a second
or third decorative face appears, the reader can no longer tell which
typographic signal is meaningful, so the system's total expressive range
collapses even though it nominally has more fonts to work with.

Example: Canela Deck reserved for the homepage hero and campaign landing pages
only; Inter for every other headline, label, and body string in the product.

Counter-example: a display serif on the homepage, a different script font on
the pricing page, and a geometric sans on the blog, each chosen locally and
looking coherent nowhere.
