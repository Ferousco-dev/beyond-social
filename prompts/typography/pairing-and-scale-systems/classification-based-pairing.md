Pair typefaces from different classifications to signal role, not from the same
classification, which reads muddy rather than intentional.

- Serif heading and grotesque sans body (Canela headline, Inter body) reads
  editorial and premium.
- Slab serif heading and humanist sans body reads sturdy and product-forward.
- Grotesque heading and grotesque body only works with large weight or size
  contrast; otherwise the two blur together.
- Never pair two serifs, or two humanist sans, with similar proportions at
  similar sizes; the reader can't tell if the difference is intentional.

Why: classification carries connotation, serif reads authority and editorial,
grotesque reads neutral and systematic, slab reads sturdy and product-driven, so
a mismatched or same-classification pair either sends no signal or a discordant
one. Same-classification pairs also lack the structural contrast that lets a
reader parse "this is a different role" without conscious effort, forcing them
to rely on size alone to find the hierarchy.

Example: "Headline in Canela Deck 48px/1.1, body in Inter 16px/1.5," a
high-contrast serif against a low-contrast grotesque.

Counter-example: "Headline in Helvetica Neue Bold, body in Arial Regular,"
nearly identical letterforms from the same classification, which reads like a
font failed to load rather than a deliberate pairing choice.
