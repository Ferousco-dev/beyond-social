Type pairing sits on a spectrum from harmonious (low risk, low distinctiveness)
to high-contrast (higher risk, more brand signal); choose a point on that axis
deliberately, based on how differentiated the brand needs to be, not by default
caution.

- Harmonious end: one superfamily, or two faces with similar proportions and
  different weights only. Safe, fast to execute, low memorability.
- Middle: same classification, different personality, one geometric sans
  against one humanist sans. Some distinction without dissonance.
- Bold end: serif display against grotesque body, or a high-contrast didone
  against a monospace. Maximum memorability, more risk if x-heights and weights
  aren't tuned.
- A commodity SaaS dashboard should sit harmonious-to-middle; a challenger
  consumer brand or luxury vertical can afford the bold end.

Why: contrast is the mechanism that makes a pairing feel intentional rather
than accidental, but more contrast also means more surface area for the
pairing to go wrong, so the choice should track how much brand differentiation
the product actually needs, not just what looks striking in an isolated
mockup.

Example: a fintech dashboard pairs Inter Medium headings with Inter Regular
body, harmonious and low risk. A fashion DTC brand pairs Canela headlines with
a monospace for product specs, bold and high signal.

Counter-example: picking a bold, high-contrast pair for an enterprise B2B tool
because it looked striking in isolation, then finding it reads as unserious
next to dense data tables.
