The F-pattern's very first horizontal stroke, the top row of a text-led page,
gets the longest and most complete read of the entire page. The single
sentence occupying that row does more work than anything below it.

The recipe:

- Reserve the first row for the core value proposition stated as a complete
  claim, not a category label.
- Keep it under roughly 8-10 words so it resolves within the stroke's typical
  reading length before attention starts to drop off.
- Avoid company-name-first openings ("Acme helps teams..."); the first stroke
  gets wasted on branding the reader doesn't yet care about.
- Don't place a second, competing headline-weight line directly under it; it
  competes for the same stroke's attention budget instead of extending it.

Why: the first stroke is read close to fully because the reader hasn't yet
decided whether to bail. It's the one place on the page where near-complete
comprehension can be assumed, unlike every row that follows, where comprehension
degrades with distance from the top-left corner.

Example: "Cut deploy time from hours to minutes." as the literal first line on
the page.
Counter-example: "Welcome to Acme Cloud Platform" as the opening line. It
spends the one fully-read stroke on a fact, the company name, that the reader
already gets from the logo sitting right beside it.
