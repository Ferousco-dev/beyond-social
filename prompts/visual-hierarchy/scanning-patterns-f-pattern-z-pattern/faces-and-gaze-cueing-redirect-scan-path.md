A photographed or rendered face in a layout overrides both F- and Z-pattern
defaults. Human vision fixates on faces first, then follows the direction of
that face's gaze, so a person's eyeline in an image functions as a pointer
that can redirect the scan path toward or away from a CTA.

The recipe:

- If a hero image includes a person, angle their gaze toward the headline or
  CTA, not off-frame or in a direction that competes with page content.
- Never place a face gazing directly at the viewer right above or beside the
  primary CTA; direct gaze holds attention on the face itself rather than
  releasing it forward.
- Crop tight enough that the face is unambiguous, but leave visual space in
  the direction of the gaze for the eye to travel into; a face looking right
  needs room to its right.
- Use this to override quadrant weighting deliberately: a face in the weak
  fallow bottom-left quadrant looking up and right can pull attention out of
  the dead zone and into the terminal area.

Why: face fixation and gaze-following are pre-attentive, happening before
conscious layout scanning starts. They don't compete with F/Z patterns on
equal footing, they hijack the starting point of the scan before geometry
takes over.

Example: a customer-testimonial photo with the subject's gaze angled toward
the pull quote beside them.
Counter-example: a founder headshot staring straight at the camera, placed
directly next to the CTA button. It repeatedly pulls attention onto the face
and away from the button rather than through it.
