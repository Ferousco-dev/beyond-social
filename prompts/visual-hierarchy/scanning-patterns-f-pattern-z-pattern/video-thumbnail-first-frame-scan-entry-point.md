A feed thumbnail or a video's first frame gets scanned the same way a
low-text hero does, entry point top-left, terminal point bottom-right or
center, before the viewer decides to tap play. The frame composition has to
win that scan in under half a second, before any motion has even started.

The recipe:

- Don't center the subject by default; bias the subject or key prop slightly
  toward the lower-right third, so it lands on the pattern's natural terminus,
  where the eye rests before the tap.
- Keep the top-left of the frame visually quiet, no competing text, logo, or
  busy background; that's the scan's entry point, and clutter there front-loads
  confusion before the viewer has decided anything.
- If on-screen text or a caption card is composited into the thumbnail, place
  it along the same diagonal the eye already travels, upper-left to
  lower-right, not stacked as a separate block forcing a second, competing
  scan.
- Light the intended subject at higher contrast than the background,
  specifically in the first frame; scan-pattern placement only works if the
  placed element is also the highest-contrast thing in that quadrant.

Why: platforms show a static or near-static frame before playback begins, or
autoplay a silent muted loop, so the same pre-attentive scan geometry that
governs a static hero image governs the make-or-break moment of a video's
discovery, before cinematography or motion design gets a chance to work at
all.

Example: a product-demo thumbnail with the product held in the lower-right
third, an out-of-focus plain background upper-left.
Counter-example: a thumbnail with the subject dead-center and a logo bug in
the top-left corner competing for the scan's entry point. Both the subject
and the logo lose clarity of priority.
