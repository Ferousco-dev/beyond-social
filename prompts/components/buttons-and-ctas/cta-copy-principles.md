Button copy should name the specific outcome the click produces, in the user's
voice, not the generic label a wireframe placeholder would use.

The recipe:

- Lead with a verb: "Start", "Get", "Download", "Send" — not a noun phrase.
- Be specific to the outcome, not the mechanism: "Get my free estimate" beats
  "Submit", because "Submit" describes what the form does, not what the user
  receives.
- Match the commitment level of the copy to the actual commitment: "Start free
  trial" for a real trial, not "Get started" hiding a credit-card requirement
  the user discovers on the next screen.
- First-person framing ("Start my trial") tests better than third-person
  ("Start your trial") in most A/B results, because it mirrors the user's
  internal monologue at the moment of deciding.
- Keep it to 2-4 words; longer labels wrap awkwardly and dilute urgency.
- Avoid "Submit," "Click here," and "Learn more" as the sole CTA on a
  conversion-critical button — they carry no information about the reward.

Why: the button is the last sentence of a sales pitch that started with the
headline. Vague copy forces the user to re-derive what happens next at the
exact moment friction is most costly — right before commitment. Specific copy
also sets accurate expectations, which lowers post-click bounce from users who
clicked expecting something else.

Example: "Get my free estimate" on a home-services lead form, not "Submit."

Counter-example: a checkout button labeled "Continue" used identically on the
cart page, the shipping page, and the final payment page — by the third
"Continue" the user has no idea whether the next click charges their card.
