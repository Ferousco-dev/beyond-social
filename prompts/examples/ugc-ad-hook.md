## Brief

"Ad for our desk organiser. Budget is small, needs to feel real, not like an ad."

## Shot plan

Authentic beats polished here, so the whole piece is handheld with available
light. The hook has to land in the first two seconds, so open on the problem at
its ugliest rather than on the product. Three beats: mess, fix, result. The
product enters as the solution, never as the opening frame.

## Generation prompts, one per beat

1. Handheld phone-camera shot looking down at a cluttered desk, cables tangled
   across scattered pens and sticky notes, ordinary room with soft window light,
   slightly imperfect framing, natural colour, 9:16.
2. Handheld close-up of hands placing the organiser onto the cleared desk and
   sliding pens into its slots, same window light, one continuous simple action,
   natural colour, 9:16.
3. Handheld medium shot of the finished desk, slow tilt up to a person leaning
   back satisfied, same room and light, warm and unforced, 9:16.

## Why this works

Every shot repeats the location, light, and camera style, so the three clips cut
together as one piece rather than three stock fragments. The hook shows the pain
before the product, which is what earns the next two seconds. Compare with
opening on a clean beauty shot of the organiser, which reads as an ad and is
scrolled past.
