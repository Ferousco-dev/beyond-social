Lead with the outcome the user gets, in their words, not your feature or category.
Specific beats clever. The reader should recognize their own problem in the first
line.

Rules: one idea per line; verbs over adjectives; concrete nouns and numbers over
vague claims ("a week of posts in ten minutes" beats "boost your productivity").
Cut hedges and filler ("simply", "just", "seamless", "revolutionary"). Write
microcopy that reduces anxiety at decision points (near the CTA, on forms). Match
the brand voice consistently.

Why: readers skim and self-select on relevance in seconds. Outcome-led, specific
copy passes that filter; generic feature-led copy does not, no matter how polished
the design around it.

Example: "Turn one photo into a week of short-form video." Counter-example:
"Empowering creators to unlock their full potential with our seamless,
AI-powered content platform."
