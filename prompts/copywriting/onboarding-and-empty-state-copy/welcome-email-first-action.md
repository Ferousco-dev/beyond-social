A welcome email's only job is to get the user to complete the single next
step of the product's activation path, so its copy and its landing page must
describe the same action in the same words, or the click-through breaks the
promise made in the subject line.

- Pick exactly one CTA action per email. A welcome email offering "explore
  templates," "watch a tutorial," and "join our community" splits attention
  across all three and completes none of them reliably.
- Match the email's link destination to a page mid-task, not the generic
  homepage. Link straight into the empty state that starts the promised
  action.
- Write the subject line as the specific outcome, not the company name: "Make
  your first video in two minutes" beats "Welcome to Beyond Social."
- Keep body copy under what fits in one scroll on mobile. A welcome email is
  skimmed, not read.
- If the user already completed the action before the email opened, common
  with same-day signups, the send should suppress or branch, not repeat a
  completed CTA.

Why: the welcome email and the in-product empty state are really one
continuous flow split across two surfaces. A mismatch in verb or destination
between them, the email says "try a template," the page opens to a blank
prompt box, reads as a broken promise even though nothing is technically
wrong, and it costs the click-through its trust.

Example subject: "Your first 15-second video is 2 minutes away," linking
directly to the prompt input with the placeholder pre-focused.
Counter-example: a subject line "Welcome to the platform!" linking to the
marketing homepage instead of into the product.
