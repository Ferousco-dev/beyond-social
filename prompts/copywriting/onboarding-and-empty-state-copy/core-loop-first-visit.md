The first empty screen a new user sees is the highest-leverage copy in the
product, because it is where they decide whether they understand what to do
next. It must state the concrete first action in the product's own vocabulary,
not describe the absence of content.

- Name the exact input and the exact output: "Type what you want the video to
  say, get a 15-second draft," not "No content yet."
- Put the CTA verb on the object the user will create: "Generate your first
  video," not "Get started."
- Show the shape of a finished item, a filled-in example prompt or a sample
  thumbnail, next to the empty list, so the user copies a pattern instead of
  guessing at one.
- Never lead with the absence as the headline. If it appears at all, "No
  videos yet" is the subhead; the action is the headline.
- Offer one action. A first-run empty state with three competing paths teaches
  indecision instead of a habit.

Why: users form their mental model of the product from the first screen. A
headline stating absence reads as a dead end, or worse, as evidence something
is broken. A headline stating the action reads as an invitation, and gives the
user the exact words and shape of the thing they're expected to make.

Example: "Describe your first video. We'll turn one product photo and a
sentence into a 15-second ad." Button: "Create your first video."
Counter-example: "You have no projects. Click 'New Project' to begin." This
states absence as fact and leaves the actual first action, what to type, what
happens next, undefined, forcing the user to explore before they understand
what the product does.
