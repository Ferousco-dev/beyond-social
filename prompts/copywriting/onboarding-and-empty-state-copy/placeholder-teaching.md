The placeholder text inside a prompt input is the highest-frequency teaching
surface in a prompt-driven product, since every new user looks at it before
typing. It should be a literal example of a good prompt, not a hint about what
category of text belongs there.

- Write a complete, specific, working example sentence at the length and
  detail the user should aim for: "A barista steaming milk in slow motion,
  warm morning light through a cafe window."
- Rotate two to three example placeholders across sessions or on focus, so
  users see the range of what works, not one memorized template.
- Never use meta-description placeholders like "Describe your video here" or
  "Enter prompt." That names the field; it doesn't demonstrate the skill.
- Match the example's product type to context when possible. An ad-creative
  flow shows an ad-style example, not a generic scene.
- Keep the example short enough to read in under three seconds. A placeholder
  requiring a scroll to finish reading defeats its own purpose.

Why: most users learn prompt-writing by imitation, not instruction. A
concrete example in the exact input they're about to type into transfers the
skill, specificity, physical detail, a stated light source, far more reliably
than a tooltip or help article they'd have to leave the flow to read.

Example placeholder: "A skateboarder landing a trick at golden hour, handheld
camera following from behind."
Counter-example: "Enter a description of your video." This tells the user
what the field is, not what a good answer to it looks like, leaving them to
guess at length, tone, and specificity.
