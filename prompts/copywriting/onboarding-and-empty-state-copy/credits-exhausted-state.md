An out-of-credits state appears exactly when a user has proven intent, they
just tried to generate something, so the copy should complete that specific
action's value rather than deliver a generic upgrade pitch.

- Reference the specific thing they were about to make: "Your 15-second
  product ad is ready to generate, add credits to finish it," not a generic
  "You're out of credits."
- State the concrete unit cost so the ask feels bounded: "This video costs 4
  credits, you have 0," rather than an open-ended request.
- Preserve their draft. Never require them to re-enter the prompt after
  topping up; losing input at the paywall reads as punitive.
- Offer the smallest sufficient top-up as the default selection, not the
  largest available plan, at the moment of a single blocked action.
- Avoid guilt or scarcity language like "Don't miss out." State the mechanic
  plainly; the user already wants the thing.

Why: a user hitting a credit wall mid-task is in the highest-intent moment
they will have in the product that session. Copy that just restates the block
wastes that intent on friction. Copy that restates the specific value they
were about to get, and the exact gap to close it, turns the same moment into
the easiest conversion the product will ever offer.

Example: "You're 4 credits short of generating this video. Add 10 credits for
$4 and it'll render right after."
Counter-example: "Insufficient credits. Please upgrade your plan to
continue." This is generic, discards the specific draft's value, and points at
a plan change instead of the smallest fix for this one task.
