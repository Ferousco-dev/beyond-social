Running out of credits or quota is a plan fact, not a personal failure, and the message should read that way: exact numbers, an exact resolution path, and no invented urgency.

- State the exact usage figure against the included amount ("20 of 20 renders used"), not a vague "limit reached."
- Say when the quota resets if it's subscription-based, or exactly what unlocks more if it's pay-as-you-go.
- Make the upgrade or buy-more action the primary button, but keep a visible dismiss or "wait for reset" path.
- Never use scarcity language that isn't literally true — no countdowns, no "limited time," no invented low-stock framing on a digital credit.
- If the account is mid-plan-change or has pending credits, reflect that instead of showing a flat exhausted state.

Why: a quota message sits directly at a monetization decision, and that's exactly why manufactured urgency backfires: users who feel pressured by a fake deadline convert less, not more, and remember being misled the next time they see any urgency copy from the product, including copy that's honest. Stating the real constraint plainly is what actually earns the upgrade click.

Example: "You've used all 20 renders included this month. More renders available now, or your quota resets on Sept 1." [Buy more renders] [Wait for reset]

Counter-example: "Credits exhausted! Upgrade now before it's too late!" invents an urgency that doesn't exist, since nothing about the account changes if the user waits, and it reads as manipulative rather than informative.
