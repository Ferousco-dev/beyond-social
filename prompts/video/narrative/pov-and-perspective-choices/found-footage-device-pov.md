Device-mediated POV, dashcam, bodycam, doorbell or security camera, phone
screen-recording, borrows built-in credibility because each device has a
specific, hard-to-fake visual signature tied to its real mount position and
compression behavior.

- Match the device's actual mount geometry: dashcam sits low and fixed with
  a sliver of dashboard or wheel visible; doorbell cams are high, wide-angle,
  and fisheye-distorted looking down at a porch.
- Bake in that device's real artifact quirks: doorbell cams show visible
  motion-triggered exposure hunting, dashcams show light streaking on wet
  glass at night, security footage often carries a burned-in timestamp.
- Keep frame rate and any static on-screen device elements (battery icon,
  recording dot) consistent for the device being claimed, not swapped
  mid-clip.
- Don't upgrade the implied device's fidelity mid-video; a doorbell cam
  that suddenly gains cinema-grade dynamic range breaks the format's own claim.

Why: these formats are so overexposed in everyday life that viewers hold a
precise, specific expectation for each one; a generic "shaky camera" pass
without the actual mount placement and artifact signature reads as a
costume version of the format, not the real thing.

Example: "fixed low dashcam angle, sliver of dashboard visible, timestamp
burned into the bottom corner, light streaking on rain-wet glass."
Counter-example: a device labeled as a doorbell cam shot from a low,
eye-level, non-fisheye angle with no timestamp; nothing about the frame matches how that device is actually mounted or renders.
