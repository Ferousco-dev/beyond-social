Fly-on-the-wall POV treats the camera as a witness physically present in the
room, and its entire credibility rests on the subject never once treating it
as an audience.

- The subject never glances at or speaks toward the lens, even in reaction
  moments.
- Hold a working documentary distance (medium-to-wide, not intimate
  close-up), as if a crew member stepped back to stay out of the way.
- Allow the camera to lag slightly behind action and reframe half a beat
  late, rather than pre-anticipating every movement.
- Write no direct-address lines into the script; all dialogue is between
  people in the scene, not to the viewer.

Why: the value of observational footage is that it reads as an unposed
record; the instant a subject's gaze or a line of dialogue acknowledges the
camera, the "objective witness" contract breaks and the footage reads as a
performance for an audience instead of a captured moment.

Example: two people talking in a kitchen, camera holds a static wide angle
from across the room, neither looks toward it even during a laugh.
Counter-example: the same scene, but one subject throws a quick glance and
half-smile at the lens mid-laugh; the entire "unobserved" premise collapses.
