The single highest-leverage POV decision in ad-creative is whose eyes the
camera borrows, the buyer discovering and using the product, or the seller
presenting it, because that choice determines what has to be shown and which
claims the footage can actually support.

- Buyer POV: hands unboxing, first use, unscripted-looking reactions; the
  brand is not shown or named until the product is already mid-use.
- Seller POV: a presenter faces camera, holds and gestures to the product,
  states claims directly and by name.
- Default to buyer POV for discovery-style and UGC ad-creative; it borrows
  "I found this myself" credibility that consistently outperforms
  brand-voice framing on scroll-stopping.
- Reserve seller POV for expert-authority or head-to-head comparison claims
  that need a credible, accountable narrator making the claim.

Why: buyer POV works because it mimics organic discovery rather than a
pitch, while seller POV works because it mimics expert endorsement; mixing
them without intent, a presenter narrating in seller POV over unboxing
footage shot in buyer POV, splits the ad's credibility strategy in two.

Example: "hands-only unboxing POV, no brand voice yet, first genuine
reaction shot before any claim is spoken."
Counter-example: a scripted presenter narrating product claims in voiceover
over unboxing hands that are supposed to read as a first-time buyer; the two POVs contradict each other's credibility source.
