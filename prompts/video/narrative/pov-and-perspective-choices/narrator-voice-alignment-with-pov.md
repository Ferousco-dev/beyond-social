Whether the voiceover is diegetic, the on-screen character speaking, or
non-diegetic, an unseen narrator describing the scene, has to be a
deliberate match or a deliberate mismatch with the visual POV, never an
accident of the edit.

- Pair first-person visual POV with first-person diegetic voice: "I almost
  gave up on this one" over hands doing the task the voice describes.
- Pair third-person observational visuals with a non-diegetic narrator
  describing what's being witnessed from outside.
- A deliberate mismatch, first-person visuals under a third-person
  narrator, can work as a reveal device but needs a clear motivation
  (a twist, a narrator identity reveal), not incidental drift.
- Check every script line against the shot list's POV before recording, not
  after the voiceover is locked.

Why: a mismatch between who is shown seeing and who is heard speaking
creates a subconscious dissonance viewers register as "off" even without
naming it, and it is the fastest way a script starts to feel like a generic
voiceover slapped over unrelated footage.

Example: hands-only first-person unboxing paired with a first-person
script line: "okay, this packaging is way nicer than I expected."
Counter-example: the same hands-only first-person footage paired with a
third-person script line ("she couldn't believe how nice the packaging was"); the pronoun contradicts whose eyes we're supposedly behind.
