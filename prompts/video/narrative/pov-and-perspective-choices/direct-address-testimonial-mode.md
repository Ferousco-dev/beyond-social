When a performer talks straight into the lens, the camera position becomes
the viewer's literal eyes, so the geometry of that conversation, distance and
gaze, has to match how a real person talks to someone a few feet away.

- Land eye contact at or just above lens center; even a few degrees off
  reads as looking past the viewer rather than at them.
- Hold a conversational distance: chest-up medium shot, not a wide
  establishing frame that implies a stage rather than a room.
- Let the gaze break naturally every few seconds (a glance down, a blink
  cluster) instead of a fixed, unblinking stare into the lens.
- Record close, present audio to match the implied proximity; a roomy or
  echoey track contradicts an intimate frame.

Why: on social, second-person address borrows peer-to-peer trust because it
mimics a private one-on-one conversation; any single cue that violates that,
wrong eyeline, wrong distance, mismatched audio space, reintroduces the
sense of being pitched at rather than talked to.

Example: "medium chest-up shot, direct eye contact with lens, close
present audio, natural blink every few seconds."
Counter-example: a wide shot of a presenter addressing the lens from across
a room with a hollow, distant mic sound; the geometry contradicts the intimacy the words are claiming.
