Hands-only POV, no face, no narrator body, focuses the viewer entirely on
the product's mechanics and mimics the moment right before a purchase
decision: "would my hands do this."

- Keep motion purposeful but slightly imperfect: a small readjustment grip,
  a beat of fumbling with packaging before it opens cleanly.
- Let real skin texture, knuckle creases, and the hand's own cast shadow
  fall across the product rather than an evenly lit, shadowless product shot.
- Avoid perfectly centered, symmetrical hand poses; real hands grip
  off-axis and adjust mid-action.
- Let a hand exit frame between actions instead of holding a static
  "presenting" pose to camera.
- Vary grip pressure visibly across the sequence, a firm grip to open a
  stiff lid, a lighter grip once it's loose, rather than one uniform hold.

Why: hands are among the easiest elements to sell as real because their
micro-movements, uneven grip pressure, small corrections, are hard to fake
and instantly readable as human; a stiff, centered, unmoving hand is one of
the fastest tells that footage is synthetic.

Example: "hands-only POV, fingers fumble the packaging tab once before it
tears open, natural shadow from the hand falling across the box."
Counter-example: a perfectly symmetrical, centered hand holding the product
motionless toward camera like a product shot; reads as a render, not a use moment.
