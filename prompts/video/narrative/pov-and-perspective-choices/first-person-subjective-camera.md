First-person POV only reads as "I am this person" when the camera moves like
it is mounted to a moving, imperfect body rather than floating on an invisible
rig.

- Sync vertical bob to an implied walking cadence (a subtle rise and fall,
  not a smooth glide) rather than gimbal-flat motion.
- Let hands and arms enter from the bottom of frame and interact with what
  they reach for; a POV shot with no hands ever visible feels disembodied.
- Have the "eyes" hesitate and settle when attention shifts to something new,
  instead of panning there in one continuous sweep.
- Let the lens get briefly occluded by a doorframe, a raised hand, or a
  passing object when moving through space.

Why: real bodies carry momentum, blink, and lose balance slightly; a
first-person shot with studio-smooth, uninterrupted movement is
indistinguishable from a drone or dolly with a face-cam label stuck on it,
which quietly cancels the identification the POV choice was supposed to buy.

Example: "handheld POV, slight vertical bob at walking pace, hand reaches
into frame to open a door, camera pauses on the doorknob before pushing
through."
Counter-example: a perfectly smooth, floating push through a doorway with no
hand ever entering frame; reads as a drone shot wearing a POV label, not a person.
