Camera height relative to a subject's eyeline is read as a power and
identification cue before a single word is spoken: eye-level invites peer
identification, a low angle grants scale and authority, a high angle
diminishes or infantilizes.

- Default to eye-level for testimonial, UGC, and peer-trust content; it is
  the height that reads as "someone like me talking to me."
- Use a subtly low angle, camera 10-15 degrees below eyeline looking up,
  for hero or before-after reveal beats; steeper angles tip into a
  comic-book heroic look that breaks realism.
- Avoid a high angle looking down on any subject the copy wants the viewer
  to trust, unless the intended read is vulnerability or smallness.
- Match the angle consistently across cuts of the same subject; a shift in
  height mid-scene silently changes how much authority they're being given.

Why: this is perceptual grammar built from a lifetime of physically sizing
other people up by eye level; a mismatched height, a condescending high
angle on someone the script positions as an expert, undercuts the words no
matter how well they're delivered.

Example: "camera at subject's eye level, slight low angle for the final
reveal line, consistent height held through the cut before it."
Counter-example: a testimonial shot from a high angle looking down at the
speaker; the framing makes them look small and uncertain while the script asks the viewer to trust them.
