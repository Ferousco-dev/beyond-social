Restricting the camera to only what one character can plausibly see, never
cutting away to information they don't yet have, manufactures suspense for
free because the viewer's uncertainty matches the character's exactly.

- No cutaways to information the POV character doesn't have access to,
  even briefly.
- Keep off-screen sound sources unconfirmed until the POV character would
  themselves confirm them by turning or moving toward it.
- Let the frame's own edges function as the boundary of what's known;
  something enters or exits frame before it gets explained.
- Resolve the withheld information only once the POV character
  encounters it directly, not through narration filling the gap early.

Why: withheld information only generates real tension if the withholding is
consistent throughout; a single omniscient cutaway breaks the contract and
retroactively makes the earlier suspense feel like a cheap trick rather than
an earned constraint.

Example: first-person POV walking toward an unexplained sound, camera never
cuts away to reveal the source until the character physically reaches it.
Counter-example: the same setup, but midway through it cuts to a shot
revealing the sound's source from an angle the character couldn't see; the mystery deflates before the character (and viewer) discover it together.
