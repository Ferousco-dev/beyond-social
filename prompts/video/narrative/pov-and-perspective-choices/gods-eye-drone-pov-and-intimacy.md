An overhead or wide observational drone POV removes the viewer from any
single character's experience entirely, trading identification for scale
and context, and dropping it into the wrong beat kills momentum a closer
POV had already built.

- Reserve god's-eye framing for scale reveals, geography or context beats,
  or a closing button shot, not for the emotional or product-detail beat
  that needs identification.
- Motivate the move: a rise that follows a subject's own upward glance or
  their exit from frame reads as earned; an arbitrary drone-up over a
  talking head does not.
- Keep visible texture in the wide shot, wind moving grass or water,
  plausible cast shadows, rather than a static, textureless overhead
  stillness that reads as a render.
- Return to a closer POV immediately after the beat the wide shot was
  spent on; don't let the video drift and stay wide.

Why: intimacy and identification are POV properties that scale inversely
with camera height and distance, so a god's-eye shot inserted into an
otherwise close first-person or OTS video resets the viewer's relationship
to the subject and has to be spent on a specific beat, not defaulted to for
generic "production value."

Example: "close hands-only POV finishing the demo, subject looks up, cut to
a slow overhead rise revealing the full workspace as a closing button shot."
Counter-example: a drone rise over a talking-head testimonial mid-sentence
with no motivating glance or beat; the sudden scale shift disconnects the viewer from the person they were just listening to.
