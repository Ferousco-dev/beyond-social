Over-the-shoulder framing puts the viewer where a character stands without
making them that character, which is why it is the default shot for
tutorials and demos that need both a trusted human and clear visibility of
what their hands are doing.

- Keep the near shoulder and back of head soft-focus, occupying roughly
  15-25% of frame width, not centered or sharp.
- Set camera height to the demonstrator's actual eyeline, not a generic
  chest-height rig position.
- Let the shoulder shift and reveal more or less of the working surface as
  the person leans in or sits back, rather than locking the framing rigidly.
- Reserve pure first-person POV (no visible person at all) for moments where
  the demonstrator's face and rapport add nothing.

Why: pure POV removes the human anchor that instructional content depends on
for trust and pacing cues; OTS keeps the demonstrator's presence, reactions,
and hand-eye coordination in frame while still orienting the viewer toward
the task, which is why cooking, tech-review, and tutorial content defaults to it.

Example: "over-the-shoulder on a person's workbench, their shoulder soft in
the left third, hands sharp in focus assembling a part."
Counter-example: a centered, sharp-focus shoulder that fills a third of the
frame and never moves; it blocks the work instead of framing it.
