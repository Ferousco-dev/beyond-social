Video is a visual medium; demonstrate the claim instead of stating it. "Fast" is
weak on screen; showing the thing happen quickly is strong. Let the footage carry
the argument and use words only to sharpen it.

Practice:

- Turn benefits into visible moments: "durable" becomes the product surviving a
  drop; "effortless" becomes one tap and a finished result.
- Prefer action and reaction to narration. A genuine reaction shot sells more than
  a line of voiceover claiming delight.
- Use the product in context, doing its job, rather than a spinning beauty shot
  with a list of features.
- When you must tell (a stat, a name), pair it with a matching visual so the eye
  and ear reinforce each other.

Why: shown claims are believed; told claims are discounted. Demonstration is also
what generative video is uniquely good at producing on demand.

Example: to show "waterproof," film the phone recording clearly underwater.
Counter-example: a title card that says "100% waterproof" over an unrelated shot.
