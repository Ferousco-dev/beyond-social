On-screen text and captions in a compressed emotional arc should only carry information the image and sound genuinely cannot, most usefully a time or context jump, and should never restate what a reaction shot is already showing.

- Use captions to compress time ("three weeks later") rather than to explain what a character is feeling.
- Keep any caption during an emotional beat under about four words; longer text competes with the visual for reading time the beat doesn't have.
- Remove all captions during the turn itself; let it play as pure image and sound.
- Cut any caption that states an emotion word outright ("heartbroken," "relieved," "grateful"); if the shot needs the word to work, the shot isn't doing its job yet.

Why: reading text and reading a face draw on the same limited channel of attention, so a caption running under a reaction shot forces the viewer to split attention between two sources of the same information, which weakens both instead of reinforcing either. Text is genuinely useful for compressing narrative time, since a generator or an editor can't otherwise imply "three weeks later" without a caption, but it is the wrong tool for stating a feeling a shot was built to convey on its own.

Example: caption "3 years later" over a hard cut, then no on-screen text through the turn and the resolution that follows.
Counter-example: the caption "She was heartbroken" appears layered over a crying reaction shot, which is redundant with what the shot already communicates and undercuts the reaction shot's own work.
