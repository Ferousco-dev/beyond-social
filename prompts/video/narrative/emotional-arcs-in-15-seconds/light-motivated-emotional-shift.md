When the light changes at the emotional turn, tie the change to a source that is visible or clearly implied in the world, a practical lamp switching on, clouds crossing the sun, a door opening onto brighter light, rather than applying a stylistic color-grade shift that has no in-world cause.

- Name the practical source and its direction explicitly in the prompt: "window light dims as clouds cross," "a lamp behind her flickers on."
- Tie the lighting change to the same beat as the external trigger that causes the turn, not to a separate moment.
- Keep the color temperature shift small, on the order of a few hundred Kelvin, rather than a dramatic full-scene tonal flip.
- Let the change build over roughly a second rather than snapping instantly.

Why: cinematographers motivate every light change with a source the audience can identify, because an unmotivated shift in color or intensity reads as a filter applied after the fact rather than something that happened in the room. This is a frequent tell in generated video, where the whole frame's color temperature jumps at a story beat with no visible cause, and it's avoidable simply by naming the source doing the work.

Example: "a practical lamp behind her flickers on as he enters the doorway, warm key light rising gradually across her face."
Counter-example: "the whole scene suddenly shifts to warm golden tones" with no described source, which reads as a filter switch rather than a lived moment in a room.
