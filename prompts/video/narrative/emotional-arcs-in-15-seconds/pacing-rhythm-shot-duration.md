Shot duration should contract as the arc approaches the turn and expand once it reaches resolution, mirroring how attention narrows under tension and loosens under release. A flat, uniform cutting rhythm across all three beats is one of the clearest signatures of a templated or auto-generated edit.

- Setup shots run roughly two to three seconds each, gradually shortening as the turn nears.
- The turn itself is the shortest cut in the piece, well under two seconds, sometimes a single hard cut with no coverage around it.
- Resolution gets the longest shot in the piece, held three seconds or more, with no further cutting inside it unless it's a slow push.
- Do not keep a constant shot length through the resolution; that flattens the emotional curve instead of letting it land.

Why: editing rhythm is a felt tempo, not just a sequence of images, and viewers register acceleration and deceleration in cut speed as tension and release even when they can't name why. A metronomic cutting pattern, the same shot length repeated throughout, reads as assembled rather than paced, because no real emotional event compresses and expands time evenly.

Example: "cuts every ~2.5s through setup, a single ~1s cut at the turn, then a static 5s hold on the resolution."
Counter-example: uniform 2-second cuts maintained straight through the resolution, which turns a felt ending into a metronome and reads as templated.
