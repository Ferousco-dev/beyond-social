Dialogue carrying the emotional turn needs natural vocal imperfection, breath, pitch variance, a small self-correction, timed to the beat it accompanies, or it reads as narrated at the audience rather than lived by a character.

- Write in a breath or a short pause immediately before the line that carries the turn.
- Allow a small restart or filler during setup dialogue ("I- I thought you'd left") rather than grammatically clean sentences throughout.
- Let pitch and rate genuinely shift with the character's state: tighter and quieter at the turn, more open at resolution, rather than staying constant.
- Avoid uniform pacing and pitch across the whole line; that flatness is the clearest tell of synthetic or under-directed delivery.

Why: prosody, the rhythm, stress, and pitch contour of speech, carries more emotional information than the words themselves, and it's also the fastest thing a listener uses to judge whether a voice is real. A perfectly even-paced, evenly-pitched delivery is grammatically correct but emotionally flat, and it is specifically the signature that separates read-aloud text from a person actually feeling something while they speak.

Example: "(exhale) I- I didn't think you'd come. [pause] ...you came."
Counter-example: the same line delivered at a perfectly even pace and pitch with no breath or hesitation, which is technically clean but reads as machine narration rather than a felt moment.
