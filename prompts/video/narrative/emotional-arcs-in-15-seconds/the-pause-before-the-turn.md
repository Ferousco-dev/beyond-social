Insert a brief held beat, stillness, near-silence, or a lingering shot, directly before the turn to make it register as a discrete event rather than one more thing that happens in a stream of things. Without this pause, a compressed arc's turn can slide past unnoticed.

- Hold a static or near-static shot for about a second and a half on a neutral or ambiguous expression.
- Drop score to near-silence or remove it entirely, leaving only ambient room tone.
- Do not let the expression or the score telegraph what's about to happen; ambiguity is the point.
- Cut hard immediately after the pause; do not ease into the turn.

Why: anticipation is built by briefly violating the expected cadence of cuts and sound, forcing the viewer's attention to sharpen right before the payoff. A turn with no preceding pause has to compete for attention with everything that came before it; a turn that follows a beat of near-silence gets the viewer's full attention because the drop in stimulus itself signals that something is about to happen.

Example: "hold static for 1.5s on an unreadable expression, ambient room tone only, no score, then hard cut to the turn."
Counter-example: a musical sting or a half-smile appears just before the turn and telegraphs the outcome, so the actual turn lands as confirmation rather than surprise.
