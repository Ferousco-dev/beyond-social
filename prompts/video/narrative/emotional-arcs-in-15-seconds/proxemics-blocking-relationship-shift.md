Use physical distance between people, and the camera's height and angle relative to them, to show a relationship changing across the arc, instead of relying on a line of dialogue to state that it changed.

- Set the initial distance deliberately in setup: conversational distance (roughly four to six feet) reads as neutral, closer reads as intimate or confrontational, farther reads as distant or guarded.
- Change that distance at the turn: a step back signals withdrawal, a step forward signals a decision.
- Match camera height to the emotional state: a slightly low angle can read as ceding power, eye-level as parity.
- Resolution should land on a new, different equilibrium distance and eye-line than the one the scene opened on; returning to the exact opening blocking undercuts the sense that anything changed.

Why: audiences read proxemics unconsciously, distance and body orientation are processed as social information almost automatically, faster than dialogue is parsed. This makes blocking one of the cheapest tools available in a compressed arc: a single step closer or a single step back can carry as much narrative weight as a line of dialogue, without spending any of the limited runtime on speech.

Example: "setup: two people about four feet apart at eye level; turn: he takes a step back; resolution: she closes the distance to arm's length, eye-lines matched."
Counter-example: the characters remain in identical blocking for the full 15 seconds while a line of dialogue announces that the relationship changed, leaving the frame visually inert and making the dialogue do work the staging should have done.
