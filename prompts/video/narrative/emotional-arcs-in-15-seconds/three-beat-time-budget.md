A 15-30 second arc fails when the three beats get equal time. Setup needs to be
the shortest beat, not the longest, because the audience does not need to be
convinced a problem exists — they need to feel the turn.

- For a 15s spot: roughly 3-4s setup, 4-6s turn, 6-8s resolution. The turn is
  short but must be unambiguous; the resolution gets the most room because it's
  what sells.
- For a 30s spot: roughly 6-8s setup, 8-10s turn, 12-16s resolution.
- Never open on a static establishing shot with no emotional information in it —
  every second of setup should already carry the "before" feeling (tension,
  boredom, frustration) so it's doing double duty.
- Mark the turn with a single hard cut or a camera move that changes speed, not
  with dialogue alone — visual punctuation reads faster than a spoken line.

Why: viewers decide whether to keep watching in the first 1-2 seconds, so a
setup that spends time establishing context instead of establishing feeling
loses the audience before the turn ever lands. Compressing setup and
front-loading it with emotional information lets the turn feel earned instead
of sudden, and it frees up resolution time, which is where purchase intent or
emotional payoff actually gets built.

Example: "0-3s: hands fumbling with a tangled charging cable, sighs; 3-8s: cut
to the same hands plugging into a single dock, click sound; 8-15s: static shot
of the desk, cables gone, person leans back."

Counter-example: an 8-second wide establishing shot of an office before any
character or problem appears — by the time the setup finishes, half the runway
is gone and the turn has no time to breathe.
