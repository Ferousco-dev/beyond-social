Resolve a compressed arc on the felt effect of the turn, a face settling, a body loosening, an object held differently, rather than cutting back to explain what caused it or having a character state the feeling out loud.

- Hold the reaction shot two to four seconds longer than instinct suggests; cutting away early is the most common way a resolution reads as unearned.
- Let a single small physical detail carry it: an exhale, shoulders dropping, eyes going glassy, a hand relaxing its grip.
- Do not add dialogue that names the emotion ("I'm so happy," "I can't believe it").
- Do not cut back to a wide shot of the cause during resolution; the cause has already done its job.

Why: this is a version of the Kuleshov effect applied to arc endings, meaning meaning gets built by juxtaposition and duration, not by explanation. A held reaction shot lets the viewer complete the emotional inference themselves, and self-completed inferences land as more true than stated ones. Restating the cause or naming the feeling in dialogue does the viewer's work for them and makes the resolution feel like an ad reading its own punchline.

Example: "close on hands slowly unclenching on the tabletop, a slow exhale, focus drifting soft toward her eyes, held for four seconds."
Counter-example: the character says "I finally feel free" directly to camera over a neutral expression, which tells the audience the resolution instead of letting them witness it, and reads like a voiceover pitch rather than a moment.
