For short-form video, open on or just after the charged moment and let the setup arrive as implied context rather than as its own establishing sequence. Retention drops hardest in the first one to two seconds, so a slow, literal setup risks losing the audience before the turn it was building toward ever happens.

- Cold open on an image or line that already contains tension: a half-packed bag, an unanswered call log, a hand pulling away.
- Use one environmental detail to imply backstory instead of a scene that states it (a second toothbrush missing from a cup, not a line explaining a breakup).
- Let a fragment of overheard dialogue imply history rather than establish it in full ("...and you never told me" needs no prior scene).
- The first frame should already contain unresolved tension, not neutral information.

Why: short-form retention curves are steepest in the opening beat, so any seconds spent on neutral establishing shots before tension appears are seconds spent losing viewers who will never see the turn at all. Folding the setup into the cold open protects the arc by making the very first frame do setup and hook work simultaneously.

Example: "open mid-argument on a single overheard line, then pull back to reveal the empty side of the bed."
Counter-example: five seconds of a calm establishing wide shot with no tension before anything happens, by which point a large share of the audience has already scrolled past and never sees the turn.
