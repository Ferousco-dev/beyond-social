The sound that triggers or accompanies the emotional turn should start slightly before the visual cut lands, not exactly on it and not after it. This single timing choice is one of the fastest ways to make a cut feel like it was made by a human editor rather than assembled mechanically.

- Pre-lap the diegetic sound (a door, a phone buzz, a breath, footsteps) two to six frames before the cut.
- Prefer a diegetic sound cue over a pure score sting to trigger the turn; score should swell into the resolution, not spike at the turn itself.
- Avoid hard silence immediately followed by a musical hit on the exact cut frame.
- If a line of dialogue is the trigger, let the last word of it bleed slightly across the cut rather than ending cleanly before it.

Why: professional editors pre-lap sound specifically to mask the cut and to make one shot feel caused by the previous one rather than simply following it. Bar-perfect, frame-locked sync between a cue and a cut is unnatural, real production audio drifts by a few frames, and generated or templated video tends toward exactly the mechanical sync that reads as artificial the moment a viewer's ear catches it.

Example: "phone buzz begins under the tail of the wide shot; cut to the close-up on the phone lands about four frames after the buzz starts."
Counter-example: the sound effect is triggered exactly on the cut frame with nothing before it, which reads as a stock transition rather than an edited moment.
