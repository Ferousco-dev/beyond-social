Even a 15-second clip is a story. The reliable short-form shape is **hook, build,
payoff**, compressed and front-loaded.

- Hook (first 1-3s): the promise or tension. State or show the payoff you are
  building toward. This is where most viewers decide to stay or scroll.
- Build (the middle): deliver on the promise with escalating interest, a process,
  a transformation, or a small arc. Keep every second earning the next.
- Payoff (the end): the reveal, result, or punchline, plus a reason to act (or to
  rewatch). Do not trail off; land it.

Additional levers: open a loop early ("wait for the end"), and consider making the
last frame flow into the first for a seamless loop. For ads, the product should be
woven into the story, not bolted on at the end.

Why: content without structure meanders and loses viewers; the hook-build-payoff
shape matches how feeds are watched and how attention actually decays.

Example: hook "You are storing coffee wrong" -> build shows the fix -> payoff shows
the better cup and "try it tomorrow." Counter-example: 15 seconds of pretty but
aimless b-roll with the product appearing only in the final second.
