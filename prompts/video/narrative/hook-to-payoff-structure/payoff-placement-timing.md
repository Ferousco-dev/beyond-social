Retention graphs on short-form video drop steadily across the runtime and
then fall sharply in the last couple of seconds as people bail once they
sense the video is ending; placing the payoff at the literal last frame means
a meaningful share of viewers never see it.

- Place the core payoff at roughly the 80 to 90 percent mark of total
  runtime, leaving a short tail after it, not as the final frame itself.
- Use the post-payoff tail for a quick reinforcement (reaction shot, result
  held on screen a beat longer, a one-line takeaway), not new information.
- For anything over 30 seconds, build a smaller secondary payoff around the
  midpoint to hold viewers who are already deciding whether to bail before
  the main resolution.
- Never stack the call-to-action and the payoff into the same final frame;
  the payoff needs its own beat to register before anything else asks for
  action.

Why: the last-second drop-off is measurable on almost every short-form
platform's analytics; a payoff placed exactly there is statistically likely
to be watched by fewer people than a payoff placed slightly earlier with a
short tail after it.

Example: a 22-second video reveals the finished result at second 18, holds
on it for 2 seconds, then closes on a 2-second reaction shot rather than
ending cold on the reveal frame.
Counter-example: the transformation reveal is the literal last frame of the
video with no tail; anyone who bails in the final second, a large share on
most platforms, never sees the thing the whole video was building toward.
