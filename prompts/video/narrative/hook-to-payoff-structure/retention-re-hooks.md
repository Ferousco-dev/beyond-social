Attention on a single continuous shot decays every few seconds even when the
opening hook worked; videos longer than about 15 seconds need a small
secondary hook planted mid-runtime to catch the attention the first hook is
losing.

- Plant a re-hook every 4 to 6 seconds for content over 20 seconds: a new
  camera angle, a cut to a different subject, an on-screen text callout, or a
  verbal "but here's the part that matters."
- Make the re-hook a genuine shift, not just a cut, changing angle alone
  without changing information rarely resets attention on its own.
- Reserve the strongest re-hook for the point in the video where the
  content is objectively slowest (a process step, an explanation), since
  that's where decay is steepest.
- Don't front-load every interesting beat near the opening hook and leave the
  middle flat; distribute the interesting material so there's always
  something new within the next few seconds.

Why: retention curves on short-form platforms decline roughly continuously
rather than staying flat after a strong open; a single hook at second zero
buys the first few seconds, not the whole runtime, so the middle needs its
own small interruptions to keep pulling attention forward.

Example: a 40-second tutorial cuts to a close-up insert shot and a fresh
on-screen callout ("this next part trips everyone up") right at the point
where the explanation gets technical.
Counter-example: one continuous unbroken take for 40 seconds after a strong
3-second hook; the opening earns the first few seconds but nothing resets
attention through the slow middle section.
