Viewers who scroll with sound already on respond to an audio cue before they
consciously process the frame, so a distinct sound at the very first moment
can stall a swipe that a purely visual hook would have lost.

- Lead with a diegetic sound that's unusual for the content type: a sharp
  mechanical click, glass, a door, an intake of breath, something the ear
  doesn't expect from the visual it's paired with.
- Avoid opening on music alone; generic background music blends into every
  other clip in the feed and doesn't register as an interrupt.
- Sync the audio spike to the first frame exactly, not a beat after it, a
  delayed sound cue arrives after the sound-on viewer has already judged the
  frame.
- Keep dialogue or voiceover out of the first quarter-second; let the
  non-verbal sound do the interrupting, then bring in speech once attention
  is already caught.

Why: sound-on viewers process auditory surprise faster than they parse a new
visual composition, an unexpected sound functions as a pre-attentive alert
that buys the extra fraction of a second a visual hook alone has to work
much harder to earn.

Example: cold open on the sharp crack of a seal breaking on a package, audio
hits exactly on frame one, dialogue doesn't start until half a second later.
Counter-example: a generic trending audio track fades in under the opening
shot with no distinct cue tied to frame one; sound-on viewers get nothing
different from what's already playing in the ten clips before it.
