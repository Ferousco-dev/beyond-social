Thumbs move at a steady cadence through a feed of similar-looking clips; the
opening beat has to visually disagree with whatever surrounds it in the feed
to stall that motion.

- Cut on unexpected motion: a whip pan that settles into the subject, a
  sudden zoom-in that overshoots slightly then corrects, a hard cut mid-gesture.
- Use a framing the feed doesn't expect: extreme close-up (texture, eye,
  hands) when most competing content opens wide, or vice versa.
- Let camera handling carry a touch of real error, a half-beat of
  refocus-hunt or a small handheld correction, rather than a mechanically
  smooth glide; over-stabilized motion reads as an ad and gets skipped faster.
- Time the interrupt to land inside the first 0.5 to 1.0 seconds, not after a
  title card or logo.

Why: attention on social is relative to the surrounding stream, not absolute;
a hook is only a hook if it looks different from the ten clips before it, so
smoothness and polish can actively work against retention.

Example: "handheld whip-pan that lands hard on a cracked phone screen,
slight refocus wobble as it settles, cut on the settle."
Counter-example: a slow, gimbal-smooth push-in that looks identical to
every other polished ad opener in the feed; nothing interrupts the scroll.
