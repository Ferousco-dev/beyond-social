A scroller decides to stay or swipe before any audio has registered and before
they know who you are, so the single opening frame has to communicate the
premise on composition and subject alone.

- Put the subject or the visible stakes dead center or on a clear third, never
  buried in a wide establishing shot with no focal point.
- Choose a frame that would still make sense as a paused thumbnail: a person
  mid-reaction, an object mid-transformation, a before/after split.
- Avoid black slates, logo cards, or a slow fade-in as frame one; start on the
  frame that already contains the question.
- Caption text, if present in frame one, should name the stakes ("this took 3
  tries"), not the topic ("today I'm making pasta").

Why: platform autoplay and thumb-scroll mean the algorithm and the viewer both
sample the first frame in isolation, out of narrative order and often muted.
A frame that requires the preceding seconds to make sense is invisible to a
system that judges it alone.

Example: open on a close-up of hands mid-repair with a visibly cracked part
in frame, no title card, no intro line.
Counter-example: three seconds of a logo animation before the subject
appears; by the time the subject shows up, the decision to swipe already happened.
