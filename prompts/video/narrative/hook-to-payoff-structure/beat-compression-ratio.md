The ratio of hook to build to payoff has to shift with total runtime; a
structure that works at 15 seconds collapses at 60 seconds if the proportions
aren't rebalanced, because build-up that's too short for a long video feels
unearned and build-up that's too long for a short video loses viewers before
it resolves.

- Under 15 seconds: roughly 20 percent hook, 40 percent build, 40 percent
  payoff plus tail, there's only room for one escalation beat before resolving.
- 15 to 40 seconds: roughly 10 to 15 percent hook, 55 to 60 percent build
  with two to three escalating beats and at least one re-hook, 25 to 30
  percent payoff and tail.
- Over 40 seconds: the hook shrinks proportionally further, but a secondary
  mid-video hook becomes mandatory, treat it structurally as two linked
  hook-to-payoff arcs rather than one long one.
- Never let the build exceed roughly two-thirds of total runtime regardless
  of length; past that point the payoff can't feel proportionate to the setup
  no matter how strong it is.

Why: viewers judge pacing relative to a video's own runtime, not against an
absolute clock, so the same six-second build reads as tight in a 15-second
video and as sluggish in a 45-second one; matching proportions to length is
what keeps pacing feeling intentional rather than accidental.

Example: a 45-second product demo spends roughly 5 seconds on the hook, 27
on two escalating build beats with a mid-video re-hook at second 20, and 13
on the payoff and its tail.
Counter-example: a 12-second hook and a 40-second slow build precede a
5-second payoff in a 60-second video; the payoff has to resolve nearly a
minute of accumulated expectation in a fraction of the time it took to build,
so it reads as abrupt regardless of what it shows.
