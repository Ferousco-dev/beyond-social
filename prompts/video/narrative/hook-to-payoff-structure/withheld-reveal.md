The strongest version of an open loop withholds what's actually in frame,
not just what's said out loud; keeping the payoff object or result physically
out of camera view until the reveal beat makes the eventual reveal function
as new information rather than a restatement.

- Frame around the payoff object during setup: shoot over-the-shoulder, from
  behind, through a gap, or with the key element cropped just out of frame.
- Use focus, not just framing, to withhold: keep the payoff object visible
  but soft in the background while foreground action holds sharp focus, then
  rack focus onto it at the reveal.
- Don't let a mirror, reflection, screen, or shadow accidentally show the
  withheld element early; check every frame of the setup for accidental leaks.
- Make the reveal shot a deliberate change, an actual cut or camera move
  toward the object, not just a continuation of the same shot; the withheld
  information should arrive with its own beat.

Why: verbal suspense alone is fragile because the viewer can often guess the
content of an unstated answer; visual withholding removes the information
entirely from what the eye can gather, so the reveal carries genuine new
data instead of just confirming a guess.

Example: setup shows only hands working and a blurred shape in the
background rack-out of focus; the reveal is a hard rack-focus and cut to the
finished object in sharp detail.
Counter-example: the finished product sits in clear focus in the
background of every setup shot while the narrator says "you'll see the
result soon"; anyone paying attention has already seen it before the "reveal."
