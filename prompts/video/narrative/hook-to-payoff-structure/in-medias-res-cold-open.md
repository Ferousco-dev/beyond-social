Short-form runtime has no budget for setup; the video should open on a frame
that's already inside the consequential moment, with any context the viewer
needs delivered after attention is secured, not before.

- Find the latest possible point in the story that still makes sense as an
  opening image, then cut everything before it.
- If a viewer needs to know who/where/why, deliver that as a single caption
  or voiceover line laid over the action, not as a preceding scene.
- Treat any shot of someone walking into frame, sitting down, or picking up a
  camera as setup to cut, not a hook, unless that motion is the actual event.
- Test it by asking: if this were frame one with no lead-in, would a
  stranger still understand what's at stake within two seconds? If not, trim
  further in, not further out.

Why: exposition can be back-filled once someone is already curious, but
curiosity can't be manufactured retroactively once someone has scrolled past;
front-loading setup spends the only currency that determines whether anyone
sees the rest.

Example: open mid-repair with the broken part already in hand and sparks
flying, voiceover ("found this in the trash, three days later it's running")
carrying the who/why over the action instead of a preceding scene.
Counter-example: a 4-second intro of someone walking to their desk, sitting
down, and saying "so today I wanted to show you" before the actual subject
appears; the video hasn't started yet by the time most viewers have left.
