Viewers pattern-match a video's genre within the first couple of seconds from
familiar visual cues, and a payoff that breaks the genre they predicted lands
harder than a payoff that simply confirms it, because the break itself is the
information.

- Borrow the visible signature of a specific, recognizable format on
  purpose: the framing of a cooking tutorial, the pacing of a horror cold
  open, the composition of a real-estate walkthrough, whatever genre the
  audience will recognize within a second.
- Commit to the borrowed genre's conventions fully for the setup, don't
  hedge with a wink or a knowing tone that tips the subversion early.
- Place the actual subject reveal at the exact moment the genre convention
  would normally deliver its expected beat, so the swap happens where the
  predicted payoff was going to land, not before or after it.
- Make sure the subverted version still resolves cleanly on its own terms;
  a subversion that just confuses without landing a real payoff reads as a
  mistake, not a twist.

Why: a fulfilled prediction is processed as unremarkable because the brain
predicted it correctly, a broken prediction forces active reprocessing of
everything that came before it, which is what makes a payoff feel
disproportionately strong relative to its actual runtime.

Example: open with the exact framing, pacing, and score of a horror
jump-scare cold open, and the "scare" resolves into an oddly mundane product
reveal at the precise beat a jump-scare would normally hit.
Counter-example: the setup uses horror cues but the voiceover jokes about it
being fake halfway through; by the time the actual reveal arrives, the
audience already knows a swap is coming and the break has no force left.
