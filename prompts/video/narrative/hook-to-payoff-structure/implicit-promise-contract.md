Every hook makes an implicit promise about what kind of resolution is coming,
in genre, scale, and tone, and the payoff has to match that promise on all
three axes or the ending reads as a non-sequitur even if it's technically
resolved.

- Extract the promise from the hook's exact wording before writing the
  payoff: "watch what happens" promises an event, "here's why" promises an
  explanation, "you won't believe" promises a scale-mismatch reveal.
- Match tone: a hook delivered as deadpan comedy needs a payoff punchline, not
  a sincere emotional turn, and the reverse breaks trust just as fast.
- Match scale: if the hook implies a dramatic result, a mild or ambiguous
  payoff under-delivers regardless of production quality.
- Re-read the hook's literal words last, after the rest of the script is
  written, and check the final beat against them one more time.

Why: viewers hold the hook's wording as a spec in short-term memory for the
whole runtime; a payoff that's good on its own but doesn't answer that
specific spec feels like a bait-and-switch even when nothing was technically
false.

Example: hook "this $12 tool replaced my $400 one" promises a head-to-head
comparison, so the payoff must show both tools performing the same task
side by side, not just praise for the cheap one.
Counter-example: hook promises "the moment it all went wrong" but the
payoff is a generic wrap-up line with no specific moment shown; the promise
of a discrete event is never actually paid off.
