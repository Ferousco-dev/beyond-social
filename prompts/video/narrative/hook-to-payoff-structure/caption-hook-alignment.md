A large share of short-form video is watched with sound off, so the on-screen
text has to carry the hook independently of the voiceover, but it fails if it
just transcribes the spoken line word for word: identical text and audio give
the eye nothing extra to read and feel redundant to viewers with sound on.

- Write the caption as a compressed version of the hook's stakes, not a
  transcript: if the voiceover says "I could not believe what happened when I
  opened this," the caption reads "opened it, immediately regretted it,"
  short, sound-off-legible, and worded differently from the audio.
- Keep the caption to one short line, large enough to read at a glance.
- Time the caption to appear on or slightly before the first frame, not a
  half-second after the video starts, sound-off viewers need it immediately.
- Let the caption ask the question and let the spoken line, if any, add
  color or timing, not the reverse.

Why: sound-off viewers rely entirely on the caption to receive the hook,
so a caption that only echoes the audio wastes the one channel that reaches
them, while sound-on viewers get a flatter experience from reading and
hearing the identical sentence at once.

Example: voiceover "watch what happens when I add heat," caption reads
"30 seconds later, this happened."
Counter-example: caption reads "Watch what happens when I add heat" while
the voiceover says the exact same sentence at the same moment; nothing new
reaches either audience.
