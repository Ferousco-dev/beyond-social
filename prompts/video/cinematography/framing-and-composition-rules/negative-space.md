Leave large, genuinely empty areas of frame around a small subject instead of
filling the composition; the emptiness itself carries meaning and directs
attention harder than any amount of added detail could.

The recipe:

- Push the subject to a third or a corner and let 40-60% of the frame stay
  unoccupied by anything with hard edges or high contrast.
- Fill the negative space with something with low visual weight: a soft
  gradient of natural light, an out-of-focus wall, open sky, not a blank
  digital void.
- Use negative space to signal isolation, calm, or premium restraint; reserve
  a busy, filled frame for energy or urgency.
- Do not confuse negative space with a plain generated backdrop — the space
  should still read as a real, lit environment, just an empty part of it.

Why: contrast in density (a lot of nothing next to one deliberate something) is
a stronger attention cue than contrast in color or brightness alone, and it
reads as an editorial choice rather than a rendering shortcut, which is the
difference between "minimalist" and "the model didn't generate a background."

Example: "subject small in the lower-right third, upper two-thirds of frame is
soft out-of-focus window light and bare wall, no other objects in shot."
Counter-example: "subject small against a flat, evenly lit gray void" — reads
as an unfinished render rather than a considered minimalist frame.
