Crop a person at a joint or a natural seam of clothing, never through the
middle of a limb, the neck, or the ankles, because a mid-limb crop reads as an
amputation and pulls attention straight to the frame edge instead of the subject.

The recipe:

- Safe crop lines: just above the elbow or wrist, mid-thigh or just above the
  knee, at the waistline or a belt line, or full-body with a small margin
  below the feet.
- Unsafe crop lines: through the wrist, the ankle, directly at the neck, or at
  the knee joint itself — all read as an accidental or badly cropped frame.
- When in doubt, frame wider than the tightest safe line and crop in post;
  it's easier to punch in later than to fix a bad crop that was baked into
  generation.
- For a vertical talking-avatar frame, the standard safe crop is mid-chest to
  just above the wrists, leaving hands either fully in frame or fully out.

Why: the eye completes familiar shapes automatically; a body cropped at a
joint reads as continuing naturally out of frame, while a crop through a limb
or the neck reads as a visible edit, and generated video is already prone to
edge artifacts that get worse exactly where a crop line lands on a joint.

Example: "medium shot cropped at the waistline, hands fully visible below,
head with normal headroom above."
Counter-example: "frame cropped mid-forearm" — the hand disappears at an
unnatural point and reads as a framing error, not a stylistic choice.
