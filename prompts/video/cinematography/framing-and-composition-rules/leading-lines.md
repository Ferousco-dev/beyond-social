Use real architectural or environmental lines (a countertop edge, a hallway, a
railing, a road, a row of windows) that converge toward the subject, so the eye
travels into the frame instead of scanning it flat.

The recipe:

- Favor diagonal lines over horizontal or vertical ones; diagonals create more
  perceived depth and energy in a single static frame.
- Anchor the line's vanishing point at or near the subject, not at an empty
  corner of the frame.
- In a product shot, let the edge of a table or shelf run from a bottom corner
  up toward the product rather than framing the product against a flat wall.
- Keep the line imperfect: a countertop with a coffee ring, a hallway with a
  door ajar. A too-clean converging line reads as a rendered vanishing-point
  grid rather than a photographed room.

Why: the human visual system follows contrast edges automatically; a line that
terminates at the subject does the compositional work of arrows without
looking like an arrow, and it manufactures spatial depth in what is otherwise
a flat 2D generated frame.

Example: "kitchen counter edge leading from bottom-left corner up to the
product on the right third, single window light, slight surface clutter."
Counter-example: "product floating against a seamless white background with no
environmental lines" — technically clean but reads as a render, not a place.
