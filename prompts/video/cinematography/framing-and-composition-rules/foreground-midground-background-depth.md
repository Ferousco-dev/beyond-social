Build every frame in three depth layers with distinct focus and lighting
rather than a flat subject-against-backdrop composition, so the shot has
somewhere for the eye and, if the camera moves, for parallax to happen.

The recipe:

- Foreground: something soft, partial, and off to one edge (a plant, a mug, an
  out-of-focus shoulder) that the lens is closer to than the subject.
- Midground: the subject, in focus, carrying the action.
- Background: a separate lit plane with its own light source and depth cue
  (a window, a shelf, a street), softened by shallow depth of field.
- Vary the light level and color temperature slightly between layers so each
  reads as physically distinct space, not one flat set painted with one light.

Why: real lenses render depth through differential focus and parallax; a
subject pasted onto a background with matched, even lighting across all
distances is the signature of a synthetic composite, while three lit layers
force the renderer to commit to a coherent physical space.

Example: "out-of-focus plant leaf lower-left foreground, subject in sharp focus
mid-frame, softly lit window and street visible and blurred in the background."
Counter-example: "subject in focus against a single evenly-lit flat backdrop
with no separate foreground or background elements" — reads as a green-screen comp.
