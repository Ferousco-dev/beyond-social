Split the frame into a 3x3 grid and place the subject's key point (usually the
eyes, for a person) on one of the four intersections rather than dead center.
Off-center placement reads as observed rather than staged, which matters for
footage that needs to avoid the too-composed AI look.

The recipe:

- Put the eyeline on the upper third line, not the vertical center of the frame.
- Place the subject on the left or right third, leaving the opposite two-thirds
  as room for action, product, or environment.
- For product shots, put the product on a third intersection and let the brand
  mark or background fall in the negative space, not centered under the logo.
- Combine with a slight lens breathing or handheld micro-drift so the thirds
  placement isn't mathematically locked to the pixel for the whole shot.

Why: the eye enters a frame at the center by default; a subject placed off that
center forces a deliberate scan path, which is what makes a shot feel composed
by a person holding a camera rather than auto-centered by software. Dead-center
symmetry is the single fastest tell of synthetic, un-authored framing.

Example: "medium shot, subject's eyes on the upper-left third intersection,
negative space to the right, slight handheld drift, 35mm equivalent."
Counter-example: "subject centered exactly in frame, symmetrical on both axes" —
reads as a stock-photo grid, not a shot a human operator composed.
