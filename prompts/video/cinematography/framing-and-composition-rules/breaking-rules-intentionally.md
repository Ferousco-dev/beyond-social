Composition rules exist to produce a default reading of comfort and balance;
break one only when the goal is discomfort, confinement, or raw urgency, and
break exactly one rule per shot so the choice still reads as intentional.

The recipe:

- Center the subject dead-on (violating thirds) to signal confrontation,
  interrogation, or a direct-to-camera address that should feel unmediated.
- Crush headroom to nearly zero to signal claustrophobia, pressure, or a
  breaking point.
- Let a line run flat through the frame instead of converging, to signal
  stasis or a stuck moment.
- Never break two rules in the same shot without a narrative reason for both;
  stacked violations read as a mistake, not a choice.

Why: a rule is only visible as broken if the surrounding grammar establishes
what "correct" looks like first; a single deviation against an otherwise
competent frame reads as authored intent, while a frame with no compositional
logic at all just reads as an AI system that never learned the rule.

Example: "subject centered dead-on, direct eye contact with lens, tight
headroom, static locked-off frame, one line of confrontational dialogue."
Counter-example: "subject off-center and also crooked and also cropped at the
ankles for no story reason" — three violations at once reads as broken, not bold.
