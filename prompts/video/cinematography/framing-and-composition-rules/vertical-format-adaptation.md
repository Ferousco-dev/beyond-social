A 9:16 frame has roughly the horizontal room of a passport photo and the
vertical room of a doorway; composition rules built for 16:9 landscape need to
be re-mapped, not just cropped, or the shot ends up with dead space top and
bottom and a cramped subject in the middle.

The recipe:

- Reframe headroom and lead room on the vertical thirds grid, not the
  horizontal one; the top and bottom thirds carry more compositional weight
  than the left and right thirds do in this format.
- Use vertical leading lines (a doorframe, a standing lamp, a body's own
  vertical axis) instead of horizontal ones, which get cropped out fast.
- Keep safe zones clear: leave the top ~10% and bottom ~15-20% of frame free of
  critical subject detail, since platform UI (captions, buttons, username)
  overlays those areas.
- For a single standing subject, a medium-close framing (chest-up) usually
  fills a vertical frame better than a wide shot, which leaves large unused
  strips on either side of the subject.

Why: vertical framing was designed around a phone held in one hand at reading
distance, not around cinema composition; rules that assume horizontal room to
breathe (wide leading lines, side negative space) simply don't have room to
exist in 9:16, so the compositional weight has to move to the vertical axis instead.

Example: "chest-up medium shot, subject's eyes on the upper third of a 9:16
frame, vertical doorframe line behind them, bottom 15% left clear for captions."
Counter-example: "a 16:9 wide shot center-cropped into 9:16" — the subject
shrinks to a sliver in the middle with dead space above and below.
