The focal length used for a face changes how the features read, because different
lenses compress or exaggerate facial geometry at the distance needed to fill the
frame with a head.

- 35mm requires the camera close to the face to fill frame, which widens the nose
  and pushes the ears back, reading as an intimate, phone-in-hand vlog look.
  Correct for UGC and casual selfie-style delivery.
- 50mm at a natural conversational distance keeps proportions close to how a
  person actually looks in person, the safest default for talking-head content.
- 85-105mm lets the camera sit farther back while still filling frame with a
  face, mildly flattening features and softly compressing the background, the
  classic "interview" or "beauty" portrait length.
- State both the focal length and the implied camera distance together, since
  the same focal length reads differently framed as a bust shot versus a
  shoulders-up shot.

Why: portrait focal length is a deliberate flattery-versus-intimacy trade,
professional photographers pick 85mm for polish and 35mm for closeness on
purpose, so naming the length signals which emotional register the shot is
going for, not just a technical camera fact.

Example: "85mm portrait length, camera at eye level roughly six feet back,
shoulders-up frame, soft compression on the background."
Counter-example: "close-up on the face" with no focal length leaves the model
free to default to a wide-lens-close-distance look, which can subtly distort
the face in a way that reads as off-model rather than intentional.
