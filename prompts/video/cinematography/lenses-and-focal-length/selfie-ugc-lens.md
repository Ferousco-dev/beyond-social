Real phone front cameras sit around 24-26mm equivalent, and that specific
focal length carries a recognizable near-far exaggeration that audiences
unconsciously read as "actually filmed on a phone," which is worth naming
explicitly when the goal is UGC authenticity rather than production polish.

- Name "26mm phone front-camera lens" or "iPhone selfie focal length" rather
  than generic "close-up," to pull in the correct arm's-length distortion
  signature.
- Let the extended arm holding the phone (or an implied one) enter the bottom
  of frame slightly off-angle, matching how people actually hold phones, not
  a perfectly centered, level composition.
- Combine with a slightly-too-close working distance: subject's hand or
  product held toward camera should look oversized relative to their face,
  the classic UGC review gesture.
- Avoid pairing this focal length with studio-smooth camera movement; the wide
  selfie look reads as fake when it doesn't also carry the small handheld
  sway of someone actually holding a phone.

Why: the wide selfie focal length is inseparable from the cultural meaning of
"unscripted, filmed by a real person," so naming the specific lens rather than
a generic close framing is what makes the output register as UGC instead of
as a studio close-up dressed down to look casual.

Example: "26mm phone selfie lens, arm's-length distance, product held up
toward camera looking slightly oversized, subject's face just off-center."
Counter-example: "vlog-style close-up, handheld" without the focal length
risks a tighter, flatter portrait lens that reads as an interview setup
wearing a UGC costume rather than genuine phone footage.
