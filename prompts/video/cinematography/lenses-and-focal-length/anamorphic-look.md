Anamorphic lenses squeeze a wide field of view onto a standard frame and
un-squeeze it in post, and that optical process leaves a specific, nameable
signature that reads instantly as "shot on film-style cinema glass" rather
than a standard spherical lens.

- Oval, not circular, bokeh: out-of-focus highlights stretch horizontally,
  name it directly ("out-of-focus lights render as horizontal ovals, not
  circles").
- Horizontal blue streak flares rather than the round, multi-colored ghosting
  of a spherical lens; specify "flare stretches in a thin blue horizontal
  streak across the frame" when a light source hits the lens.
- Slightly softer corners and a subtle vertical compression to faces are part
  of the authentic signature, not a defect, worth naming if the goal is a
  premium, cinema-trailer feel.
- Reserve this look for hero brand moments or a cinematic cold open; it reads
  as overproduced if used across an entire fast-cut UGC-style ad.

Why: anamorphic characteristics are specific enough that naming generic terms
like "cinematic" without the oval bokeh and horizontal flare vocabulary
produces a vaguely nicer-looking spherical-lens image rather than the
distinct anamorphic signature audiences associate with big-budget film.

Example: "anamorphic lens character, oval bokeh from the string lights
behind the subject, thin horizontal blue flare as they turn toward the window."
Counter-example: "cinematic lens flare" alone gives no shape or direction,
likely producing a generic round multicolor flare that doesn't read as
anamorphic to anyone who recognizes the real thing.
