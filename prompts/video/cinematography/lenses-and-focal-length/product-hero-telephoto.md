Product photographers reach for a short telephoto, typically 90-135mm on a
still camera or the equivalent field of view in video, to isolate a product
from its surroundings without the flattening plainness of a plain studio
backdrop.

- Specify 100mm-equivalent telephoto with the camera pulled back rather than a
  standard lens pushed in close, since the compression (not just the magnification)
  is what stacks background elements into a soft, unified color field behind
  the product.
- Keep the product itself on a single focal plane facing the lens; telephoto
  compression makes any front-to-back tilt in the product read as more
  softened blur than intended, since depth of field narrows as focal length grows.
- Pair with a mid aperture like f/4 rather than the widest stop, so packaging
  text and logo stay legible while the background still separates.
- Use this length for the "reveal" or "beauty" shot in a product video,
  reserve wider lenses for shots that need to show the product in context
  or in a hand.

Why: telephoto compression is what makes a product look like it is floating
in considered, designed space rather than sitting in a room, because it
removes the perspective cues (converging lines, size falloff) that reveal an
ordinary environment behind it.

Example: "100mm telephoto, camera six feet back, product centered and sharp,
background reduced to a smooth gradient of bokeh."
Counter-example: "close-up on the product" using an implied wide lens pushed
in tight will show background elements with exaggerated size and perspective
distortion instead of the smooth isolated look the shot is going for.
