When a prompt sequence or a multi-clip generation covers several shots inside
one continuous scene, unstated or drifting focal length between shots is a
quiet but reliable tell, because real productions shoot a scene with a
deliberate, limited set of lenses, not a different optical character every cut.

- Pick two or at most three focal lengths for a scene (for example a 35mm
  wide and an 85mm close-up) and reuse them across every shot in that scene,
  naming the same number in each prompt.
- Keep compression character consistent: if the wide shot has natural,
  uncompressed depth, the matching close-up should use a length that doesn't
  suddenly flatten the same background into a different-looking space.
- When cutting from a wide establishing shot to a closer shot of the same
  subject, jump focal length meaningfully (35mm to 85mm, not 35mm to 40mm),
  matching how editors avoid a "jump cut" feeling by making the size change
  obvious rather than incremental.
- If the product or scene reappears later in the piece, reuse the exact same
  focal length used the first time that setup was shot, treating it as part
  of the scene's visual continuity, not a free variable per shot.

Why: a real crew works from a fixed lens kit and blocks a scene with intent,
so consistent focal length across cuts is a byproduct of real production
logistics; drifting compression or distortion between shots of the same space
breaks the illusion of a single continuous location shot by one camera setup.

Example: "shot 1: 35mm wide of the counter and barista. shot 2: 85mm
close-up on the cup, same counter, same light, same compression family."
Counter-example: describing shot 1 as a wide establishing shot and shot 2 as
simply "close-up" with no focal length risks two visually incompatible
compression looks stitched into what should read as one continuous space.
