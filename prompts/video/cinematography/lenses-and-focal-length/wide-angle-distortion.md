Wide lenses stretch whatever sits near the frame edge or close to the lens,
which is desirable for energy and intimacy but destructive for facial accuracy
once the focal length drops much below 24mm at close range.

- Under 20mm close to a face: nose and forehead enlarge, ears and jaw recede,
  produces the "phone held too close" look, fine for comedic or exaggerated UGC,
  wrong for a brand spokesperson.
- Keep faces near frame center on wide lenses; distortion compounds sharply
  toward the edges, so a face at the edge of a 16mm frame will warp far more
  than the same face centered.
- If the shot needs a wide environmental feel but an accurate face, split the
  difference: name a 28-35mm lens rather than defaulting to "wide angle."
- For product shots, wide-angle distortion on straight edges (packaging,
  logos, text) reads as a flaw, not style, so keep hero product shots at 35mm
  or longer unless the distortion is an intentional stylistic choice.

Why: this is a known, physically motivated failure mode of short focal lengths,
naming it lets the prompt writer either lean into it on purpose for a punchy
selfie-vlog energy or explicitly avoid it by choosing a longer focal length,
instead of getting an accidental warped face that reads as an AI generation flaw.

Example: "28mm lens, subject centered in frame, arm's length selfie distance,
straight lines in the background kept true."
Counter-example: "wide shot of someone talking directly to camera, very close"
with no focal length named risks an ultra-wide default that balloons the
nose and chin in a way no real creator would intentionally shoot.
