Focus breathing is the slight change in a lens's field of view as it racks
focus, a byproduct of most real (non-cinema-corrected) lenses that most
viewers have absorbed unconsciously from decades of consumer and prosumer
footage, and naming it deliberately keeps a focus pull from looking too clean.

- Describe it directly: "as focus shifts from foreground to background, the
  frame breathes very slightly wider," rather than only describing the focus
  change.
- Keep the effect small, a few percent of frame width at most; heavy breathing
  reads as a cheap zoom lens or a mistake, not a texture.
- Use it on any shot with a visible rack focus, since a pull with zero frame
  change is what an idealized cinema lens does, and idealized-everything is
  exactly the flatness that reads as synthetic.
- Skip mentioning it on locked-off shots with no focus movement; there is
  nothing for it to attach to.

Why: real zoom and prime lenses (outside of expensive cinema glass with
floating elements designed to suppress this) subtly shift magnification when
focus changes, so a video that racks focus with a perfectly stable frame is
missing a texture that trained eyes, even non-technical ones, register as
"too smooth to be a real camera."

Example: "rack focus from the sign in the foreground to the storefront
behind it, a faint breathing widening of the frame as focus lands."
Counter-example: describing only "focus shifts to the background" with an
otherwise perfectly stable frame produces the over-corrected, cinema-lens-perfect
look that under-sells the shot's handmade origin.
