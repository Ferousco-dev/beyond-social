Composition directs the eye inside the frame. A few durable rules cover most shots,
and they translate directly into prompt language.

- Rule of thirds: place the subject or its key feature (an eye, a horizon) on a
  third line, not dead center, unless symmetry is the point.
- Headroom and lead room: leave a little space above a head, and space in the
  direction a subject looks or moves, so the frame does not feel cramped.
- Leading lines: roads, rails, edges that draw the eye to the subject.
- Depth in layers: a foreground element, the subject, a background separates planes
  and makes 2D footage feel dimensional.
- Negative space: emptiness is composition; it isolates and elevates a subject.

For vertical short-form, recompose for 9:16: subjects sit higher, centered
horizontally, with detail concentrated in the middle third that survives cropping.

Why: centered, flat, evenly-filled frames read as amateur and "AI-default."
Intentional composition is a large part of what makes footage feel directed.

Example: "Medium shot, subject on the left third looking right into open space,
foreground bokeh of leaves, background street soft." Counter-example: "person
centered, filling the frame, plain background, no depth."
