A person in frame reads as more natural off-center, placed on a thirds line with
their eyes near the upper third intersection; a hero product reads as more
deliberate and worshipped when placed dead-center, symmetrical to the frame edges.

- People: place the eyes on the upper-third line, body on a vertical third,
  leaving the other two-thirds as lookroom or environment.
- Hero product beauty shots (perfume, phone, packaging): center the object on
  both axes, background symmetric or evenly lit on both sides.
- In 9:16 vertical, keep the subject inside the middle 60% of frame width so it
  survives crop to 1:1 or 4:5 on other placements.
- Do not center a person doing a UGC-style talking-head read; centered people
  read as mugshots or ID photos, which is a common AI-generated-video tell.
- Do not off-center a hero product past a thirds line unless copy space is the
  reason; an off-center hero product reads as an accident, not a choice.

Why: centering is a claim of importance and stillness, which suits an inanimate
object being presented for inspection. Off-center placement mimics how a human
eye naturally frames another human, leaving room for gesture, gaze, and reaction,
which is why documentary and UGC footage almost never centers a face.

Example: "product bottle centered in frame, symmetrical soft shadow, no crop"
paired with "presenter's eyes on the upper-left third line, body angled right."
Counter-example: a talking-head shot with the face locked dead-center for the
entire duration — it reads as a passport photo, not a person talking to camera.
