Where the camera sits relative to the subject's eyeline is a statement about
who or what holds authority in the frame, before a single word of dialogue.

- Eye-level (camera at the subject's eye height): neutral and relatable, the
  default for UGC talking-heads and testimonial-style delivery.
- Low angle (camera below eyeline, tilted up): the subject reads as
  aspirational, confident, larger than the viewer; use for founder or hero
  brand-voice moments, sparingly, since overuse feels like propaganda.
- High angle (camera above eyeline, tilted down): diminishes or humanizes a
  subject, useful for vulnerable or confiding beats, not for hero shots.
- Product hero angle: roughly 15-30 degrees above the product's horizontal
  centerline, high enough to read the top surface and logo, low enough to keep
  the object looking substantial rather than flattened like a diagram.
- Do not default every shot to eye-level; a scene with no height variation
  across its beats feels visually inert even if the content changes.

Why: camera height exploits the same instinct humans use reading real social
space — looking up at someone signals their dominance, looking down signals
vulnerability. Because generated video has no cinematographer improvising on
set, this has to be decided and written explicitly per shot, matched to what
that beat of the script is actually saying about the subject.

Example: "camera at the presenter's eye level for the testimonial line, tripod
height, no tilt" versus "low angle, camera at waist height tilted up 10 degrees,
for the confident brand statement."
Counter-example: shooting a founder's vulnerable admission from a low
aspirational angle — the visual grammar contradicts what the line is saying.
