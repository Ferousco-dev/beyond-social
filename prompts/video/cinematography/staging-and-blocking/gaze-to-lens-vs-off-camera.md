Whether a subject looks directly into the lens or slightly off it changes
whether the footage reads as a direct pitch to the viewer or as an observed,
documentary moment, and a vague "looking at camera" instruction tends to
produce a flat, unmotivated stare that is one of the clearer AI-avatar tells.

- Direct-to-lens (eyes on the exact optical center): use for direct-response
  hooks and CTAs, where the intent is to address the viewer personally.
- Off-lens by roughly 5-10 degrees, toward an implied interviewer or a point
  just past the camera: use for narrative, testimonial, or documentary-style
  delivery, where the subject appears to be talking to a person, not a lens.
- Always give the gaze a motivated target, even off-lens — a specific point,
  not "looking around" — since an unmotivated gaze drifts and looks unfocused
  rather than natural.
- When cutting between a direct-to-lens hook and an off-lens body section,
  stage the shift as a deliberate beat (a slight head turn into the CTA line),
  not an unexplained jump between two gaze states.
- Do not describe a stare with no blink or micro-movement implied; specify a
  natural gaze that can include a blink or brief glance-away, since a fixed,
  unblinking direct stare for an entire clip is a common uncanny-valley signal.

Why: direct-to-lens address exploits the same psychology as eye contact in a
real conversation, borrowing trust and urgency, which is why direct-response
ads use it for the ask. Off-lens gaze borrows the credibility of something
being observed rather than performed, which suits proof and storytelling. Both
only work when the gaze target is specific enough for the model to render
believable focus rather than a vacant, centered stare.

Example: "eyes locked on lens for the CTA, direct address" preceded by "gaze
angled slightly past camera-left, addressing an implied listener, natural
occasional blink" for the story section.
Counter-example: "person looking at the camera" for the entire clip with no
target or variation specified — produces the flat, wide-eyed, unblinking stare
common to unrefined AI avatar output.
