A talking-avatar script has beats that carry different weight, and the frame
size should shift with them instead of holding one static shot for the whole
clip, which is what makes AI avatar videos feel like a webcam recording rather
than an edited piece.

- Hook (first 1-2 seconds): tight close-up, chin to just above the eyebrows,
  maximizes facial expression and stops the scroll.
- Explanation / body: medium shot, mid-chest to just above the head, gives room
  for hand gesture without cutting off elbows, the standard trust-building frame.
- Demonstration (holding a product, pointing at an on-screen graphic): medium-
  wide, waist up, wide enough that hands and the object being shown both stay
  inside frame with margin.
- CTA / close: return to close-up or push in slightly from the body shot,
  mirroring how a real presenter leans toward camera for emphasis.
- Do not hold one identical medium shot from hook to CTA; a locked static frame
  for 30+ seconds reads as a static render, not a shot list.

Why: real single-camera talking-head content is still edited, even if it looks
like one continuous take. Editors punch in and out to match energy to content,
and a fixed, unchanging frame size is one of the more reliable tells that a
video was generated rather than shot and cut.

Example: "0-2s: tight close-up, chin to brow. 2-18s: medium shot, chest up,
gesturing hand entering frame. 18-22s: push to close-up for the offer line."
Counter-example: one unbroken medium shot from the first word to the last —
technically correct, but reads as flat and unedited next to real UGC.
