A shot composed to fill the entire frame edge-to-edge breaks the moment a
platform crops it to a different aspect ratio or lays interface chrome over it,
so critical elements need to be staged inside a safe interior margin from the
start, not adjusted after the fact.

- Keep the subject's full head and any product logo at least 8-10% in from
  every frame edge, so a repost or auto-crop to a tighter ratio doesn't clip
  through them.
- On 9:16, treat the bottom 15% and top 12% of frame as UI-occupied territory
  (captions, follow button, username, progress bar) and keep essential visual
  information out of those bands.
- Never stage a body joint (wrist, elbow, ankle, neck) exactly at the frame
  edge; a crop or reframe that lands precisely on a joint reads as an amputation
  even at a glance, whereas cropping mid-forearm or mid-shin reads as normal.
- If the same generation will be repurposed across 9:16, 1:1, and 16:9, stage
  the subject and product within the safe zone common to the tightest of the
  three ratios, not the widest.
- Do not stage a wide two-shot with subjects pushed to the extreme left and
  right edges; a center or 1:1 crop will lose one of them entirely.

Why: a single generated clip is frequently repurposed across ratios and
platforms that each add their own UI on top, and none of that cropping or
overlay happens with awareness of what's precious in the frame. Staging with
margin built in is cheaper than regenerating a shot because a logo got clipped
or a caption sat on top of a face.

Example: "subject staged with head and shoulders kept 10% clear of all frame
edges, product logo held well clear of the bottom UI band on vertical format."
Counter-example: a product logo staged flush against the bottom edge of a 9:16
frame — invisible the moment the platform's caption bar renders over it.
