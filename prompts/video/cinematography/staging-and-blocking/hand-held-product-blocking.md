A product held up to camera in UGC-style footage needs its own blocking rules,
distinct from how a person is staged, because the object's label, shape, and
scale all have to survive a handheld, moving frame.

- Hold the product at a slight three-quarter angle to camera, not flat and
  perpendicular to the lens; a flat-on angle flattens the packaging and hides
  its depth, while a hard profile hides the label.
- Bring the hand in from below or the side of frame, not from dead center or
  from directly behind the product; entering from below reads as a natural
  reach, entering from center reads as the object materializing in frame.
- Keep the product at a consistent distance from the lens for the duration it's
  being shown, roughly forearm's length; drifting closer during a hold causes
  distortion on wide-angle phone-style lenses that undercuts the product shape.
- Angle the label toward camera and slightly up, matching how someone actually
  tilts a product to make text legible for another person, not flat to the sky.
- Do not center the hand and fingers as the visual subject; the product should
  occupy the compositional weight, with fingers doing the minimum necessary
  work to hold it steady, visible at frame edges rather than wrapped over text.

Why: this blocking mirrors how a real person shows something to a friend — they
angle it, hold it at a stable working distance, and keep their grip out of the
way of what they're showing. Skipping any of these steps is what makes AI
product-hold shots look like the product is floating near a hand rather than
being held by one.

Example: "product held at three-quarter angle, forearm's length from lens,
entering frame from the lower right, label tilted up toward camera."
Counter-example: product held flat and centered, dead-on to the lens, fingers
wrapped fully across the label — the shape reads as a flat rectangle and the
brand name is unreadable.
