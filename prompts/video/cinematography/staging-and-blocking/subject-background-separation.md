Selective focus only isolates a subject from clutter if the subject is
physically staged far enough from the background for the lens to actually
separate the two planes; describing "shallow depth of field" over a subject
standing flush against a wall gives the model nothing to defocus.

- Stage the subject at least several feet in front of any background surface
  or clutter, stating the gap explicitly ("standing well clear of the wall
  behind them") rather than assuming distance.
- For product shots, keep the product on a surface with visible falloff behind
  it (a table receding into blur) rather than a product flush against a
  seamless backdrop, which reads flat even with a blur instruction.
- Pair the staged distance with a stated shallow-focus cue ("background soft
  and out of focus") so the two directions reinforce each other.
- When the background needs to stay legible (an establishing or context shot),
  reduce the staged gap instead of fighting it with a deep-focus instruction.
- Do not rely on the word "bokeh" alone to fix a flat composition; bokeh
  describes the quality of blur, not the staging that creates the blur at all.

Why: real lens defocus is a function of physical distance between planes, not
a filter — a subject standing a foot from a wall cannot be separated from it no
matter what aperture is named, because there's no depth for the optics to work
with. Staging the gap first is what makes a defocus instruction physically
plausible rather than just a keyword floating over a flat scene.

Example: "subject staged several feet clear of the background wall, background
softly out of focus, clear separation between subject and surroundings."
Counter-example: "subject against the wall, shallow depth of field, blurred
background" — the staging contradicts the blur instruction and the model has
no real gap to render as out of focus.
