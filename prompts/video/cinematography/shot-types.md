Shot size is the single most useful word in a video prompt because it fixes the
subject-to-frame relationship. Name it explicitly; do not let the model guess.

The ladder, from far to near:

- Extreme wide / establishing: subject small in the environment. Sets place.
- Wide / full: whole subject, some context. Shows action and body language.
- Medium: waist up. The workhorse for people talking or handling something.
- Close-up: face or object fills the frame. Emotion and detail.
- Extreme close-up / macro: texture, a single feature. Intimacy or product detail.

Use size to control meaning: cut wider to orient, closer to intensify. For product
work, macro sells material and craftsmanship; for a person, medium builds trust and
close-up builds connection. Short-form usually lives in medium-to-close because the
frame is small and the subject must read instantly.

Why: without an explicit size the model averages to a nondescript medium-wide, the
most generic and least intentional framing. Naming the size is free direction.

Example: "Extreme close-up of a hand pressing a mechanical keyboard key, macro,
shallow focus." Counter-example: "a video of someone typing" (no size, no intent).
