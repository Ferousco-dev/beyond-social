A background that holds still or loops a generic texture reads as a
backdrop. Giving the midground or background independent, motivated activity
unrelated to the main subject makes the space read as a continuous world the
camera happens to be in.

- Name one small, self-contained background action distinct from the main
  subject's action: a barista steaming milk, a cyclist passing the window,
  curtains shifting in a draft.
- Keep it minor in scale and screen time relative to the subject; background
  life should support depth, not compete for attention.
- Give it its own timing that doesn't sync with the subject's movements;
  unsynchronized motion reads as independent, real activity, while
  synchronized motion reads as choreographed or looped.
- Prefer activity with a visible cause, wind, a passerby, steam, over ambient
  shimmer with no source.

Why: real locations are never inert; something is always moving somewhere in
frame, traffic, foliage, other people. Its absence is a strong tell of a
generated "stage set" scene built only around the subject, because a model
defaults to rendering only what was explicitly asked for and nothing more.

Example: "in the background, a cyclist crosses the far side of the street,
unrelated to and unsynced with the subject's motion in the foreground."

Counter-example: "empty street behind the subject, nothing else moving"
reads as a set built around one actor rather than a place other people
actually pass through.
