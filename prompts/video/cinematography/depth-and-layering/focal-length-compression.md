The implied focal length changes how much space appears to exist between
foreground, midground, and background: telephoto lenses compress planes
together, wide lenses expand the distance between them. Naming that choice
controls whether the layering reads as tight and dramatic or open and
spacious.

- For a compressed, stacked look, a crowd feeling dense, background looming
  large behind the subject, specify "telephoto compression, 135mm look,
  background magnified and close-feeling."
- For an open, spacious layering, subject small against an environment,
  foreground and background clearly separated in distance, specify "wide
  angle, 24mm look, background feels pulled back."
- Match focal length to emotional intent: compression for tension, crowding,
  or urgency; wide expansion for scale, isolation, or establishing context.
- Do not mix compression cues, a magnified distant background, with
  wide-angle cues, exaggerated near-object size, in the same shot
  description; they contradict each other optically.

Why: perspective compression is a function of camera-to-subject distance and
focal length, not a stylistic filter. Naming a specific focal length gives
the model a physically consistent recipe for how much the planes should
visually separate or stack, rather than leaving the amount of blur or
spacing arbitrary.

Example: "telephoto 85mm look, layers of pedestrians stacking toward the
camera, background storefronts compressed close behind the subject."

Counter-example: "zoomed in but background still looks far away and small"
contradicts telephoto compression physics and reads as an inconsistent,
synthetic composite.
