A generated frame reads flat when everything sits at one visual distance from
the lens. Staging distinct elements at foreground, midground, and background
gives the camera something to parallax through and the eye something to
travel across.

- Name an object for each plane in the prompt: a foreground object partially
  in frame (out of focus is fine), a midground subject doing the main action,
  and a background element with its own texture or motion.
- Keep at least one plane closer than arm's reach to the lens, and one plane
  far enough to soften with depth of field or haze.
- Give the background something to do that isn't static wallpaper: passing
  traffic, drifting steam, a second person, so it reads as a place, not a
  backdrop.
- Avoid placing all key elements at a single distance from camera, the
  classic "line of objects" arrangement that reads as a stage flat.

Why: real cameras record actual depth because of lens optics and true
parallax between objects at different distances. Text-to-video models default
to centering and isolating the described subject, which collapses everything
onto one plane unless separate depths are named explicitly. Naming three
planes forces the render to commit to real spatial separation instead of a
cardboard-cutout arrangement.

Example: "foreground: blurred cafe railing at the bottom edge of frame;
midground: woman drinking coffee at a table; background: street traffic
passing forty feet behind her, softly out of focus."

Counter-example: "woman drinking coffee, plain empty background" reads like a
studio cutout, since there is no spatial information for the camera to
reveal.
