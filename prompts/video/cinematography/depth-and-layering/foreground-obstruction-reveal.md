Moving the camera or subject past a blurred foreground obstruction mid-shot,
a post, a passing vehicle, a doorway edge, briefly occludes and then reveals
the scene, a specific and learnable proof of real spatial depth that a
locked-off flat shot cannot offer.

- Place an out-of-focus object between the lens and the action early in the
  move's path, a pillar, a parked car, a doorframe, so it enters and exits
  frame naturally as the camera or subject travels.
- Time the occlusion briefly, roughly a quarter to half second of the
  subject being fully or partly blocked, not a sustained blackout.
- Pair it with a lateral move, a truck, or the subject walking across camera,
  rather than a push-in; parallax past a near object needs sideways motion
  to read.
- Use it at a scene's start or a beat change, revealing a new subject or
  location, rather than mid-action, where it would read as an accidental
  obstruction instead of a device.

Why: this move only works because the world has physical objects at multiple
distances the camera must travel past, a spatial constraint a flat,
unobstructed render has no reason to reproduce. Specifying it forces the
model to commit to an object nearer than the lens's focus target, not just a
background.

Example: "camera trucks right, a blurred lamppost sweeps through the
foreground briefly obscuring the subject before she's fully revealed walking
on the far side."

Counter-example: "camera moves smoothly past the subject with a clear
unobstructed view throughout" has no near-plane object, so the move cannot
demonstrate any parallax depth at all.
