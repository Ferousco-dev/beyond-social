Pulling focus from one plane to another mid-shot is a specific, nameable
technique that reveals depth and directs attention, and reads as a
deliberate choice made by a person with a follow-focus, not a rendering
default.

- Specify direction and trigger: "rack focus from the sign in the foreground
  to the runner approaching in the background as she enters frame."
- Keep the pull motivated by an event, a character turning, a phone lighting
  up, rather than arbitrary; real focus pulls in production are cued to
  action, not timed to a clock.
- Hold each end of the pull for at least half a second so both planes
  register before and after the shift, rather than blurring constantly
  throughout the clip.
- Don't rack focus more than once in a short clip; repeated pulls in a few
  seconds read as an effect, not a technique.

Why: a focus pull is a manual, mechanically constrained operation, a focus
puller turning a wheel against marks on the barrel. Naming it invokes that
physical vocabulary and its natural micro-imperfection, a hair of lag or
overshoot, which generic AI output otherwise skips in favor of an
instantaneous or randomly wandering focus shift.

Example: "rack focus from blurred foreground flowers to the couple walking
up the path as they come into frame, focus settling on their faces."

Counter-example: "focus shifts around the scene" gives no motivated trigger
and no endpoint, likely rendering as a random focus wobble rather than a
purposeful pull.
