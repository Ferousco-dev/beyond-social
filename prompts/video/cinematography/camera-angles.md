Angle and lens carry emotion before content does. State both when they matter.

Angles:

- Eye level: neutral, honest. Default for talking and UGC.
- Low angle (looking up): power, scale, heroism. Great for product-as-hero.
- High angle (looking down): smallness, vulnerability, or a clean flat-lay.
- Overhead / top-down: order and process; ideal for hands, food, unboxings.
- Dutch tilt: unease, energy; use sparingly.

Lens / focal length:

- Wide (~18-24mm look): expansive, exaggerated depth, slight distortion up close.
- Normal (~35-50mm): natural, how the eye sees.
- Telephoto (~85mm+): compressed background, flattering faces, strong subject
  separation and bokeh.

Pair intent with choice: a low-angle telephoto makes a sneaker monumental; an eye
level normal lens makes a founder feel trustworthy.

Why: unspecified angle and lens default to a flat, mid-focal snapshot. Naming them
is the difference between a photograph of a thing and a shot with a point of view.

Example: "Low-angle macro on the watch, 100mm look, compressed background,
shallow focus." Counter-example: "nice shot of a watch."
