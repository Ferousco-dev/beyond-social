Car interiors at night are lit by a specific small set of practicals, each
with its own color, direction and duration: dashboard glow, oncoming
headlights, and streetlights passing overhead.

- Dashboard or instrument glow is dim and steady, cool blue-green or amber
  depending on the vehicle, uplighting the lower face and hands from below.
- Oncoming headlights are brief and hard, a bright white flash sweeping across
  the face in under a second, then gone.
- Streetlights passing overhead pulse dim-bright-dim on the ceiling and upper
  face, faster than a walking scene since the car is moving.
- Keep ambient cabin light low overall; real car interiors at night are mostly
  dark except for these transient hits.
- Let a faint reflection of the dashboard appear on the inside of the
  windshield glass.

Why: a moving vehicle passes multiple isolated sources in quick succession, so
lighting on an occupant's face should be genuinely dynamic and momentary
rather than a single soft key tinted blue to suggest "night driving."

Example: "dim blue dashboard uplight on the hands and chin, oncoming
headlights sweeping across the face in a brief hard flash, cabin otherwise
dark."

Counter-example: "soft constant blue light on the driver's face for the whole
scene." No transient headlight hits means it reads as a gel on a key light,
not a real drive.
