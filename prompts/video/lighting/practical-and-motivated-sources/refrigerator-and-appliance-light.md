Small appliance practicals (an open fridge, an oven window, a microwave, a
closet light) are brief and contained, casting a distinct box-shaped light
with hard edges that only exists while the door or panel stays open.

- Fridge light reads cool white (roughly 4000-5000K) at moderate brightness,
  casting upward on the face if the subject is looking down and in, with a
  hard rectangular edge matching the door opening.
- The light exists only while the door is open; as it starts to close, the
  light should visibly dim, not hold as a static state.
- Keep falloff fast and steep given the small opening: only the immediate
  area and the subject's face or hands close to it are affected, the rest of
  the kitchen stays on ambient room light.
- An oven window is warmer and dimmer, a glow-through-glass quality rather
  than a hard-edged opening.
- Set a genuinely dim ambient room light behind it so the appliance glow reads
  as a distinct, secondary source instead of getting lost in an already-bright
  kitchen.

Why: these are transient, geometrically defined light events tied to a
physical action, opening a door, so treating the light as a fixed ambient wash
rather than an action-linked, edge-defined pool breaks the connection between
the visible cause and its effect.

Example: "fridge door open, cool light spilling out in a hard-edged rectangle,
illuminating the subject's face from below and in front, rest of the kitchen
dim."

Counter-example: "kitchen evenly bright with the fridge open in the
background." The appliance's light contributes nothing distinguishable, so the
open door becomes decorative rather than a real light source.
