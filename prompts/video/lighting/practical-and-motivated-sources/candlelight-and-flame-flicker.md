Flame-based practicals (candles, fireplaces, matches) light through constant
small fluctuations in intensity and color, and a flat, steady "warm light"
fails to sell fire as the actual source.

- Keep flicker irregular, roughly 2-6 fluctuations per second with no steady
  rhythm; true randomness, not a metronomic pulse.
- Let color drift warmer and deeper (near 1800-1900K) at low troughs and
  slightly brighter and yellower at peaks.
- Keep the light small and close: falloff is steep, lighting only the near
  side of the face or hand within about a foot or two, with the background
  staying dark.
- Let shadows shift slightly with the flame rather than locking them static.
- Where the flame sits below eye level, cast shadows upward from the chin and
  nose, a specific cue that distinguishes flame from any other low practical.

Why: flame is a chemical combustion process, not a manufactured filament, so
its instability and steep falloff are physically inevitable. Smoothing it into
a constant warm glow removes the one visual cue that separates candlelight
from a gelled studio light standing in for it.

Example: "single candle below frame, warm flickering uplight on the face,
shadows shifting slightly, everything past arm's length falling to black."

Counter-example: "steady warm orange light described as candlelight." Constant
intensity with no fluctuation gives away an artificial source pretending to be
a flame.
