A subject walking at night moves through discrete pools of streetlamp light
with dark gaps between them, and exposure and color should shift as they cross
those pools rather than stay constant.

- Sodium-vapor streetlamps read warm amber-orange (around 2200K) with poor
  color rendering, giving skin a faint yellow-green cast.
- Modern LED streetlamps read cooler white to blue (4000-5000K) with a
  harsher-edged falloff.
- Between lamps, let the subject genuinely dim for a beat rather than holding
  constant brightness the whole walk.
- Cast a small, hard-edged pool of light on the ground under each lamp; the
  subject brightens entering it and dims leaving it.
- Let shadow direction shift or flip as the subject passes under and beyond
  each lamp's overhead position.

Why: streetlamps are isolated point sources spaced yards apart, so real
nighttime footage cycles in brightness as a subject moves through it. Keeping
a walking subject evenly lit the entire time implies an invisible light rig
following them, something no real street produces.

Example: "subject walks between streetlamps, brightening under each amber pool
of light and dimming in the gaps between them."

Counter-example: "subject evenly lit throughout a night walk." This implies a
lighting rig tracking them and is a classic tell of an ungrounded generated
scene.
