A laptop, phone, or TV screen as the only light source produces a dim, cool,
low-contrast wash, and it reads as authentic only when the light's brightness
tracks what's actually happening on screen.

- Use a cool color temperature, roughly 6500-9000K, with a slight blue or cyan
  cast on skin.
- Tie brightness or subtle flicker to on-screen content; a bright cut on the
  screen should visibly lift the light on the face for a beat.
- Keep output low: screens are dim sources, so the surrounding room should stay
  mostly dark or underexposed, not brightly lit to compensate.
- Let the light fall off fast, mainly hitting the near side of the face or
  hands within two or three feet, with little reaching anything farther back.
- Give the eyes a rectangular catchlight matching the screen's shape, not a
  generic round highlight.

Why: screens are unusually dim and spectrally narrow sources. Holding exposure
low and the color cool is what actually sells "this room has no other light,"
while brightening the rest of the scene contradicts the stated source and
reveals an added, unmotivated key.

Example: "subject's face lit only by a laptop's glow, cool blue cast, rest of
the room falling to near black, a rectangular catchlight visible in the eyes."

Counter-example: "screen glow plus a fully exposed, bright room." This
contradicts the stated single source and gives away that the actual lighting
isn't coming from anything in frame.
