Electrical practicals have their own small, real instabilities, from
fluorescent flicker to LED PWM strobing to a loose bulb connection, and
referencing the right one for the right fixture adds a specific authenticity
cue.

- Fluorescent tubes carry a fast, near-imperceptible flicker at mains
  frequency, a slight greenish color cast, and a slow warm-up when first
  switched on.
- Cheap or older LED fixtures can show a faster strobe or PWM flicker,
  especially if it's described as caught mid-frame rather than smooth.
- An old or failing bulb dims irregularly, with a visible half-second before
  it settles, or a faint buzz-synced brightness ripple.
- Overhead fluorescent panels in offices or stores read flat and slightly
  cool-green, evenly distributed, but with the ceiling grid's shadow pattern
  faintly visible.
- Use this sparingly and only where the fixture type justifies it; a clean
  new LED panel or daylight has no reason to flicker.

Why: real electrical fixtures are imperfect manufactured objects with age,
wiring, and driver-circuit quirks, and naming the specific imperfection tied
to the specific fixture type is more convincing than a generic "flickering
light" note, which risks misapplying the wrong flicker signature to the wrong
source.

Example: "overhead fluorescent tubes with a faint greenish cast, one tube
flickering slightly at the edge of frame."

Counter-example: "a warm LED desk lamp flickering like an old fluorescent
tube." Mismatched fixture and behavior is a detail a real crew would never
write, and it quietly signals the scene wasn't observed, just generated.
