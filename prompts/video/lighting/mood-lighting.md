Contrast ratio sets emotional tone. Choose a lighting key deliberately:

- High-key: bright, low contrast, few shadows. Optimistic, clean, commercial.
  Beauty, food, lifestyle, upbeat ads.
- Low-key: dark, high contrast, deep shadows, selective light. Dramatic, premium,
  moody. Tech reveals, luxury, tension.
- Chiaroscuro: a single hard source carving the subject out of darkness. Striking
  and painterly; use for a hero moment.
- Practicals: lamps, neon, screens, candles visible in frame as motivated light
  sources. They add realism and color and anchor where light comes from.
- Color in light: gels and ambient color (a blue window, a red sign) set mood
  without changing the subject.

Match key to message: a budgeting app might want high-key trust; a whiskey brand
wants low-key warmth.

Why: naming the key and any practicals gives the model a coherent lighting logic,
which reads as intentional cinematography rather than a random exposure.

Example: "Low-key, single warm practical lamp lighting the subject's face, deep
shadows, dark room, neon sign glowing blue in the background." Counter-example:
"dramatic lighting" with no key, source, or contrast specified.
