Golden hour outdoor lighting is a nameable sun geometry, not just "warm light";
naming the geometry is what actually sells it.

- Sun angle is low, roughly 0-10 degrees above the horizon. Shadows are long,
  several times subject height, and stretch away from the sun.
- Place the sun behind or to the side of the subject for warm rim or backlight
  with soft edge flare, rather than lighting the face flat-on into harsh
  squinting light.
- Color shifts warm across the whole scene, roughly 3000-3500K, including fill:
  even shadow areas pick up warmth from nearby warm-lit surfaces, unlike
  midday's cool-shadow split.
- Name atmosphere if present: light haze or dust in the low sun catches visible
  rays, "god rays through the low sun, backlighting the subject's hair."

Why: "golden hour" alone under-specifies the geometry a working DP actually
controls, sun height, shadow length, and backlight placement, and naming those
prevents the model from defaulting to a generic warm color grade laid over
midday-style shadow behavior, which is a common and visible mismatch.

Example: "sun low on the horizon behind the subject, long shadows stretching
toward camera, warm rim light on hair and shoulders, faint lens flare at frame
edge."
Counter-example: "beautiful golden hour lighting" with no sun position or shadow
length specified, becoming a warm filter over otherwise midday shadow logic.
