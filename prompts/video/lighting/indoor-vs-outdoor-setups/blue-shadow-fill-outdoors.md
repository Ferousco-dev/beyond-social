On a sunny day, outdoor shadow areas are lit by the blue sky acting as fill, not
plain darkness. Naming this keeps outdoor shadows readable and color-correct
instead of defaulting to flat black.

- Describe shadow areas as "cool blue-tinted shadow," not "dark shadow." The sky
  itself is the fill source.
- State both temperatures in one shot for contrast: sunlit areas read warm
  (roughly 5000-5600K direct sun), shaded areas read cool (roughly 7000K+
  skylight).
- This warm-highlight, cool-shadow split is strongest at midday and weakens
  toward golden hour, when direct sun itself goes warm and shadow fill goes more
  neutral.
- A subject standing entirely in open shade (under a tree, an awning) is lit
  purely by sky-blue fill. Call it "lit entirely by open shade, cool and soft,"
  not "in the dark."

Why: this is one of the more reliable outdoor tells against indoor footage, where
shadow fill instead comes from bounced room light and skews toward whatever wall
or floor color surrounds it. Naming the sky's blue fill stops the model from
defaulting to neutral-grey or crushed-black shadow, which reads flat and
studio-lit rather than outdoors.

Example: "sunlit side of the face warm and bright, shadow side cool blue from
open sky, no artificial fill light."
Counter-example: "shadows are pure black" on a described clear day outdoors,
physically wrong since sky fill always lifts them above black.
