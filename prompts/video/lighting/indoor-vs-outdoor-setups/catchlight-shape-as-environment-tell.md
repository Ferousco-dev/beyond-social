The shape of the catchlight in a subject's eyes is a small but legible clue to
whether a scene reads as indoor or outdoor, and it's worth naming on any close-up.

- Indoors near a window, the catchlight is a soft rectangle or a set of
  window-pane rectangles (mullions). Name it in close-ups: "catchlight shows the
  rectangular shape of the window in the eyes."
- Indoors near a practical lamp or ring-style fixture, catchlights appear as a
  small warm dot or ring, off-axis and close to the pupil.
- Outdoors under open sky, the catchlight is a broad, soft highlight from the
  whole sky, not a single point; under direct sun it can also show one small,
  hard, bright point.
- On overcast days catchlights nearly vanish or read as a very dim, diffuse
  highlight, a face with no window and no direct sun but a strong sharp catchlight
  will look wrong.

Why: this is a detail most generic prompts skip, but for close talking-head shots
it's one of the cheapest words-to-realism-payoff cues available, and mismatching
it against the stated light source, a round sun-catchlight in a scene lit by a
window, is a subtle continuity break a trained eye catches immediately.

Example: "close-up, soft rectangular window catchlight visible in both eyes,
matching the stated window-light key."
Counter-example: describing a window-lit interior close-up with no catchlight
guidance, leaving the model free to render a mismatched round highlight that
contradicts the stated source.
