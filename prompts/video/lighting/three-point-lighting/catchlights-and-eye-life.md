A catchlight is the small reflected highlight of a light source visible in the
eye, and its presence or absence is one of the fastest things a viewer
subconsciously reads as "alive" versus "dead-eyed" in a face.

- The key light, if it is in front of and above the subject at all, produces a
  catchlight automatically at the position matching its angle — a key at
  camera-left-and-above puts the catchlight in the upper-left of the iris.
- A rim or backlight alone does not put a catchlight in the eyes, since it
  comes from behind the subject; a face lit only by rim and ambient fill
  routinely reads as flat and lifeless in close-up.
- For a close-up or talking-head shot specifically, state that the eyes should
  catch the key light source — this pulls the model's attention to rendering
  that highlight rather than leaving eyes as flat dark discs.
- Two catchlights from two visible sources (key and a secondary window, say)
  read as natural in a multi-source room; a single, isolated catchlight reads
  as a clean studio setup — either is fine, but pick one deliberately.

Why: human perception is unusually tuned to eye highlights as a liveness
signal, evolved for reading faces at conversational distance, so a close-up
face with no catchlight registers as subtly wrong even to viewers who cannot
say what's off.

Example: "close-up on the subject's face, key light camera-left and above,
catchlight visible in the upper-left of each eye."
Counter-example: "close-up, well lit face" with the key placed behind or
beside without regard to eye reflection — often renders flat, catchlight-less
eyes that read as subtly artificial.
