A three-point rig described as an abstract "key, fill, rim" reads as a studio
diagram. The same rig described as light coming from things actually visible
or implied in the scene reads as a place, not a set.

- Assign each of the three lights a real-world source: key becomes "window
  light," fill becomes "bounce off a light wall or the ceiling," rim becomes
  "the sun low behind the subject" or "a lamp in the next room."
- If the source is visible in frame (a window, a lamp, a screen), state its
  position relative to the subject so the light direction and the visible
  object agree with each other.
- Match intensity to the claimed source: a phone screen as a key light should
  be described as dim and blue-tinted, not bright and neutral — mismatched
  intensity-to-source is a fast tell.
- Let the "fill" be implicit ambient bounce from the room rather than a named
  second light when the scene is a home or office; not every setup needs three
  explicit sources.

Why: real footage is lit by things that are there for a reason — a window, a
lamp, the sky — and viewers read unmotivated, sourceless light as staged even
when they can't articulate why; naming the source ties the geometry of light
to the logic of the room.

Example: "key light from a large window camera-left, soft bounce fill off a
pale wall, warm rim from a lamp visible in the background behind the subject."
Counter-example: "three-point lighting setup, key fill and rim" with no
in-scene source — technically correct rig, but it floats independent of the
room and reads as a lit set rather than a real space.
