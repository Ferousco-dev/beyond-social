Talking-head and UGC-style avatar content reads as authentic when the lighting
matches what a normal creator actually has access to — a window, a cheap ring
light, a lamp — not a polished three-point studio rig, even though the same
key/fill/rim logic still applies underneath.

- Key: daylight from a window just off to one side, or a small ring light
  slightly above eye level, close to the face — soft, a little uneven, not a
  perfectly even wrap.
- Fill: whatever the room provides on its own — a pale wall, a second window —
  described as ambient rather than a dedicated second instrument.
- Rim: often skip it entirely, or let it be incidental (a lamp visible in the
  background, a bit of window light catching the hair) rather than a
  deliberately placed edge light — an obvious rim light reads as produced,
  which undercuts the UGC read.
- Let exposure be slightly imperfect: a touch of window blowout, a slightly
  underlit far side of the face — perfectly balanced exposure is itself a
  studio tell in this format.

Why: the entire value of the UGC aesthetic is that the lighting looks
found rather than designed, so importing full studio three-point discipline
into this format undercuts the authenticity it's supposed to sell, even
though the same directional logic (one dominant source, some fill, minimal or
no rim) still explains why the shot looks coherent.

Example: "soft window light from camera-left as the only real source, room
ambient fill, slightly uneven exposure across the face, handheld phone
framing."
Counter-example: a fully rigged three-point setup with a crisp visible rim
light on a UGC-style talking-head clip — technically correct lighting that
reads as an ad, not a real person's phone video.
