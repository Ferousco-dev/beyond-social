Rim light sits behind the subject, angled down onto the shoulders, hair, and
edge of the face, and its only job is to trace a bright edge that pulls the
subject off the background. It is the single fastest way to make flat footage
read as shot, not generated.

- Placement: behind the subject, roughly 30-45 degrees above and to one side,
  aimed back toward camera across the top of the shoulder or hairline.
- Intensity relative to key: a rim brighter than the key reads as a
  stylized halo (fine for a hero product beat); a rim at or slightly below key
  brightness reads as naturalistic separation.
- It needs a plausible source: late-sun through a window, a streetlight, a
  practical lamp behind the subject, a softbox just out of frame. Name one so
  the highlight has a physical reason to exist.
- On dark hair or dark clothing against a dark background, rim light is not
  optional — without it the subject's edge disappears into the background.

Why: without a described rim, the model tends to light background and subject
with the same flat wash, so edges blend together; a stated rear source with an
angle gives it a highlight to render along the silhouette, which is the depth
cue human vision uses to separate foreground from background.

Example: "warm rim light from a streetlamp behind the subject, tracing the
edge of the shoulder and hair, background falling into shadow."
Counter-example: "make the subject stand out from the background" with no
light described — the model has no mechanism to act on and usually falls back
to a generic vignette instead of an actual separation light.
