Low-angle golden-hour sun hitting any wet or reflective surface, ocean, lake, wet
sand, rain-slicked street, throws back a long specular highlight path toward
camera, a glitter path, which is a distinct phenomenon from the ambient warm
light itself and needs separate handling in a prompt.

The recipe:

- Run the glitter path directly between camera and sun position, breaking into
  individual sparkling highlights on textured water, waves, ripples, rather than
  one smooth streak; smoothness only appears on glass-flat water.
- Scale path width and brightness with sun elevation and surface roughness:
  choppier water makes a wider, more broken glitter field; calm water makes a
  narrow, near-mirror path.
- On wet pavement or sand, keep the same specular streak but shorter and warped
  by surface texture, tire tracks, footprints, an easy, cheap realism cue for
  street-level shots.
- Silhouette anything crossing the glitter path, a boat, a person, birds, to
  near-black with a bright rim, since it's backlit by the brightest part of the
  frame.
- Keep ambient exposure moderate so the glitter highlights read brighter than
  their surroundings; if the whole frame is already blown, the glitter path
  disappears into general overexposure.

Why: water and wet surfaces are specular, not diffuse, reflectors, they bounce
the sun's disc itself back toward the viewer rather than scattering it broadly,
and since the sun is a near-point source at golden hour, that reflection stays a
bright, narrow, textured streak instead of an even sheen.

Example: "golden hour, low sun behind camera-right, glitter path breaking across
choppy water toward camera, a silhouetted swimmer crossing it."

Counter-example: a warm-toned ocean shot with a smooth, uniform golden sheen
across the entire surface. No discrete glitter path, no wave-driven breakup; it
reads as a color grade, not a lit reflection.
