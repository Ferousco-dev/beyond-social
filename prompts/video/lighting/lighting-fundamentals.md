Light is the largest single lever on how professional footage looks, and it is
cheap to specify. Describe the **source, direction, and quality** of light and the
model renders depth instead of flatness.

- Direction: front (flat, safe), side (shape and texture), back/rim (separation
  from background, a premium halo), top, or under (unnatural, ominous). Side and
  back light almost always look more cinematic than flat front light.
- Quality: soft (diffused, gentle shadows, flattering, "big source") vs hard
  (crisp shadows, drama, "small source"). Name it.
- The classic setup: key (main), fill (softens shadows), back/rim (separates).
  You rarely need to spell out all three, but naming a key direction plus a rim is
  a reliable recipe.
- Color temperature: warm (tungsten, golden) vs cool (daylight, blue hour). It
  sets mood before anything else does.

Why: unspecified lighting defaults to flat, even, sourceless illumination, the
signature of amateur and AI footage. A named direction and quality instantly reads
as intentional.

Example: "Soft key from camera-left, warm rim light behind the subject separating
them from a dark background." Counter-example: "well lit, bright, clear."
