Wardrobe, hair state, and accessories need the same pinning discipline as
props, because clothing is the detail viewers track most instinctively when
judging whether a sequence was shot in one continuous session.

The recipe:

- Describe exact garment color, fit, and any distinguishing detail (rolled
  sleeves, unbuttoned collar) and repeat it verbatim across every shot.
- Lock hair state — tied back, loose, a specific side part — instead of
  letting "casual hairstyle" get reinterpreted differently each generation.
- Track state changes deliberately: if a jacket comes off between shots, name
  it as an explicit action in the prompt rather than letting it happen
  invisibly.
- Watch jewelry, glasses, and watches specifically — small accessories are the
  first thing to vanish or duplicate across generations if left unnamed.
- For a talking-avatar or spokesperson format, treat the outfit description as
  a fixed block of text copy-pasted into every shot prompt in the sequence.

Why: wardrobe continuity errors are a classic real-production embarrassment —
it's a large part of why script supervisors exist — because audiences are
primed to notice a shirt color or hairstyle changing between adjacent cuts.
Skipping this discipline in AI generation reproduces the exact same tell.

Example: "navy crew-neck sweater, sleeves pushed to forearm, hair in a low
ponytail" stated identically across all five shots of the sequence.
Counter-example: "business casual outfit" as the only wardrobe cue — shot 3
renders a blazer that was never established, and shot 5 has different hair
length.
