Keep every camera setup on one side of the action line (the 180-degree rule)
so screen direction and spatial relationships stay legible across cuts.

The recipe:

- Before generating multiple shots of one scene, define the action line — the
  line between two subjects, or a subject's direction of travel — and keep
  every shot's camera position described relative to the same side of it.
- State subject screen position explicitly per shot ("subject A remains
  screen-left, subject B screen-right"), not just "close-up of subject A."
- For a subject walking, keep their direction of travel across frame
  consistent shot to shot, unless a doorway, turn, or mirror motivates a
  reversal.
- If crossing the line is a deliberate creative choice, motivate it with a
  visible turn or a neutral cutaway rather than an unexplained jump.

Why: the 180-degree rule is how an edit preserves the viewer's internal map of
a space without ever showing the whole room at once. Break it silently and the
geography becomes unreadable — a face that should be looking toward another
subject suddenly appears to be looking away from them.

Example: "camera on the north side of the table throughout; subject A remains
screen-left, subject B remains screen-right" held across four shots.
Counter-example: shot 2 flips to the opposite side of the same conversation
with no motivated transition — the subjects appear to have swapped seats.
