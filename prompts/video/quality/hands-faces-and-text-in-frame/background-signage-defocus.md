Real environments are full of incidental text, street signs, store windows,
posters, packaging on a shelf, that a location scout or DP never worries about
because a shallow depth of field naturally dissolves it into shape and color; use
the same tool to keep background text out of the model's failure zone.

How to apply it:

- Set an explicit depth-of-field instruction (shallow depth of field, background
  soft, subject sharp) whenever the environment naturally contains signage,
  shelving, or screens, rather than leaving focus unspecified.
- Describe background elements by shape and color, not content: "a blurred row
  of shelved products behind the subject" instead of naming what's printed on them.
- Choose environments and framing where background text sits far from camera or
  at a steep angle to it; distance and oblique angle both reduce how much detail
  the model has to resolve even before defocus is applied.
- If a background sign or screen must stay recognizable as a location cue (a
  coffee shop's menu board, a gym's wall branding), keep it small in frame and
  treat it with the same locked-asset caution as a foreground logo, or accept it
  will need a post-production fix.
- Avoid moving the camera toward background text over the course of a shot; a
  push-in that brings a sign into focus forces the model to resolve it right when
  it has the least frame-to-frame room to get it wrong.

Why: this is exactly the compositional discipline a working DP already applies
to keep an audience's eye on the subject, and it has the side benefit of removing
the input that most reliably breaks in generated video, incidental legible text
the model was never actually asked to get right.

Example: "shallow depth of field, subject sharp in the foreground, softly blurred
shelving and signage in the background."
Counter-example: "deep focus wide shot with a fully legible menu board and price
signs sharp in the background" — puts unrequested, unmanaged text fully in resolution.
