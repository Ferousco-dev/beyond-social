Music is half the emotional payload of a video and often decides retention on
sound-on platforms. Choose it as deliberately as the visuals.

- Match tempo to pacing: cut on the beat; the edit and the track should share a
  pulse. Fast track wants faster cuts, and vice versa.
- Match genre to brand: the same footage feels luxury with a slow piano, hype with
  trap, wholesome with acoustic. The music, not the footage alone, tells the viewer
  how to feel.
- Use dynamics: a drop, a swell, or a silence lands the hero moment. Build to it.
- Platform reality: on TikTok and Reels, trending audio can carry reach, but it
  must still fit the mood; a mismatch reads as lazy.
- Leave room: do not fight a voiceover with a loud track; duck the music under
  speech.

Why: the wrong music can sink strong footage, and the right music can rescue
average footage. It is the cheapest, highest-leverage emotional lever available.

Example: "slow, warm piano building to a single swell as the product is revealed."
Counter-example: generic upbeat stock music applied regardless of the piece's tone.
