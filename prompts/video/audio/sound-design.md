Sound design is the layer viewers do not consciously notice but always feel. It
makes footage tangible and real.

- Diegetic sound: the sounds of the scene (a coffee pour, a keyboard, footsteps,
  fabric). Even subtle SFX make a shot feel physically present and less "AI."
- Accents: a whoosh on a fast move, a click or thud on a cut, a satisfying pop on a
  reveal. These punctuate edits and boost perceived quality.
- Voiceover: keep it conversational and specific; write for the ear, short
  sentences, one idea each. Sync key words to the matching visual.
- Mix hierarchy: voice on top, music under, SFX for texture. Duck music beneath
  speech so words stay intelligible.
- Silence: a deliberate beat of quiet before a drop or reveal is powerful.

Why: audiences equate rich, well-mixed sound with production value; thin or absent
sound design flattens even beautiful visuals.

Example: "soft ambient cafe noise, a crisp cup-on-saucer clink on the final shot,
warm music low underneath." Counter-example: loud music over everything, no scene
sound, voiceover buried beneath the track.
