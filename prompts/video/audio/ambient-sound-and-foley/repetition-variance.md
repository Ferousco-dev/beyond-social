No two occurrences of the same real-world sound are acoustically
identical, so any repeated action, footsteps, typing, chewing, tapping,
needs deliberate variance rather than a looped hit.

- Vary volume, pitch, and timing slightly between repeats of the same
  sound.
- Alternate the contact detail slightly, heel-first versus flatter step,
  harder versus softer tap.
- Avoid perfectly even spacing between repeated hits; natural rhythm
  drifts.
- Call out "no obvious loop" explicitly for extended repeated actions like
  walking, typing, or running.
- Let secondary detail vary too, not just the main hit: a footstep's
  trailing shoe-scuff or a keystroke's faint case-click should not repeat
  in lockstep with the primary sound.

Why: a looped or repeated audio sample is one of the easiest artifacts for
the ear to catch, because the auditory system is tuned to detect
periodicity as a sign of synthesis. The same exact sound recurring at the
same exact interval is a pattern that does not occur in nature, where
every foot strike, keystroke, or tap involves a slightly different angle,
force, and surface contact.

Example: "typing on the keyboard, each keystroke slightly different in
pitch and force, no metronomic repetition."

Counter-example: an eight-second walking cycle with an identical footstep
sample repeated on a fixed beat, which the ear flags as a loop within two
or three repetitions.
