Sound events placed outside the visible frame make the world feel larger
than what the camera shows, something picture alone cannot do on its own.

- Place at least one sound source explicitly off-frame per scene: a door
  closing down the hall, a dog barking next door, traffic beyond a window.
- Keep off-frame sounds spatially plausible relative to the framing,
  behind camera versus beyond a visible wall or window.
- Do not resolve every sound to something visible in shot; leave some
  unexplained but plausible.
- Vary the direction and distance of off-frame events across a scene
  rather than repeating the same one.

Why: real environments extend beyond any single frame. A scene where every
audible sound source is visible on screen unconsciously reads as a sealed
set rather than a slice of a continuous world; off-frame sound is the
cheapest way to imply the space keeps going past the edges of the shot.

Example: "kitchen scene, close on hands chopping vegetables, a door closes
somewhere else in the house, footsteps briefly audible upstairs."

Counter-example: a scene where literally every sound source is on camera,
which flattens the space into a diorama with edges the camera happens to
frame perfectly.
