Ambient sound and foley should stay strictly diegetic, sourced from
something visible or implied in the scene's world, unless a score is
explicitly and separately called for, because mixing the two categories
muddies the realism cue.

- Keep every specified ambient or foley element traceable to a plausible
  in-world source.
- Do not default to adding music or tonal pads "to fill space" where the
  brief calls for a realistic, unscored moment.
- If mood needs lifting without breaking realism, reach for a diegetic
  source, a radio playing in another room, someone humming, rather than a
  score.
- Treat score as a separate, deliberate layer requested on top of a
  complete diegetic bed, not a substitute for one.

Why: non-diegetic music dropped under an otherwise realistic shot signals
an edited, produced object, while a fully diegetic soundscape signals a
captured moment. Conflating them, a swelling orchestral pad under
handheld UGC-style footage, is one of the fastest ways to undercut an
otherwise convincing authentic-look video.

Example: "handheld kitchen shot, ambient only: fridge hum, faint radio
playing from another room, no score."

Counter-example: an ambient product shot with an unexplained cinematic
music bed rising underneath it, which turns a documentary-feeling capture
into an obvious ad the instant the score enters.
