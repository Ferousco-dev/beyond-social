Footstep sound must match the visible walking surface and footwear, or the
mismatch breaks physical trust even when everything else in the shot looks
right.

- Hardwood: sharp, resonant tap. Carpet: dull, muffled contact. Gravel:
  crunch with visible give underneath. Concrete: flat, hard slap. Grass:
  soft step with a rustle undertone.
- Match footwear to the surface: heels click on hardwood or concrete,
  sneakers thud, bare feet slap softly.
- Track the sound's weight and pace to the actor's visible gait speed and
  apparent body weight, not a fixed cadence.
- Include the surface's incidental reaction: loose gravel settling after
  weight passes, a floorboard creak.

Why: footstep sound is one of the most over-learned auditory patterns from
a lifetime of walking on different ground. A slight mismatch, a rubber-sole
thud on what is visibly bare concrete, triggers subconscious doubt before a
viewer can articulate why the shot feels wrong.

Example: "she crosses the gravel driveway, each step a crunch with loose
stone settling after, boots scuffing slightly on the uneven ground."

Counter-example: generic "footstep sounds" specified with no surface
named, which models render as a flat carpeted-hallway thud regardless of
whether the ground on screen is stone, wood, or grass.
