The volume, clarity, and reverb of a sound must match how far the source
sits from the implied camera or mic position, mirroring the lens choice on
screen.

- Close-up: tight, present, low-reverb sound; dialogue or foley feels
  close-mic'd.
- Wide or establishing shot: sound pulls back, gains more room reflection,
  loses high-frequency detail.
- A source moving toward or away from camera should shift proportionally
  in volume and clarity as it moves.
- Match reverb amount to the visible size of the space: a small bathroom
  gets a short slap-back, a gymnasium gets a long decay.

Why: sound perspective is the acoustic analog of depth of field.
Audiences have internalized that close things sound close and far things
sound distant and diffuse, so a wide shot with tight, dry, close-mic'd
audio, or a close-up with distant, echoey audio, creates a spatial
contradiction between what the eye and ear each report.

Example: "wide shot of the two of them across the courtyard, their voices
distant with visible room echo off the stone walls."

Counter-example: a wide exterior shot scored with tight, dry, studio-close
dialogue, which sounds like a voiceover pasted over the picture rather
than sound recorded in that space.
