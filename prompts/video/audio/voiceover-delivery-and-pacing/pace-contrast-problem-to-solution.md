A problem/solution script structure needs a corresponding pace shift: slower
and lower-energy through the problem, then an audible acceleration and pitch
lift at the exact moment the solution is introduced.

The recipe:

- Deliver the problem statement 10-15% below the script's average pace, with
  more space between words, so it sits with the discomfort rather than rushing
  past it.
- Mark the sentence that introduces the product or solution as the pace
  inflection point — this is where rate and energy should audibly step up, not
  gradually drift up.
- Use a short pause (200-300ms) directly before the solution sentence to set up
  the contrast, rather than blending straight from problem into solution.
- Keep the acceleration in the voice, not just in the edit — a fast cut into the
  solution shot with a voice still delivering at problem-pace reads as
  mismatched.

Why: pace change is one of the clearest non-verbal signals a listener uses to
track emotional shift in a narrative; slowing down for a problem and speeding
up for the fix mirrors how people actually talk about frustration turning into
relief, and that acoustic shape does more to sell the "before/after" than the
words alone.

Example: slow, flat "I used to spend hours reformatting these decks." [pause]
then faster, brighter: "Now I just drop them in and it's done."
Counter-example: both sentences delivered at the same even pace and energy —
the structural contrast written into the script never reaches the ear.
