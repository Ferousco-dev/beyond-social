When a video has kinetic on-screen text or word-by-word captions, the visual pop
of each word should be triggered by that word's actual vocal stress, not by a
uniform per-word timer.

The recipe:

- Time each caption word or emphasis pop to the onset of that syllable in the
  audio waveform, not to an evenly divided time grid across the sentence.
- Give only the stressed word in each breath group a visual treatment (scale
  bump, color change, bold) — matching the single-stress-per-phrase rule of the
  spoken read, so type and voice agree on what matters.
- Let unstressed function words (a, the, to, of) appear at normal size and pass
  by quickly, exactly as they're spoken quickly.
- If the read has a rushed run of words, let the captions for that run appear
  together in one beat rather than popping individually at a fixed interval.

Why: viewers process word-level captions fastest when the visual rhythm mirrors
the audio rhythm they're simultaneously hearing; a caption engine that pops every
word on a flat interval fights the natural stress pattern of the voice and makes
the piece feel like two separate, uncoordinated systems rather than one.

Example: captions for "it's not FASTER, it's CHEAPER" pop-scale only on
"faster" and "cheaper," synced to the exact vocal stress onset.
Counter-example: every word, including "it's" and "not," pops at an identical
size on a fixed 200ms interval regardless of how the line is actually spoken.
