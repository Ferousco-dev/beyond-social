Declarative claims about a product need declarative pitch contours; a rising
inflection on a statement of fact undercuts the claim even when the words
themselves are confident.

The recipe:

- Audit every factual or benefit-bearing sentence for a rising pitch on its
  final word; if present, flag it for a re-take or a different TTS reference
  clip with a flatter or falling terminal contour.
- Keep hedge words ("kind of," "I think," "honestly") out of claim sentences
  entirely at the script stage — removing the word is more reliable than trying
  to deliver it confidently.
- Reserve rising inflection for the actual questions in the script (rhetorical
  hooks like "know that feeling?") so the contrast between question and
  statement stays legible.
- When choosing between TTS reference voices or takes, prefer the one with more
  pitch range on stressed syllables and a cleaner fall on statement endings over
  one that sounds generically "warm" but flat.

Why: rising terminal pitch is the same acoustic signature used for questions and
tentative, checking-in speech; applying it to a factual claim ("this saves you
two hours a week?") makes the claim sound like the speaker is seeking
validation rather than stating a fact, which erodes perceived authority even
though the transcript reads identically either way.

Example: "This cuts your editing time in half." delivered flat-to-falling.
Counter-example: the identical line delivered with a rise on "half" — the
words claim confidence, the pitch contour asks for permission.
