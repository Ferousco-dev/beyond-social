A quiet, natural breath at each breath-group boundary should usually stay in
the mix rather than be gated or edited out, because a voice with zero breath
noise is one of the quieter tells of synthetic or over-processed audio.

The recipe:

- Keep breaths that fall at genuine phrase boundaries; remove only the ones
  that land mid-thought or are unusually loud or long relative to the
  surrounding read.
- Duck breath volume by roughly 6-10dB relative to the surrounding speech
  rather than silencing it completely — audible but not competing with the
  words.
- On UGC-style or talking-avatar deliveries specifically, err toward leaving
  more breath texture in, since the format's whole credibility depends on
  sounding like an unscripted, single-take capture.
- On tightly produced ad-creative VO, thin breaths out more aggressively but
  don't remove all of them — total silence between every phrase is its own
  tell.

Why: noise gates and de-breath plugins are tuned for broadcast VO where the
goal is a pristine, breath-free bed; but a video optimized to look and sound
authentically human benefits from the opposite bias, because completely
breathless speech is not something an unedited human voice actually produces,
and viewers register that absence as processed even without naming why.

Example: a quiet inhale sits under a caption cut between two sentences, ducked
but present.
Counter-example: every breath fully gated to silence across the whole VO track
— clean, but it starts to sound like a voice with no body behind it.
