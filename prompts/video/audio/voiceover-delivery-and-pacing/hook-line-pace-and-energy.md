The first line of voiceover needs a faster syllable rate and a rising pitch contour
than the rest of the script, because it is competing with a scrolling thumb, not
with an attentive listener.

The recipe:

- Push the hook to roughly 4.5-5.5 syllables per second versus 3.5-4 for the body;
  the differential itself signals "something is starting."
- Start the pitch slightly above the speaker's natural resting pitch and let it
  rise further on the operative word, not the first word.
- Cut the pre-roll: the first syllable should land within 0.3-0.5 seconds of frame
  one, no throat-clear runway.
- Drop the pace back down for the second sentence so the hook reads as a spike,
  not the new normal.

Why: a flat, evenly-paced read has no attention peak for the algorithm's early
retention window to grab onto. Human speech naturally speeds up under excitement
and interest; matching that physiological tell in the first 1.5 seconds reads as
"this person has something to tell you" rather than "this is an ad script being
read aloud."

Example: "Okay— nobody's talking about this," delivered fast and slightly high,
then a normal-paced follow-up: "But it changed how I do my mornings."
Counter-example: an evenly metered "Hi everyone, today I want to show you
something," at the same pace as the rest of the script — it gives the scroll
nothing to stop on.
