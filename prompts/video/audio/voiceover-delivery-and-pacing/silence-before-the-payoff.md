A dramatic pause placed right before the payoff word or the reveal shot creates
more anticipation than any vocal inflection can, because silence forces the
viewer to fill the gap themselves.

The recipe:

- Hold for 0.4-0.8 seconds of true silence (no VO, no music swell) directly
  before the punchline, price reveal, or product reveal — long enough to
  register as a choice, short enough not to feel like dead air.
- Cut any underscore or ambient bed during that pause too, not just the voice;
  a pause that still has a full music bed under it doesn't read as a pause.
- Land the pause right before the payoff word, never after it — the suspense is
  in the gap before the information, not in the silence following it.
- Use this device once per script; a second dramatic pause in the same piece
  reads as a tic rather than a beat.

Why: anticipation is generated by withheld information, and a full sensory drop
(voice and music both cutting out) signals to the viewer's attention "something
is about to happen" more reliably than a vocal trick can, because it removes
every other stimulus competing for their focus in that instant.

Example: "...and the price for all of that is—" [0.5s full silence, no music]
"nine dollars."
Counter-example: the same line with music continuing to swell through the
pause — the anticipation gets pre-empted by the score doing the work instead of
the silence.
