When a line ends right at a cut, the silence should fall a few frames before the
cut lands, not after it — the cut itself should feel like it happens inside the
pause, not before it.

The recipe:

- End the spoken phrase 3-6 frames ahead of the visual cut point, leaving a short
  pocket of silence that the cut then punctuates.
- Never let a word's final consonant get chopped by the edit; give it its full
  decay before the next shot starts.
- For a hard, percussive cut (impact edit), shorten that pocket to 1-2 frames so
  the cut reads as landing on the silence itself, almost like a beat hit.
- For a soft dissolve or match cut, widen the pocket slightly so the audio and
  picture both breathe through the transition.

Why: a cut that lands mid-word or immediately on the last consonant reads as a
splice, because natural speech doesn't get interrupted by scene changes — the
editor is imposing a beat the speaker didn't take. Letting the voice finish and
leaving a sliver of silence before the cut makes the edit feel like it was built
around the performance rather than stamped on top of it.

Example: VO "...and that's the whole trick." [3 frames silence] CUT to product
shot.
Counter-example: the cut lands exactly on "trick," truncating the k sound —
technically synced, but it reads as an audio splice rather than a considered edit.
