A voiceover script should be broken into breath groups, the chunks a real speaker
can say on one lungful, not into grammatical sentences that may run long.

The recipe:

- Cap a breath group at roughly 8-14 words or 3-4 seconds of speech; a comma does
  not guarantee a safe breath point, only a genuine phrase boundary does.
- Write the line break where the breath actually goes, and prompt the model or
  voice actor to pause there, not just where punctuation happens to fall.
- Give longer or emotionally weighted claims their own breath group so they are
  not rushed to fit inside one exhale.
- Read the script aloud yourself before locking it; if you run out of air, the
  group is too long regardless of word count.

Why: sentences written for the page and sentences written for the lungs are
different objects. A 22-word sentence with two subordinate clauses is fine typed
out, but forces a speaker to either gasp mid-phrase or race through the second
half, and a rushed second half is where clarity and emphasis both collapse.
Breath-group scripting keeps every clause audible and evenly weighted.

Example: "This app doesn't just track your spending. // It tells you what to
do about it." — two breath groups, each landing clean.
Counter-example: "This app doesn't just track your spending, it actually tells
you exactly what to do about it every single time you open it" as one unbroken
line — the back half gets swallowed chasing the front half's air.
