Punctuation marks map to specific, non-interchangeable pause durations in a
spoken read, and scripts should be punctuated for the ear's timing, not for
grammatical correctness on the page.

The recipe:

- Comma: roughly 150-250ms, a breath-catch, not a stop; pitch stays mid-level
  going into it.
- Period / full stop: roughly 400-600ms, with a falling pitch contour completed
  before the silence starts.
- Em dash (interruption or pivot): a hard, near-instant stop with no trailing
  decay, then a new pitch start on the next clause.
- Ellipsis (trailing thought): let the final word decay in volume rather than
  cutting cleanly, then pause 500ms+ — this is the only mark where the pause is
  preceded by fading, not stopping.
- If a script needs a pause length punctuation can't express, write it
  explicitly as a bracketed instruction rather than stacking punctuation marks.

Why: punctuation in a script is a timing instruction for the reader, not a
grammar rule for a page a viewer will never see; treating a comma and a period
as interchangeable "short pause" markers flattens the rhythm and removes the
cues that tell a listener whether a thought is continuing or complete.

Example: "It's simple, it's fast — and it's already on your phone." uses a
short comma pause, then a hard dash pivot before the final clause.
Counter-example: over-punctuating with "It's simple... it's fast... and it's
already on your phone..." forces a fade-pause after every clause, making a
confident line sound hesitant.
