The overall speaking rate of a voiceover should track the average shot length of
the sequence it plays under: fast-cut montages want a tighter, denser read, and
slow, held shots want a more spacious one.

The recipe:

- For average shot lengths under 1 second (whip-cut montage), keep the VO near
  4.5+ syllables per second with almost no mid-sentence pausing, so the ear has
  something continuous to hold onto while the eye is overloaded.
- For average shot lengths over 3 seconds (contemplative or testimonial pacing),
  drop to 3-3.5 syllables per second and let pauses run longer than they would
  in conversation.
- When the cut rate changes mid-video (montage into a held product shot), change
  the VO rate a beat before the cut rate changes, so the ear anticipates the
  shift rather than trailing it.
- Never let VO pace and cut pace fight each other — a slow, ponderous read over
  a rapid-fire montage makes the montage look frantic rather than energetic.

Why: voiceover and edit rhythm are two channels the brain is trying to fuse into
one experience; when they run at mismatched tempos the viewer has to consciously
reconcile them, which reads as effortful and cheap rather than as a considered
directorial choice. Matched tempo is what makes a cut sequence feel scored.

Example: a 5-shot-per-second product montage paired with a clipped, rapid VO
line list, then a held final shot paired with one slow, spacious closing line.
Counter-example: a slow, deliberate voiceover narrating over a fast TikTok-style
jump-cut montage — the two rhythms cancel each other out.
