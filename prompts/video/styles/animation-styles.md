Non-photoreal styles are strong differentiators and often more forgiving of
generation artifacts than realism. Name the medium precisely and add its signature
cues.

- Anime: cel-shaded, bold linework, expressive eyes, dramatic speed lines and
  lighting; specify a sub-style (90s cel vs modern) for consistency.
- 3D animation: "Pixar-style" soft rounded characters, global illumination, subtle
  subsurface skin; clean and family-friendly.
- Claymation / stop-motion: visible fingerprints, slight jitter, tactile handmade
  materials; charming and tactile.
- 2D motion graphics: flat vector shapes, bold color, kinetic type; ideal for
  explainers and data.
- Mixed / hybrid: a real product composited into a stylized world.

Match style to message and audience: playful brands and explainers gain warmth from
animation; luxury usually wants photoreal restraint.

Why: a committed, named style yields coherent output and a memorable brand look,
and stylization hides the small realism glitches that break live-action generation.

Example: "modern anime, cel-shaded, bold outlines, dramatic rim light, speed lines
on the action." Counter-example: "cartoon style" with no medium, era, or cues,
yielding an inconsistent mush.
