User-generated-content style deliberately looks unpolished, because on social,
"real" outperforms "produced." It is the highest-converting ad style on TikTok and
Reels precisely because it does not look like an ad.

The recipe:

- Phone-camera look: handheld with slight natural shake, everyday framing, not
  perfectly composed.
- Natural, available light: window light, a normal room, a little imperfection.
- A real person talking to camera, casual and specific, not a scripted spokesmodel.
- Minimal grade: true-to-life color, not a glossy commercial finish.
- Native captions and trend-aware audio; feels made on the phone, for the phone.

The paradox: it takes intention to look unintentional. Keep it grounded but still
legible, well-lit enough to see, audible enough to hear.

Why: audiences trust peers over brands; the authentic look lowers ad-resistance and
lifts conversion, which is why brands emulate it.

Example: "handheld phone-camera look, real person in a kitchen with window light,
casual, slightly imperfect framing, natural color." Counter-example: a glossy,
tripod-locked, color-graded studio spot pretending to be UGC.
