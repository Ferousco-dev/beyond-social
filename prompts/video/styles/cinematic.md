"Cinematic" is not one word to add; it is a stack of specific choices. Invoke it by
naming the ingredients, not the label.

The cinematic recipe:

- Shallow depth of field: subject sharp, background soft. The strongest single cue.
- Motivated, slow camera movement or a confident locked-off frame.
- Directional, contrasty lighting with a rim to separate the subject.
- A deliberate color grade (warm filmic, or teal-and-orange), lifted blacks.
- Wider aspect feel and considered composition (thirds, depth layers).
- Slower pacing that lets moments breathe; slow motion on hero beats.
- Fine texture: gentle film grain, natural imperfections, atmospheric haze.

Combine three or four of these and footage reads as cinematic even without the
word. Overuse the label alone and the model returns a generic teal wash.

Why: the look is emergent from concrete techniques; specifying them gives the model
executable direction and avoids the hollow default "cinematic" filter.

Example: "shallow depth of field, slow dolly-in, warm rim light, filmic grade with
lifted blacks, subtle grain, slow motion." Counter-example: "cinematic, epic,
movie-like, 4k."
