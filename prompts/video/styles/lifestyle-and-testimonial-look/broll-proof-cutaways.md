Testimonial b-roll should show the literal claim being made, in the same
environment as the interview, cut in as proof rather than as generic mood
footage.

The recipe:

- Match the cutaway's location and light to the interview setup so it reads
  as the same session, the same day.
- Cut to the product mid-use exactly on, or just after, the sentence that
  makes the claim: "it's so quick" cuts to the actual quick action.
- Favor point-of-view or over-the-shoulder framing for these cutaways over
  posed product shots; it should look caught, not composed.
- Keep cutaways short, one to three seconds, and specific to one action, not
  a scenic montage of the space.
- Return to the interview face before the next claim, rather than stacking
  several cutaways in a row.

Why: in testimonial advertising, b-roll's job is evidentiary — it
corroborates a spoken claim in real time — unlike lifestyle-brand b-roll,
which is often mood-based and can wander. That evidentiary function is what
makes timing and specificity matter more here than composition or beauty.

Example: cut on "it saved me twenty minutes" to a handheld point-of-view shot
of hands finishing the task in the same kitchen, then back to her face for
the next line.

Counter-example: generic slow-motion lifestyle footage of someone laughing,
with the product nowhere in frame — it looks pleasant but proves nothing
about the claim being made.
