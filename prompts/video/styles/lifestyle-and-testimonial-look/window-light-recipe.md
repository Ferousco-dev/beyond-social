Testimonial lifestyle scenes should use window light as the key, shaped with a
single bounce or diffusion, because that is what real interview crews
actually rig in homes and offices, and it reads as unstaged even though it is
controlled.

The recipe:

- Position the subject at roughly 45 degrees to the window, not backlit flat
  against it and not facing it head-on.
- Place a white bounce card or foam board camera-side to fill the shadow side
  so the ratio stays soft, not a hard split.
- If the light is direct sun through glass, call for diffusion explicitly
  ("sheer curtain diffusion") rather than letting it read as harsh spot light.
- Let the window itself run slightly overexposed in the background rather than
  gray and featureless — that overexposed blow-out is how real testimonial DPs
  actually expose these rooms.
- Keep white balance true to the location's daylight or overcast tone; do not
  warm it toward a studio-lamp color.

Why: window light is the default in real production because glass and
curtains already act as a giant diffuser, so it flatters skin without a
diffusion frame, and it is the light every viewer unconsciously expects a
home or office interview to be lit by, at any time of day.

Example: "seated at a kitchen table, 45 degrees to a large window, soft
daylight key, white bounce camera-side fill, window slightly overexposed in
the background."

Counter-example: "even, shadowless studio lighting from all sides" — this
flattens the face into the sterile look no real testimonial ever has, and the
absence of any window or shadow direction removes the location cue entirely.
