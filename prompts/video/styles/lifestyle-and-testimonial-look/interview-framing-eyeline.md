There are two distinct testimonial formats defined by eyeline, and mixing them
without intent reads as amateur: the interview format, where the subject
looks slightly off-axis at an unseen interviewer, and direct address, where
the subject looks straight into the lens.

The recipe:

- Interview format: place the imaginary interviewer 5-15 degrees off the lens
  axis, keep the eyeline consistent to that point, camera at or just below eye
  level.
- Direct address: subject looks straight into the lens, camera at eye level;
  reserve this for punchier, confessional-style lines.
- Keep headroom modest and leave looking room on the side the eyeline points
  toward, rather than centering the subject dead-on.
- Default to a medium, chest-up shot; extreme close-ups read as a beauty ad,
  not testimony.
- Stay consistent for a given subject across the whole edit — cutting between
  eyelines mid-testimonial breaks the illusion of one continuous conversation.

Why: eyeline is one of the strongest subconscious authenticity cues on
camera. Off-axis reads as "answering a real question"; direct-to-lens reads
as "confiding in you." Either works on its own, but an inconsistent or
dead-centered eyeline reads as posed, because neither a real interview nor a
real confession looks like that.

Example: "medium shot, eyes 10 degrees off-lens toward an unseen interviewer,
camera at eye level, looking room to the left."

Counter-example: a symmetrical, centered frame where the subject's gaze
drifts between the lens and off-camera — this reads uncertain and staged
rather than either format.
