Camera movement in this style should read as a person holding a stabilized
rig, not a tripod and not a phone in a shaking hand: a small, slow, organic
drift.

The recipe:

- Describe a gentle handheld sway or breathing drift, small in amplitude, with
  no jitter or shake artifacts.
- Avoid "locked-off tripod" language, which reads too polished and corporate
  for this style.
- Avoid "shaky handheld" or "phone shake" language, which pushes the shot
  toward UGC instead.
- If the camera follows the subject to another spot, keep it slow and
  single-purpose: one movement per shot, not a wander.
- Let drift settle back toward center rather than continuously roam; a small
  correction reads as human, endless wander reads as seasick.

Why: a gimbal-smoothed handheld (Ronin, Movi, and similar rigs) is the actual
tool real lifestyle and testimonial crews use, because it keeps the
intimacy and presence of handheld camera while removing the amateur shake
that would undercut trust in the production. It is the movement grammar that
sits precisely between UGC and cinematic.

Example: "subtle handheld drift, gimbal-smoothed, slow settle back toward
center, no jitter."

Counter-example: "shaky phone-in-hand camera" — this collapses the shot into
UGC territory and undercuts the produced-but-real balance the style depends
on.
