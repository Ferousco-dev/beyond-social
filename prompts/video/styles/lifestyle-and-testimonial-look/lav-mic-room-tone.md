Testimonial audio should sound like a lavalier mic in a real room, not a
studio boom and not a phone's built-in mic: close vocal presence, a light
room tone underneath, and the occasional handling noise, never
noise-gated silence.

The recipe:

- Keep a low bed of room tone under speech, a fridge hum, faint traffic, HVAC
  air, rather than dead silence between sentences.
- Avoid "studio-clean" or "broadcast" audio language, which strips the
  location of its sonic identity.
- Allow a very occasional soft handling or fabric-brush sound, consistent
  with a lav clipped to a shirt, rather than pristine isolation.
- Keep the voice close and intimate in level even when the shot is a medium
  frame; testimonial audio perspective does not have to match visual
  distance.
- Avoid heavy de-essing or compression artifacts; leave natural sibilance
  and audible breath.

Why: an over-cleaned track is one of the fastest ways a viewer's ear, even
without naming it, flags a testimonial as fabricated, because real rooms are
never silent and a real lav clipped to a shirt never sounds like a podcast
booth.

Example: "close, warm voice presence, faint room tone (refrigerator hum,
distant street), natural breath audible between sentences."

Counter-example: "pristine, silent-background, broadcast-quality voiceover" —
sonically this stops sounding like a room a person is actually sitting in.
