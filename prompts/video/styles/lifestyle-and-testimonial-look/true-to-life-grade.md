The color grade for this style should be a light, consistent correction, not
a stylized cinematic LUT and not an uncorrected phone default. The goal is
"this looks like the room actually looked," slightly improved.

The recipe:

- Neutral-to-slightly-warm white balance matched to the scene's actual
  practical source (window daylight or a warm lamp), not a stylized
  split-tone.
- A gentle contrast and shadow lift for legibility, not a crushed-blacks
  filmic curve.
- Keep skin tones natural and consistent shot to shot, prioritized over any
  mood grading.
- Avoid heavy vignettes, teal-orange splits, or grain overlays; these signal
  "cinematic ad," which is the wrong register here.
- Keep saturation close to natural; a small lift is enough, not a stylized
  pop.

Why: the grade's job in this style is to be invisible. Any grade a viewer can
name by genre, "that's a cinematic teal-orange look," pulls the footage
toward "produced" and away from "testified." The actual craft is making the
correction disappear while still looking cleaner than an unfixed raw file, a
much harder discipline than applying a strong look.

Example: "true-to-life color, neutral white balance matched to window
daylight, gentle contrast, natural skin tones."

Counter-example: "warm filmic grade, teal shadows, lifted blacks, film
grain" — a cinematic-ad signature that reads as produced, not testified.
