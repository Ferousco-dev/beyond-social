Testimonial lifestyle shots want a medium depth of field: enough separation
to flatter the subject, but not so shallow that the environment — the visual
proof of "a real place" — dissolves into mush.

The recipe:

- Specify a normal-to-short-telephoto equivalent (roughly 35-50mm full-frame)
  rather than a wide lens, which distorts faces at close range.
- Describe a moderate aperture feel (around f/2.8-f/4): background softened
  but still recognizable, not razor-thin bokeh.
- Keep enough of the room legible behind the subject that a viewer can
  identify the location type — kitchen, office, car — at a glance.
- Avoid extreme background blur that could belong to any studio; it erases
  the environmental cue the format is built on.
- For product-in-hand moments, let focus rack naturally from face to product
  and back, rather than holding both perfectly sharp at once.

Why: testimonial credibility depends on a legible, verifiable context. A
cinematic razor-thin depth of field disconnects the subject from a real
place; a phone lens's everything-in-focus look can read flat and un-crafted.
The middle ground does both jobs — subject separation and location proof — at
once.

Example: "50mm equivalent, medium depth of field, subject sharp, kitchen
counter and window behind softly recognizable."

Counter-example: "85mm portrait lens, wide open, background a total blur" —
flattering, but it erases the real-place cue the entire format depends on to
be believed.
