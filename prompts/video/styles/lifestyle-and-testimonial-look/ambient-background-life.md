Let ordinary background activity continue in frame during a testimonial,
someone passing through, a kettle boiling, traffic outside, rather than
clearing the scene for the subject. An empty, controlled background is a
tell.

The recipe:

- Include one low-key background event during the take: a partner walking
  past in the kitchen, a dog crossing the frame, steam rising from a kettle.
- Keep it subordinate to the subject: soft focus, off to the side, never
  competing for attention or timed to land like a joke.
- Let ambient sound from that activity bleed into the audio bed at a low
  level, alongside the room tone, rather than isolating the subject's voice
  completely.
- Avoid staging the background event so it looks choreographed; a person
  walking through on a fixed count reads as blocked, not incidental.
- Use this once per scene, not continuously, or it becomes a distraction
  from the subject.

Why: real domestic and workplace spaces are never perfectly still. An
interview shot where absolutely nothing else happens in the background reads
as a set cleared and locked down for filming, which is exactly the
artificiality this style exists to avoid.

Example: "in the background, slightly out of focus, someone crosses the
kitchen with a laundry basket, unbothered by the camera."

Counter-example: a perfectly still, empty background with no incidental
activity for the whole take — this reads like a set cleared and locked down
for the shoot, not a room someone actually lives in.
