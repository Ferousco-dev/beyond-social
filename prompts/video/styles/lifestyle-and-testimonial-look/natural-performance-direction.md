Direct the performance for cadence, not perfect delivery: real testimonial
subjects think while they talk, so the speech pattern itself needs texture,
not just the words.

The recipe:

- Build in a beat of thought before the key sentence: "a short pause, then
  she says..."
- Vary sentence length; real speech is not evenly metered like ad copy.
- Let a hand gesture land slightly before or after the word it illustrates,
  not in tight lockstep with it.
- Describe the moment as "answering a question," not "delivering a line" —
  the framing changes the implied rhythm.
- Keep the emotional register grounded: mild enthusiasm, relief, quiet
  confidence, rather than presenter-energy peaks.

Why: the clearest tell of a scripted read is uniform rhythm and gestures
pre-timed to words, because real recollection has variable pacing and
imperfect sync between thought and gesture. Prompting explicitly for that
variability is what keeps a generated performance from collapsing into
"spokesperson voice," which is the fastest way a testimonial stops being
believed.

Example: "she pauses, looks down for a second like she's remembering, then
says the line with quiet certainty."

Counter-example: "delivers the tagline confidently and clearly to camera" —
this reads like a scripted ad read, not a testimonial, regardless of how
natural the words themselves are.
