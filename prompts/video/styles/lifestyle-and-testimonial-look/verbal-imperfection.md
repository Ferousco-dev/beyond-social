A single audible imperfection, a false start, a self-correction, a small
stumble, is a deliberate authenticity cue for testimonial dialogue, not a
flaw to smooth away, but it must be sparse and never comedic.

The recipe:

- Prompt for one natural disfluency per statement, a restart ("I- I mean") or
  a filler ("honestly"), placed near the start of a thought, not the
  punchline.
- Never place a stumble on the key claim or benefit sentence; clarity matters
  more than texture exactly there.
- Avoid stacking multiple disfluencies in one breath, which reads as a
  nervous or unreliable speaker rather than a natural one.
- Let a small laugh or exhale land between sentences occasionally, as a
  reaction rather than something scripted into the line.
- Keep overall pacing natural rather than uniformly clipped and even.

Why: perfectly clean delivery is itself an artifact of scripted advertising.
Real recollection includes a small amount of self-correction, and audiences
use that unconsciously as a lie-detector for whether a statement was actually
recalled rather than recited, so its sparing presence measurably increases
believed authenticity.

Example: "'It's honestly— I mean, it's the easiest part of my week now,' she
says, with a small laugh after."

Counter-example: a flawless, evenly-paced paragraph with zero hesitation
anywhere — this reads rehearsed even when the words themselves are identical
to a natural take.
