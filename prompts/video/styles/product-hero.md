The product-hero look makes an object the star: crisp, controlled, and desirable.
It is the language of premium commercials and the default for a beauty shot.

The recipe:

- Clean, controlled studio or minimal set; uncluttered background that isolates the
  product.
- Macro and close detail shots that sell material, finish, and craft.
- Slow, precise camera moves: orbit, push-in, or top-down; or a locked frame with
  the product on a turntable.
- Sculpted lighting: a soft key plus a rim to trace edges; reflections and
  highlights that reveal form. Gradient or dark backgrounds for contrast.
- Deliberate motion around the product: rising steam, sifting powder, pouring
  liquid, drifting fabric, to add life without stealing focus.
- Shallow depth of field and a restrained grade that flatters the material.

Keep the product's shape, label, and color exact; identity is the whole point.

Why: these techniques concentrate attention and signal quality, turning a plain
object into something the viewer wants; a flat, evenly-lit product shot does not.

Example: "macro orbit around the watch on dark slate, soft key plus edge rim light,
shallow focus, a bead of water rolling off." Counter-example: the product centered
under flat overhead light on a cluttered desk.
