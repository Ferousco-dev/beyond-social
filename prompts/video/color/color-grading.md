Color grade is the finishing layer that unifies a look and sets emotion. Describe
it as a palette and a relationship between shadows and highlights, not just a
color name.

Recognizable looks:

- Teal and orange: warm skin against cool backgrounds; the modern blockbuster look.
  Punchy, commercial.
- Warm / golden: nostalgic, cozy, premium lifestyle.
- Cool / desaturated: serious, tech, editorial, or somber.
- High saturation, high contrast: energetic, youthful, social-native.
- Muted / filmic with lifted blacks: understated, "shot on film," tasteful.
- Monochrome or duotone: bold, graphic, brand-forward.

Tie the grade to the brand and platform: social-native content often wants
brighter, punchier grades that pop on a phone; luxury wants restraint and warmth.
Keep the grade consistent across every shot in a piece (see color continuity).

Why: an unnamed grade yields flat, default color that undoes good lighting. A named
palette gives the piece a coherent, branded signature.

Example: "Warm filmic grade, slightly lifted blacks, golden highlights, gentle
teal in the shadows." Counter-example: "colorful and vibrant."
