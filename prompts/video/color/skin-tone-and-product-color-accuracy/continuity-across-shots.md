A product's rendered color needs to survive not just within one shot but
across every cut of the same edit, since even a small hue or saturation shift
between the hero shot and a cutaway is one of the fastest ways an ad reveals
itself as generated rather than filmed in one continuous session.

- Track the product's reference color, hex or Pantone or source image, as a
  single source of truth used to prompt or grade every shot in the sequence,
  not re-derived per shot from a verbal description.
- When shots differ in lighting mood, bright opening versus a moodier close,
  let the surrounding light and shadow shift while explicitly holding the
  product's own hue constant across the sequence.
- Before final edit, pull a still frame of the product from every shot in the
  sequence and compare them side by side; treat any visible mismatch as a
  required re-grade, not a stylistic difference.
- Apply the same rule to skin tone for a recurring presenter or avatar across
  a multi-shot spot; the same face should not shift undertone between the
  opening line and the call to action.

Why: real footage of one product shot in one session naturally holds
consistent color because it's the same physical object under a continuous, if
varying, lighting setup, while independently generated shots have no shared
memory of the object's exact color unless that consistency is deliberately
enforced, so continuity has to be treated as a pipeline requirement, not an
assumed outcome.

Example: "product color held at the same reference hex across opening shot,
mid demo shot, and closing CTA shot, only ambient light mood changes."

Counter-example: three generated shots of the same bottle in one ad, cut
together, where the bottle is noticeably more orange in the middle shot than
in the other two, an inconsistency invisible in any single shot but obvious
the moment they're edited together.
