Golden hour is a monochromatic warm grade, not a complementary split like
teal-orange — it reproduces low-angle, heavily scattered sunlight and suits
skincare, food, travel, and aspirational lifestyle work. It is wrong wherever
accurate cool metallics matter, like tech and gadget shots.

The recipe:

- Raise color temperature roughly 300-500K equivalent across the frame.
- Lift midtone warmth without crushing shadow detail.
- Add a soft, ungraded highlight rolloff rather than a hard clip.
- Warm skin a half-stop past neutral, stopping well short of an orange cast.
- Lift the black point slightly for a hazy, atmospheric softness.

Why: golden hour light is physically low-angle and warm because it travels
through more atmosphere, which scatters blue wavelengths out before they reach
the lens. The grade is reproducing a specific, real lighting condition, and
that physical specificity is what keeps it from reading as a generic warm filter.

Example: "golden hour warmth, low warm key light, soft highlight rolloff, skin
a half-stop warmer than neutral."

Counter-example: cranking global warmth on a midday flat-lit product shot —
produces a flat orange cast with none of golden hour's directional falloff,
reading as an Instagram filter rather than lighting.
