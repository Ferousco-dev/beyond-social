Desaturated naturalism looks almost ungraded: true-to-life color, saturation
pulled just slightly below camera-native, contrast close to what the eye
actually perceived on set. It fits testimonials, wellness, and healthcare,
where "produced" reads as "hiding something."

The recipe:

- Pull saturation 5-15% below camera-native; more than that starts to look
  deliberately styled again.
- Preserve natural skin variance instead of evening it into a uniform tone.
- Match white balance to the real light source in the room rather than forcing neutral gray.
- Let shadows keep visible detail; do not crush blacks for punch.

Why: the eye and brain already do constant local color and contrast
adaptation, so unstylized footage that matches lived visual experience reads
as trustworthy precisely because it withholds the cues — heavy saturation,
crushed blacks, a color cast — that signal commercial persuasion.

Example: "kitchen interview shot, true-to-light color, slight desaturation,
visible shadow detail, no color cast."

Counter-example: running a punchy teal-orange preset over a customer
testimonial — the stylization contradicts the authentic-voice premise and
audiences read the subject as staged.
