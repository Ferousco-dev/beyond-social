Any aggressive named look — teal-orange, bleach bypass, neo-noir — risks
pushing skin into an unnatural hue. Protecting it is a required secondary
correction step for every stylized grade, not an optional polish pass.

The recipe:

- Isolate skin with the vectorscope's skin-tone reference line, or an HSL
  secondary keyed to roughly hue 20-40 degrees.
- Apply the stylized grade to the rest of the frame first.
- Dial back saturation and hue shift specifically on the skin key afterward.
- Watch for the tell-tale failures: a green or cyan cast on skin, an
  over-orange sunburn cast, or crushed shadow skin losing all texture.

Why: viewers have a lifetime of calibration for human skin and will detect a
few degrees of hue error on a face far faster than the identical error on a
wall or a sky. Skin needs its own correction pass even when — especially
when — the rest of the frame is heavily stylized.

Example: "teal-orange grade applied to background and wardrobe; skin isolated
and held near-neutral warm, protected from the teal shift."

Counter-example: applying one global LUT with no skin isolation on a
talking-head ad — the presenter's face picks up the same teal cast as the
background and looks ill rather than stylized.
