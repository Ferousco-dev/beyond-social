Pastel high-key is a soft, low-contrast, lifted-black look common in beauty,
wellness, baby, and Gen-Z lifestyle work — it removes visual weight rather
than adding it, which is the opposite direction from bleach bypass or neo-noir.

The recipe:

- Lift the black point so shadows sit at soft gray, never true black.
- Keep highlights gentle; avoid hard clipping on skin or backgrounds.
- Desaturate 15-25% globally, but hold the product's hero color true —
  don't desaturate the one thing the brand needs recognized.
- Keep the contrast curve shallow across the whole tonal range.

Why: high-key lighting with lifted blacks reduces an image's perceived weight
and drama, which matches the emotional register beauty and wellness brands
are chasing — light, approachable, non-threatening. It is a deliberate
trade-off against contrast and depth, not an absence of grading decisions.

Example: "pastel high-key grade, lifted soft shadows, gentle desaturation, the
packaging's mint color kept true."

Counter-example: applying pastel high-key to a tactical or automotive brief —
the lack of contrast reads as weak and washed out rather than premium,
undercutting a brief that wants weight and precision.
