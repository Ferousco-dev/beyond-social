Bleach bypass emulates a film lab process that skips the bleach step and
retains silver in the print, which boosts contrast, strips saturation, and
adds a glow around highlights. It fits gritty, tense, or streetwear briefs; it
is wrong for anything warm, soft, or reassuring.

The recipe:

- Desaturate globally 30-50%, but keep a single cast (steel-blue or sickly
  green) rather than going to pure grayscale.
- Push an S-curve for deep, crushed shadow contrast without full clipping.
- Add a soft bloom around practicals and specular highlights.
- Keep grain visible and slightly clumped, not fine — bleach bypass grain is coarse.

Why: the original lab technique was valued because it increased apparent
sharpness and grain while stripping color, giving footage a harsh, reportage
quality that reads as urgent and unpolished rather than commercial. That
"something is wrong" tension is the entire point — it is a mood tool, not a
neutral stylization.

Example: "tactical boots product spot, bleach-bypass grade, crushed shadow
detail, steel-blue cast, visible film grain."

Counter-example: bleach bypass on a soft skincare ad — the crushed contrast and
grain read as damage or aggression, directly undermining a gentle brief.
