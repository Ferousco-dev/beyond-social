Not every brief wants a look. E-commerce, app walkthroughs, and price-sensitive
DTC ads need color-accurate, neutral grading because the product's actual
color has to read correctly — a stylized cast that misrepresents the product
is a return-rate liability, not an aesthetic choice.

The recipe:

- White balance to the actual light source used on set.
- Keep saturation at or near camera-native.
- Use only a mild S-curve for screen presence; never crush blacks or blow highlights.
- Never let a stylized cast alter the hero product's hue.
- Reserve any stylization for background or environment elements only, never the product itself.

Why: for shoppable and utility-driven content, the grade is functionally part
of the product spec sheet. A shirt that reads a different blue on-screen than
in hand generates returns and complaints, so choosing no look is a deliberate,
correct craft decision, not an absence of craft.

Example: "color-accurate grade, product hue locked to reference swatch,
neutral white balance, mild contrast only."

Counter-example: applying a moody teal-orange grade to a product-detail shot
for an online store — the garment's true color becomes unrecoverable from the
video, misleading buyers.
