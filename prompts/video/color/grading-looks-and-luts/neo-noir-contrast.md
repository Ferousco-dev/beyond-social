Neo-noir grading is near-desaturated, high-contrast, with one motivated color
source held fully saturated against an otherwise gray frame — a single
neon sign, a taillight, a logo. Unlike bleach bypass it stays clean, not
grainy; it fits premium tech, automotive, and spirits work.

The recipe:

- Pull global saturation 60-80%.
- Isolate one motivated color source with a secondary or power window and
  keep it at full saturation.
- Push contrast for deep, clean blacks — not muddy, and not grain-heavy.
- Keep highlight detail sharp rather than soft or glowing.

Why: isolating a single saturated color against a desaturated frame directs
eye-attention with near-total control, a technique cinematography borrowed
from noir and neo-noir stylists. It works because saturation contrast is a
stronger attention cue to the eye than brightness contrast alone.

Example: "desaturated neo-noir frame, deep clean blacks, the single red
taillight kept fully saturated as the only color in the shot."

Counter-example: desaturating everything including the product's brand-color
accent — removes the exact visual anchor the technique depends on and the
frame just reads as gray and lifeless.
