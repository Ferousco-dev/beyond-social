When a piece is more than one shot, consistency of light and color is what makes
it feel like one film instead of stitched clips. Generated shots drift by default,
so continuity must be stated, not assumed.

Practice:

- Fix a look and repeat it verbatim in every shot's prompt: same time of day, same
  key direction, same grade words. Copy the lighting and color clause across shots.
- Keep the subject's identity stable: same wardrobe, same product colorway, named
  each time if it matters.
- Hold white balance: do not let one shot go warm and the next cool unless the
  scene changes justify it.
- Match energy: pacing and camera style should belong to the same family across
  shots.

Why: viewers read inconsistent color and light as "cheap" or "glitchy" even when
each shot is individually good. Continuity is a large part of perceived production
value in multi-shot short-form.

Example: every shot ends with the same clause: "golden hour, warm filmic grade,
soft key from left." Counter-example: shot 1 is cool daylight, shot 2 is warm
lamplight, shot 3 is high-contrast neon, with no story reason.
