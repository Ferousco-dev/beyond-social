The cut is the default and almost always the right choice; fancy transitions are
the exception, not the rule. Know the vocabulary and reach for the plainest option
that serves the story.

- Hard cut: instant change. Invisible when motion and logic match; use it for
  ~95% of edits.
- Match cut: cut on a shared shape, motion, or graphic between two shots (a wheel
  to a clock). Satisfying and premium; great for transformations.
- J-cut / L-cut: audio from the next/previous shot overlaps the picture. Makes
  dialogue and voiceover feel professional and smooth.
- Cross-dissolve: signals a passage of time or a dreamy tone. Sparingly.
- Whip pan / motion transition: hide a cut inside fast movement for energy; a
  short-form staple but easy to overuse.

For AI-generated pieces, prefer hard and match cuts between separately generated
shots: they are the most robust and read as deliberate. Reserve elaborate
transitions for a single intentional moment.

Why: overusing transitions is the fastest way to look amateur; a clean cut on
action is the fastest way to look professional.

Example: "Match cut from the spinning bike wheel to a spinning vinyl record."
Counter-example: star-wipe and spin transitions between every clip.
