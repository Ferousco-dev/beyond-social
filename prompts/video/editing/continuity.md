Continuity keeps a multi-shot scene coherent so cuts feel invisible. The classic
rules still apply to assembled generated shots.

- 180-degree rule: keep the camera on one side of the line between subjects, so
  screen direction stays consistent (a subject facing right keeps facing right).
  Crossing the line makes people seem to swap places and disorients the viewer.
- Eyeline match: when someone looks off-frame, the next shot shows what they see,
  from a consistent direction.
- Screen direction: something exiting frame-right should enter the next shot
  frame-left to feel continuous.
- 30-degree rule: change the angle enough between cuts of the same subject or the
  cut looks like a jump.

For generated multi-shot pieces, specify consistent facing and movement direction
across prompts so the assembled cut obeys these rules.

Why: broken continuity registers as "off" even to viewers who cannot name why, and
it undermines otherwise strong footage.

Example: shot A "subject walks frame-left to right", shot B "subject enters
frame-left, continues right." Counter-example: subject exits right, then abruptly
faces and moves left with no reason, so it reads as a mistake.
