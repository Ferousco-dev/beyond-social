Manipulating time is a storytelling tool, not a gimmick. Each speed choice says
something.

- Slow motion: elevates and dramatizes a moment; makes ordinary action feel
  premium (liquid pouring, fabric moving, a smile forming). The workhorse of
  luxury and product beauty shots.
- Timelapse / fast motion: compresses time; process, change, scale (clouds, a city,
  a build). Good for explainers and montages.
- Speed ramp: shift from slow to fast (or the reverse) inside a shot for impact,
  usually ramping into or out of an action beat.
- Real time: honest and grounded; the right choice for testimonials and UGC.

Prompt it plainly: "slow motion", "high-speed slow-mo", "timelapse", "speed ramp
into the jump." For generated video, slow, deliberate motion also tends to render
more cleanly than fast, complex action.

Why: a deliberate time choice adds production value and directs emotion; using real
time everywhere leaves easy drama on the table, while slow-mo everywhere drains it.

Example: "Slow motion of milk splashing into coffee, droplets suspended."
Counter-example: everything at normal speed, including the one hero moment that
begged to be slowed down.
