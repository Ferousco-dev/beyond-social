A jump cut lands cleanest when it rides motion already happening in the shot,
either the subject's or the camera's, so the cut feels like it's carried by
the movement instead of interrupting stillness.

- Cut on a head turn, a step toward camera, a gesture crossing frame, or a
  snap zoom, so motion continues across the cut point.
- Use a fast push or snap zoom, 2-4 frames, right before the cut so the eye
  lands on a blur instead of a hard stop.
- If the subject is static, add a small camera move, a handheld micro-shift
  or a slow push, on one side of the cut so it isn't a still-to-still jump.
- Loosely match the direction of motion across the cut, a gesture moving
  right should be followed by continued rightward energy, unless the reversal
  is itself the intended punch.

Why: editors cover cuts with action because the eye tracks movement rather
than comparing two static compositions frame by frame. A cut hidden inside
motion reads as energetic and intentional; the same cut between two static
holds reads as a skipped frame.

Example: "cut as the speaker's hand reaches the top of the gesture, into a
closer angle continuing that same gesture."

Counter-example: cutting between two shots where the subject is motionless in
an identical pose in both. The cut has nothing to ride on and reads as a
freeze-frame error.
