Cut density across a short-form video should follow an arc: fastest at the
open to hook attention, varied through the body to match content beats, then
either fast again for payoff or slow for a sincere close, rather than one flat
cutting rate for the whole runtime.

- Hook, 0-3s: fastest cutting, establishes "this is produced, keep watching."
- Body: cut rate follows the information, tightening on lists and proof
  points, loosening on explanation.
- Pre-CTA or emotional beat: slow down noticeably; a flat, mechanical rate
  here reads as insincere.
- CTA or payoff: either a final fast burst for urgency, or a single held shot
  for trust, chosen by what the offer needs, not by habit.

Why: viewers unconsciously read cut rate as a proxy for a video's confidence
and mood. A flat rate through every section collapses all of those signals
into one, so the moment meant to feel urgent and the moment meant to feel
sincere end up feeling identical, which undercuts both.

Example: "fast 0.5s cuts for the three-item hook list, slowing to 3-4s holds
through the trust-building close."

Counter-example: applying the same aggressive hook-cadence all the way
through the testimonial section at the end, making a personal, sincere line
feel like it's being rushed off screen.
