A jump cut needs a discontinuity large enough for the eye to register as
deliberate. Too small a change between two shots of the same subject reads as
a technical error, a twitch, instead of a cut.

- Change at least one of framing scale (10%+ difference), head or body angle,
  or position in frame; changing none of these produces a flash-frame
  stutter.
- If two takes have nearly identical framing, either punch in digitally to
  force a scale change, or don't cut them back to back.
- Angle change under roughly 5 degrees with identical scale is the danger
  zone, the classic "why does this look broken" complaint.
- Across a run of several jump cuts, alternate the direction and degree of
  change so the sequence doesn't read as a repeating loop.

Why: the visual system treats a small unexplained shift as an error signal,
like a dropped frame, because there's no compositional cue telling the brain
the editor meant it. A bigger, legible change supplies that cue and lets the
brain file the cut as punctuation instead of a fault.

Example: cut from a medium shot to a visibly closer close-up, not a medium
shot to a barely-different medium shot.

Counter-example: two takes with nearly identical framing and a 3-degree angle
difference cut back to back. The audience reads it as a stutter or a dropped
frame, not as an edit.
