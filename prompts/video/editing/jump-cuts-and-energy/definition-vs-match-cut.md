A jump cut removes time between two shots of the same subject from a similar
angle, and the resulting discontinuity is the point. It is not a continuity
error, and it is not a match cut, which hides a scene change by aligning shape
or motion across it. Naming the wrong one in a brief gets you the wrong shots.

- Jump cut: same subject, same or near-same framing, angle change under
  roughly 30 degrees, a chunk of time missing on purpose.
- Match cut: two different subjects or scenes joined because a shape, motion
  line, or composition lines up, used for transitions, not talking-head pace.
- Continuity error: an unintentional mismatch the editor never meant the
  audience to notice; a jump cut is intentional and foregrounded, not hidden.
- State which one you want in the brief: "jump cut for pace," "match cut into
  the next scene," or "no visible cut, keep continuity."

Why: each device needs different coverage to pull off. A jump cut needs two
takes of the same setup. A match cut needs a bridging shape or motion across a
scene change. A continuity-locked cut needs matched blocking and timing on
both sides. Mixing up the vocabulary in a brief gets you footage shot for the
wrong purpose, and no amount of editing recovers that.

Example: "cut from the mid-shot to a slightly closer version of the same
setup, two seconds of dialogue removed — a jump cut, not a scene change."

Counter-example: briefing "jump cut" when what's wanted is a smooth transition
into a new location. The result looks like a broken continuity error, because
there was no reason given for the viewer to lose those two seconds.
