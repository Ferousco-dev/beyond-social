In a product-demo video, jump cuts should compress the walk between features,
not the features themselves: cut out the reach for the mouse or the loading
spinner, keep each feature's actual moment of use intact.

- Cut out navigation dead time, opening a menu, waiting for a page load,
  scrolling to find a button, between two feature beats.
- Keep the actual interaction, click to result, uncut wherever possible;
  that's the moment that proves the product works.
- Jump cut straight from "feature A demonstrated" to "feature B starting,"
  skipping the walk back to a home screen.
- If more than three features are shown, vary the compression amount per cut,
  some 2 seconds skipped, some 8, rather than a uniform rhythm that starts to
  feel like a slideshow.

Why: a demo's credibility rests on showing enough of each interaction that a
skeptical viewer believes it's real within the beat, while the connective
tissue between beats, menus, loading, repositioning, carries no proof value
and only costs watch time. That connective tissue is the correct place to
spend the jump cut's compression.

Example: "jump cut from clicking 'export' straight to the exported file
appearing, skipping the four-second render wait."

Counter-example: jump-cutting mid-interaction, right as the cursor clicks the
key button, leaving the viewer unsure whether the click actually produced the
result they're about to see.
