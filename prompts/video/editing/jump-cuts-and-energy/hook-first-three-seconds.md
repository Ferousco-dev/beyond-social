The first jump cut should land in the first one to two seconds, before the
viewer decides whether to keep scrolling. A static, uncut opening frame reads
as a thumbnail, not as something already in motion.

- Open on a shot already in progress, mid-gesture or mid-sentence, then jump
  cut to a reframe within the first two seconds.
- Skip a slow zoom or a held wide shot as the literal opening frame; it reads
  as passive video rather than something worth stopping for.
- Pair the first cut with a real scale or position change, not a bare
  recompose: close-up to tighter close-up, or a step toward camera.
- Save the biggest visual change (new location, wardrobe, lighting) for the
  second or third cut, not the opening frame, so the autoplay thumbnail
  doesn't misrepresent the video.

Why: short-form feeds autoplay muted and weight early watch-time heavily. A
cut inside the first beats functions the way a loud opening line does in
audio: it resets the viewer's scroll reflex by signaling that the video is
already being edited, not just recorded and left running.

Example: open tight on a hand starting an action, cut half a second later to
the speaker's face mid-word, already talking.

Counter-example: five static seconds of someone adjusting their position
before speaking, no cut, no reframe. It reads as dead air and gets scrolled
past before the hook has a chance to land.
