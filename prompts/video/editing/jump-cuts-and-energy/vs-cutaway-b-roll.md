Choose a jump cut when the goal is compressing time in the same shot, and a
cutaway to B-roll when the goal is hiding an edit that would otherwise be too
jarring, or covering an action not worth showing in the main shot.

- Use a jump cut when the audience should feel time pass on the same subject,
  a demo getting to its result faster.
- Use a cutaway when the discontinuity would be too large for a jump cut to
  read cleanly, a full costume or location change, or removing 30+ seconds.
- Use a cutaway to cover an uninteresting action, screwing in ten screws,
  while the audio track continues over it.
- Don't default to B-roll for every skip; if the on-camera face is what's
  selling the moment, a jump cut keeps that face in frame instead of cutting
  away from it.

Why: a jump cut foregrounds the discontinuity as part of the energy, while a
cutaway's whole function is to make an edit invisible by giving the eye
somewhere else to look during the skip. Picking the wrong one either exposes
an edit that needed hiding, or hides a moment that needed to stay visible.

Example: "jump cut across the three quick reps of the same demo; cutaway to
the finished product to skip the 40-second wait in between."

Counter-example: jump-cutting straight across a 40-second gap with no B-roll
bridging it, producing a jarring skip that reads as a mistake, not a choice.
