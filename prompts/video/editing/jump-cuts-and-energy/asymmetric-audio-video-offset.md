A jump cut doesn't have to move picture and sound on the same frame. Offsetting
them with a split edit, a J-cut or an L-cut, softens the visual jar while
keeping the pacing benefit the jump cut provides.

- L-cut: let the outgoing shot's audio continue briefly after the picture has
  already cut to the next shot.
- J-cut: let the incoming shot's audio start slightly before its picture
  appears, so the voice leads the cut.
- Use a 3-8 frame offset for subtle smoothing; stretch to a half second when
  bridging a bigger jump.
- Reserve a hard, simultaneous cut for moments meant to feel the
  discontinuity, not soften it, a big reveal or a comedic beat.

Why: real conversation and edited documentary rarely cut picture and sound in
perfect lockstep. A split edit mimics how attention actually works, a voice
registers before the eye turns to look, so it reads as a more natural cut than
a synchronized hard cut, without losing the compression a jump cut provides.

Example: "the new shot's dialogue starts four frames before the picture cuts
to it — a J-cut into the reveal."

Counter-example: hard-syncing picture and audio to the same frame on every
single cut in the video. Technically correct, but it produces a metronomic,
slightly robotic rhythm across a full minute of cuts.
