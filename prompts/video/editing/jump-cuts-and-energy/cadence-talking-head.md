For UGC-style talking-head video, cut frequency should track speech rhythm:
roughly one jump cut every two to four seconds through the informational body,
tightening on the hook and loosening on the trust-building beat.

- Body and explainer sections: cut every 2-4 seconds, aligned to clause or
  sentence boundaries, not arbitrary timecode marks.
- Hook, first three seconds: cut faster, sub-2-second, to establish energy.
- Trust or CTA moment: let a sentence play uncut for 4-6+ seconds; a face that
  never holds still there reads as anxious, not confident.
- Vary shot scale slightly between cuts, chest-up to head-and-shoulders,
  rather than reusing identical framing, or the cuts read as a stutter.

Why: cadence encodes tone. Constant fast cutting signals urgency and can
undercut a moment meant to build trust, like an offer or a guarantee, while a
cadence that never varies trains the eye to filter the cuts out as noise
instead of reading them as punctuation.

Example: "cut on each new clause for the first eight seconds, then hold the
CTA sentence uncut for a full five seconds."

Counter-example: a uniform hard cut exactly every three seconds regardless of
content, including through the price reveal, which flattens the one moment
that should feel like the speaker is looking the viewer in the eye.
