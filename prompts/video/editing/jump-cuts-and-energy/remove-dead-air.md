The core practical use of a jump cut in single-camera talking content is
removing unusable time between two good phrases: fillers, false starts,
breaths, thinking pauses. It's a repair tool before it's a decoration.

- Cut at the boundary of a completed thought, not mid-word; the surviving
  sentence should sound whole even though seconds are missing.
- Trim on the outbreath after a phrase, before the next inhale, so the splice
  doesn't clip a breath sound in half.
- Leave 2-4 frames of natural pause around the cut instead of trimming silence
  to zero, or the speech sounds artificially glued together.
- If more than two consecutive fillers need removing in one spot, treat that
  as a sign the take is bad, not a sign to cut faster around it.

Why: this is the original function of the jump cut in single-take
documentary and vlog editing. It lets one continuous take stand in for what
would otherwise need a full retake, while the visible jump tells the audience
that time was compressed, rather than pretending nothing happened.

Example: cut from "...so what I did was—" [4 seconds of restart removed]
"—I switched the order of the two steps."

Counter-example: cutting mid-syllable to shave a fraction of a second,
producing a clipped consonant that sounds like a glitch instead of an edit.
