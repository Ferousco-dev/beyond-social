Rapid cutting has a point of diminishing returns. Sustained past roughly one
cut per second for more than a few seconds, an edit reads as frantic rather
than energetic, and comprehension of what's being said drops with it.

- Reserve sub-1-second cut rates for short bursts, a hook, a list rundown, a
  beat-synced moment, not the full runtime.
- After a fast burst, decompress: give the next 3-5 seconds a slower cut rate
  so the eye can recover before the next burst arrives.
- If a section's information density is low, don't compensate with faster
  cuts; simplify or trim the line instead of dressing it up with edits.
- Avoid fast cutting on the section carrying the core claim or offer; slow
  down exactly where the viewer is deciding whether to trust the message.

Why: cut rate carries a cognitive load, not just a style. Every cut asks the
viewer to re-orient to a new framing, sometimes a new background, and stacking
that demand on top of parsing new information exceeds working memory. The
rate that felt energetic in a 2-second hook becomes exhausting once sustained.

Example: "burst-cut the three-item list at 0.6s per cut, then hold the
explanation shot for four seconds."

Counter-example: a 30-second video cut at a uniform 0.8 cuts per second
throughout, including during the technical explanation, leaving viewers
unable to follow the claim being made.
