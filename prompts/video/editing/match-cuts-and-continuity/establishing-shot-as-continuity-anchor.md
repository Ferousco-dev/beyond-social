A clear establishing shot pre-loads the viewer's mental model of a space, which
makes minor continuity slips in the coverage that follows far less noticeable,
because the brain fills small gaps against an anchor it already trusts.

- Open a multi-clip sequence with one wide shot that fixes geography, scale, and
  the relationship between subject and space before cutting into tighter coverage.
- Reuse details visible in that establishing shot, such as a doorway, a sign, or a
  horizon line, as anchor points the following clips should not contradict.
- Skip the establishing shot only when the style is intentionally disorienting or
  the piece is a single continuous shot; otherwise it is cheap insurance against
  every other continuity gap in the sequence.
- Keep it short: two to three seconds is enough to register the space before
  cutting to closer coverage.

Why: without a wide reference, every subsequent clip has to re-establish the space
from nothing, so any small inconsistency between clips has no anchor to be
forgiven against and reads as a mistake rather than a natural gap the mind fills.

Example: a two-second wide of the full kitchen before cutting to three tighter
clips of hands working at the counter.
Counter-example: opening cold on a tight close-up with no wide shot, so any later
mismatch in the background is immediately glaring with nothing to anchor it.
