Composition is a spatial anchor as much as light or color; a subject that hops
between opposite sides of frame for no narrative reason forces the eye to
re-search the frame at every cut, which reads as amateur even when nothing else
is wrong.

- Keep the subject anchored in roughly the same region of frame, such as the right
  third, across a run of cuts on the same subject, so the eye does not have to
  relocate them each time.
- When composition must change, from wide to close, move the subject's anchor
  point deliberately and only once per cut, not incrementally drifting frame to
  frame across a sequence.
- Match headroom and lead-room conventions across the sequence; inconsistent
  headroom between near-identical shots reads as sloppy even without breaking any
  named rule.
- Use one consistent safe-zone appropriate to the aspect ratio, center-weighted
  for 9:16 social, across every clip so a later crop or reframe does not fight the
  original composition.

Why: the eye tracks a subject's position frame to frame as part of following the
story; unmotivated jumps in that position cost the viewer a beat of attention on
every cut, and those small taxes add up to a sequence that feels unsettled.

Example: the subject held in the right third of frame with consistent headroom
across three consecutive cuts of a talking sequence.
Counter-example: the subject centered in one clip and crammed against the left
edge in the next, with no camera move or blocking reason for the shift.
