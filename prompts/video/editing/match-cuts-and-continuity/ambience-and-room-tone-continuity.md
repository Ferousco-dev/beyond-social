Audio continuity is often more load-bearing than picture continuity, because the
ear notices an unmotivated discontinuity faster than the eye does, and a clean
continuous bed is what makes the picture cut disappear.

- Establish one base ambient bed (room tone, street hum, cafe chatter) for a
  location and carry that same bed under every clip cut into that scene, even if
  each clip's picture came from a separate generation call.
- Match reverb character to the implied space; a tiled bathroom rings differently
  than a carpeted bedroom, and that difference should stay consistent shot to shot.
- Where clips have no native audio, lay one continuous ambience track under the
  whole sequence in post rather than stitching four separately sourced beds.
- Treat any audio cut with the same care as a picture cut; an ambience bed that
  stops and restarts is as jarring to the ear as a jump cut is to the eye.

Why: viewers forgive small visual mismatches far more readily than they forgive an
audio bed that resets, because sound is processed continuously in the background
and a break in it is registered even when attention is on the picture.

Example: one continuous cafe-ambience track laid under four separately generated
clips of the same conversation.
Counter-example: each clip carries its own independently generated background
chatter, so the room tone audibly resets at every cut.
