Light direction encodes physical world logic, so naming and repeating a single
key-light direction and quality across every clip in a scene keeps the sequence
reading as one place at one moment.

- Name a single key-light direction and hold it: "sun camera-left, roughly 30
  degrees above horizon," repeated verbatim in every clip's prompt.
- Specify quality the same way each time: "hard sunlight" or "soft window light,
  slightly warm," never a vague "nice lighting."
- Keep shadow length and direction consistent between clips; mismatched shadow
  angles on the same subject are one of the fastest tells of a stitched sequence.
- Keep this file's discipline to within-scene continuity; if time is meant to
  pass, move the light on purpose and show that change rather than letting it
  drift by accident between generations.

Why: light direction is a physical-world cue the eye reads unconsciously; when it
flips between clips the brain infers two different rooms or two different moments,
even when every other element in the frame matches.

Example: "hard sunlight from camera-right, long shadows falling screen-left" held
identically across three consecutive clips of the same sequence.
Counter-example: dramatic side light in the wide shot cut into flat frontal light
in the coverage that immediately follows it.
