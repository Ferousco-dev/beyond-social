Do not translate a word or idiom in the voiceover literally into an image; it
reads instantly as stock footage reaching for a synonym instead of showing the
product or process the ad is actually about.

- Ban direct word-to-image translation for idioms and abstractions: "growth" is
  not a plant growing, "connect" is not interlocking puzzle pieces, "fast" is
  not a cheetah.
- Replace the metaphor shot with literal product or process footage tied to
  the actual claim being made in that line.
- If no product footage exists for an abstract line, hold on the speaker
  rather than reach for a stock-footage metaphor.
- Test every planned b-roll shot with one question: does this exist because of
  the product, or because of a word choice? Cut anything that only exists
  because of the word.

Why: the literal visual pun is one of the most recognizable stock-footage
signatures there is. It tells the audience the editor is illustrating
vocabulary rather than showing them the actual thing being sold, which is
exactly the generic-ad read that a premium buyer's eye is trained to skip past
in half a second.

Example: for the line "your team stays in sync," cut to an actual shared
dashboard updating in real time across two devices side by side.
Counter-example: for the same line, cutting to stock footage of a synchronized
swim team or a metronome — visually clever, thematically empty, and instantly
recognizable as filler pulled from a footage library.
