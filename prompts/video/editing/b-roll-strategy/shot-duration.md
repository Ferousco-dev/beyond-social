How long a b-roll cutaway holds should depend on how much the shot is asking
the viewer to do: glance, recognize, or actually read. Treating every b-roll
shot as the same length wastes time on simple shots and outruns the viewer on
dense ones.

- Simple single-subject inserts (a hand, a product, a label glimpsed): hold
  0.6-1.2s, just long enough to register.
- Shots carrying new readable information (on-screen text, a chart, a
  multi-step action): hold 1.5-3s, enough to read once at a normal pace.
- Never hold a static b-roll shot past about 4s without a secondary motion (a
  push-in, the subject's action visibly progressing) or it starts to read as a
  freeze rather than a held beat.
- On information-dense scripts, budget roughly one b-roll shot per clause, not
  per sentence, so hold time tracks how much is actually being said.

Why: cut length is a pacing signal the viewer reads whether or not they notice
it consciously. Too-short holds on information-bearing shots outrun normal
reading speed and feel chaotic; overlong holds on simple inserts stall
momentum that short-form's attention economy does not forgive.

Example: a 1.8s shot of a pricing table lets a viewer register "$29/mo" before
the cut; a 0.7s shot of a finger tapping "buy" keeps pace without needing to be
read.
Counter-example: a 2.5s static shot of a coffee cup carrying no information and
no motion — the extra time communicates nothing new and reads as dead air.
