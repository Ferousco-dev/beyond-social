The overall arc of shot scale and cut rate across a video should mirror the
arc of the argument it's carrying: broad context first, tight evidence in the
middle, a return to a human scale at the close.

- Open with wider, slower-paced shots that establish subject and setting,
  matching an opening line that is still setting up context rather than
  making a specific claim.
- As the VO moves into specific features or steps, shift to tighter inserts
  and a faster cut rate, roughly one shot per concrete detail named.
- Near the CTA, pull back out slightly to medium shots at a slightly slower
  pace, so the ask lands on a person rather than as the last item in a
  rapid-fire feature list.
- Plan this as a rough curve across the timeline (wide/slow, tight/fast,
  medium/moderate) rather than holding one shot scale or cut rate throughout.

Why: shot scale and pace are themselves a form of emphasis, not just a style
choice. Matching the edit's arc to the argument's arc (context, then evidence,
then the ask) reinforces the persuasion structure instead of running against
it, and keeps the middle from dragging or the emotional close from feeling
rushed past.

Example: 0-5s a wide establishing shot of the kitchen; 6-30s tight, fast
product inserts cutting roughly every 1.5s; 31-40s a medium shot of the
speaker slowing back down into the CTA.
Counter-example: identical tight, fast-cut inserts running from second one
straight through the CTA — the emotional close gets the same jittery treatment
as the feature list and lands with no added weight.
