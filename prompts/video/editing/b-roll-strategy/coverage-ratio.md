B-roll share should track what the segment is doing rhetorically, not sit at a
fixed ratio for the whole video. Process- and feature-heavy sections lean
b-roll heavy; trust-building sections lean back on a visible face.

- Default 40-65% b-roll for a 30-60s product ad or explainer body. The opening
  hook (first 1-3s) usually stays on a-roll or one strong establishing shot.
- Push b-roll share up to 70% for dense feature walkthroughs; drop it to
  20-30% for testimonial or trust-building beats, letting the face carry it.
- Never run more than 8-10 consecutive seconds of b-roll without returning to a
  human face or a UI element clearly tied back to the speaker.
- Return to a-roll for at least one beat around the CTA so the ask comes from a
  person, not a montage.

Why: b-roll carries information density and prevents visual monotony, but in
short-form, persuasion and trust still route through a visible person making a
claim. Over-indexing on b-roll turns a personal recommendation into an
anonymous stock-footage reel and strips out the endorsement effect that made
the format work in the first place.

Example: a 45s ad opens on 4s of face (hook), runs 24s of b-roll across 6-8
cuts demonstrating the product, returns to 10s of face for a testimonial line,
then closes on 7s of face for the CTA.
Counter-example: an ad that is 90% product b-roll with the speaker's face only
in the opening second — viewers have nobody to attribute the claims to, so it
reads as an ad rather than a recommendation.
