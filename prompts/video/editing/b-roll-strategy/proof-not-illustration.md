Treat every b-roll cut as a piece of evidence for a specific claim in the
voiceover, not as a mood shot that merely sits alongside it. If the shot could
be swapped for any other pleasant footage without weakening the point, it is
not doing its job.

- For each claim, ask what a skeptical viewer would need to see to believe it,
  and cut to that, not to a generic aesthetic shot.
- Favor process and result footage (before-state, the action, after-state) over
  reaction or ambiance shots.
- Show hands performing the specific action named, not a stand-in gesture that
  merely gestures at the general idea.
- If no literal proof shot exists, hold on a close, deliberate insert of the
  actual object in question rather than cutting to an unrelated lifestyle shot.

Why: b-roll's persuasive power comes from functioning like a demonstration or
exhibit presented alongside a claim. Once it only decorates the sentence, the
video becomes audio-only content padded with pictures, and viewers who are
already skeptical of an ad register the absence of any real support for what
is being said, even without articulating why.

Example: the claim "cuts editing time in half" cuts to a split screen: a
stopwatch over the old manual process next to the same task finishing in the
new tool.
Counter-example: the same claim illustrated with a shot of someone smiling at a
laptop in a sunlit coffee shop — pleasant to look at, but it proves nothing
about the specific claim being made.
