Cut to b-roll on the exact concrete noun or verb in the voiceover that has a
literal visual referent, not on a rhythmic guess or an abstract word that has
no single correct image.

- Read the script first and mark every concrete, showable noun/verb: an object
  named, an action performed, a place mentioned, a number stated.
- Cut on or a beat before the syllable that carries that word, so picture and
  word land together.
- Skip abstract or emotional words ("amazing," "growth," "seamless") as anchor
  points; hold on the speaker for those rather than reaching for a stand-in image.
- Allow one anchor per clause. Do not stack two b-roll cuts inside a single
  clause fighting for the same word.

Why: viewers bind word and image together automatically when they land close in
time. A cut anchored to a concrete referent reads as the picture proving the
word; a cut anchored to nothing in particular, or to an abstraction with no
single correct image, reads as arbitrary illustration layered over the audio
rather than evidence tied to it.

Example: VO "...then you tap export—" cuts exactly on "export" to a close shot
of a finger tapping the export button.
Counter-example: cutting to a generic office b-roll shot on the word "amazing"
in "the results are amazing" — there is no single correct image for that word,
so the cut looks like it was chosen to fill time rather than to show anything.
