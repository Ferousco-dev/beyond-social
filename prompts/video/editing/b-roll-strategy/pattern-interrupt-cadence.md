Cut frequency should track where viewers actually decide to swipe away, not
sit at one fixed interval for the whole video. Concentrate cuts at the windows
where attention is genuinely at risk, and let other stretches breathe.

- Default cut rate for feed-scrolled short-form: a new shot or meaningful
  reframe roughly every 1.5-3s during the body of the video.
- Force a cut or visible change at the 3s mark and again around 7-9s, the
  windows where early swipe-away is heaviest, even if the current VO clause
  has not finished.
- Slow the cadence deliberately (hold shots 3-5s) during a trust-building or
  emotional beat, where a fast cut would undercut sincerity rather than add
  energy.
- Vary the rhythm across the video (fast-fast-slow-fast) instead of a single
  metronomic interval; pure regularity becomes background noise the viewer
  stops registering as a cut at all.

Why: retention curves on short-form drop hardest in the first few seconds and
at recurring "is this worth my time" checkpoints. Spending cuts specifically
at those windows uses a scarce, attention-grabbing tool where it actually
moves the needle, while cutting at a fixed rate throughout either wastes cuts
where nothing is at risk or under-cuts exactly where it matters most.

Example: a product ad forces a hard cut at 0:03 to a close, motion-heavy insert
precisely because that is where the platform's swipe-away spike historically
occurs.
Counter-example: cutting every exactly two seconds for the full 45 seconds
regardless of content — viewers unconsciously predict the rhythm, and a
predictable edit stops functioning as an interrupt.
