Three shot types get lumped together as "b-roll," but each carries a different
implicit promise to the viewer about what it's showing, and using the wrong
one for the job creates confusion the script never intended.

- Establishing shot: wide, orients the viewer to a place or context. Use once
  per new scene or location, rarely mid-scene, since re-establishing what's
  already understood wastes a beat.
- Insert shot: a tight, detail-level shot of an object or action integral to
  the demonstration (a button pressed, a label read). Use to prove a specific
  claim, not to set a mood.
- Cutaway shot: a shot that steps outside the main action's literal space (a
  reaction, a related but separate detail), used to bridge a trim or add
  context without claiming to share the same continuous moment as the shot
  around it.
- Don't substitute a cutaway where an insert is needed (it won't prove the
  specific claim) or open a sequence on an insert with no establishing shot
  first (it disorients before the story has earned any ambiguity).

Why: each shot type carries an implicit contract about what it's claiming to
show. Conflating them, most commonly opening cold on a tight insert with no
wide to orient the viewer, forces the audience to spend their first seconds
figuring out where they are instead of absorbing the pitch.

Example: open on a wide of the workspace (establishing), cut to a tight shot of
the field being typed into (insert) to prove the claim, then a brief satisfied
glance (cutaway) to bridge into the next line.
Counter-example: opening a product demo cold on an extreme close-up of a single
UI element with no establishing shot — viewers spend the first two seconds
orienting instead of listening to the pitch.
