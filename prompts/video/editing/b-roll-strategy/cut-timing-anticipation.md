Once you know which word a b-roll cut is anchored to, the cut should land
slightly before that word's onset, not on it or after it. Leading the audio by
a few frames reads as the image driving the sentence; lagging behind it reads
as a reaction shot.

- Cut 2-4 frames before the anchor word's onset as a starting default.
- For percussive, hard-consonant words ("click," "snap," "tap"), align the cut
  frame-exact to the consonant attack rather than guessing early.
- For soft or vowel-led words, lead a little more, since the ear perceives a
  soft onset slightly later than a hard one.
- Never let the cut land after the stressed syllable has already finished
  playing; by then the moment to feel driven by the image has passed.

Why: viewers unconsciously read causality from timing. A cut that lands just
ahead of the word plays as the picture prompting the sentence, which feels
deliberate and tight; a cut placed frame-locked to a beat grid without
listening for the actual transient still lands late relative to perception,
because the ear registers a sound's attack faster than a fresh frame can
register as "new."

Example: "...watch what happens—" cuts three frames early to the button, so the
tap on screen appears to trigger the word "click" that follows it.
Counter-example: cutting exactly on a metronome-locked beat marker without
listening to the actual consonant attack — technically on-grid, but it still
reads as sluggish because the audio transient reaches the ear before the cut
reaches the eye.
