When trimming filler words, false starts, or a pause out of a continuous
talking take, cover the cut point with relevant b-roll instead of letting the
same shot jump-cut on itself, but choose the cover shot for its relevance
first, not just its length.

- Cover a trim with 1-2s of b-roll that shows what the speaker is discussing at
  that exact moment, not a generic shot picked mainly to fill the gap.
- Keep the covered trim under about 1.5s of removed material; covering a
  5-second excision with one cutaway still causes an audio pace mismatch a
  viewer feels even without seeing the jump.
- Sync the cover shot's content to the surrounding words so it reads as an
  intentional cutaway rather than a patch placed to hide an editing seam.
- In deliberately UGC-style pieces, leave a few natural jump cuts in on
  purpose; a perfectly smoothed talking segment can undercut the raw,
  unproduced feel the format depends on.

Why: a jump cut on a static or lightly moving frame is one of the most legible
"this was edited" signals to a general audience. Covering the trim with
content-relevant footage removes the visible seam while adding information;
covering it with an irrelevant shot chosen only for its duration just swaps
one visible tell for a subtler one.

Example: trimming a stumble out of "...and then—the results, uh—the results
speak for themselves" and covering the cut with a second of the actual results
dashboard the speaker is describing.
Counter-example: covering every trim in the video with the same generic
skyline stock shot regardless of what is being said — the repetition itself
gives away that it is a patch, not an edit choice.
