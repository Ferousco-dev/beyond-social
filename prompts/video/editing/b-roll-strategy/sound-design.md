Silent or music-only b-roll is one of the fastest ways footage reads as
generated or stock, because a real camera in a real space always captures some
sound. Every physical action and every space shown needs its own audio layer
built underneath the mix.

- Record or source a sync foley sound for every physical action shown (a tap, a
  page turn, a cap unscrewing) and duck the music briefly under it.
- Layer a location-appropriate ambient bed (room tone, street tone, a faint
  cafe murmur) under every b-roll shot so the space feels acoustically present,
  not vacuum-silent.
- Match the reverb and roominess of the foley to the visible space; a small
  kitchen insert should not carry the reflections of a hall.
- When generated video has no native audio, build this layer in post as
  deliberately as if it were captured live sound, not as an afterthought added
  once picture is locked.

Why: silence or a bare music bed under b-roll is one of the fastest tells that
footage is generated or pulled from a library, because real single-take
footage never arrives acoustically empty. Adding a believable sound layer is a
high-leverage, largely invisible fix for making footage feel physically
present in a space rather than composited onto one.

Example: a shot of a laptop lid closing gets a soft foley thunk synced to the
frame of contact, sitting under a faint room-tone bed and the ducked music.
Counter-example: six consecutive b-roll shots carrying only a music bed with no
foley or ambience at all — technically watchable, but it subconsciously reads
as a slideshow rather than a filmed moment.
