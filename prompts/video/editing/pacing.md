Pacing is rhythm: how long each shot holds and how that length changes. It is the
heartbeat of a piece and the strongest lever on energy and retention.

- Short shots (under ~2s) feel fast, urgent, energetic; the language of hooks and
  hype edits. Fast cutting demands strong individual shots or it becomes noise.
- Longer holds (3s+) feel calm, confident, premium; they let a moment breathe and
  trust the subject. Luxury and emotional work lives here.
- Vary the rhythm: a run of quick shots then a held beat creates emphasis. A
  metronomic same-length cut is monotonous.
- Cut on motion and on the beat of the music; edits that land with the track feel
  intentional and satisfying.
- Match pace to platform and intent: social hooks open fast; a brand film can hold.

Why: pacing, more than any single shot, controls whether a viewer feels energy or
boredom, and whether short-form retains past the first seconds.

Example: three 1-second detail shots building to a 4-second hero hold on the
product. Counter-example: eight identical 2-second shots in a row with no build or
variation.
