Facial motion should be described as a specific, small muscle action with a
duration, not as a single emotion word standing in for the whole performance.

The recipe:

- Name the action, not the state: one corner of the mouth lifts, an eyebrow
  ticks up, the jaw tightens.
- Pair every action with a duration: a half-second smirk, not a held smile for
  the whole shot.
- Sequence, don't sync: eyes narrow a beat before the laugh starts, they do not
  move together.
- Avoid pure emotion labels. "Surprised" becomes "eyebrows jump, mouth parts,
  head tips back slightly."

Why: real faces move in fast, small, sequential units, what facial-expression
researchers call action units, layered with imperfect timing. Given only an
emotion label, generation models default to one broad, symmetrical, static
expression held for the shot's duration, which is the uncanny "AI smile" look.
Naming the sequence of small actions and their timing reproduces the asynchrony
that reads as an unscripted, real reaction rather than an applied expression.

Example: "her eyes widen a beat before her mouth breaks into a grin."
Counter-example: "she looks happy" — collapses into one static, symmetrical
smile held for the entire shot, with no lead-in and no asymmetry.
