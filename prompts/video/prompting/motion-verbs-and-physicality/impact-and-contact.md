Moments where two bodies or surfaces meet need a verb that names both the
manner of contact and the surface involved, not a generic "touches."

The recipe:

- Use contact-specific verbs: taps, presses, grips, brushes, slams, grazes.
- Pair every impact verb with a reaction beat: a flinch, a settle, a recoil, a
  small bounce.
- Name the surface explicitly: knuckles on wood, palm on glass, fingertip on
  skin.
- For a full collision, describe the follow-through, not only the moment of
  the hit: what recoils, what keeps moving.

Why: the instant of contact is a case generation models frequently struggle
with, since two surfaces briefly overlapping in space is an ambiguous geometry
problem, and models often resolve it by smearing or letting one surface pass
through the other. Naming the exact contact verb, the surface, and the reaction
beat gives the model a concrete, frequently-seen visual pattern to draw on
instead of an unresolved overlap.

Example: "his knuckles rap twice on the door, then he steps back."
Counter-example: "he touches the door" — ambiguous duration and pressure, with
no named surface or reaction, which is exactly the kind of contact moment prone
to clipping and smearing.
