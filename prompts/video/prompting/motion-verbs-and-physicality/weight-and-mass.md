The physical weight of an object or body shows up in the effort around the
action, not in an adjective attached to the noun. Saying "heavy" does nothing if
the motion described around it is a simple, effortless lift.

The recipe:

- Show weight through preparation: a knee bend before a lift, a two-hand grip, an
  exhale timed to the effort.
- Show weight through speed decay: heavy objects accelerate slowly into motion
  and stop hard when set down (a thud, not a glide to rest).
- Show weight in the body carrying it: a loaded bag pulls one shoulder down and
  shortens the stride on that side.
- For light objects, reverse all of it: quick pickup, no wind-up, and let the
  object drift slightly as if caught by air currents.

Why: animators call this simply "weight," and it is entirely conveyed through
timing and how a body accommodates a load, not through labels. Models don't
reliably translate the adjective "heavy" into altered physics; they will render
a normal-speed, effortless pickup regardless of the label unless the effort cues
themselves are in the prompt. Describe the accommodation and the weight reads
itself.

Example: "he plants his feet, bends his knees, and heaves the crate up onto the
truck bed, exhaling with the effort."
Counter-example: "he picks up the heavy box" — the adjective is doing all the
work and none of it transfers; the described action is still a simple, flat
lift.
