Speed should be encoded in the verb itself rather than stacked onto a neutral
verb as an adverb. The verb is what the model matches against a physical
pattern; the adverb is a weaker, easily-averaged-out modifier.

The recipe:

- For fast: dart, bolt, snap, whip, lunge.
- For slow: creep, ease, drift, settle, inch.
- For genuinely unremarkable pace: step, reach, turn, used only when the pace
  truly does not matter to the shot.
- Never pair a fast verb with a slow adverb, or the reverse. Contradictory
  signals get averaged into a mushy middle.

Why: "quickly walks" forces the model to reconcile two competing signals, a
neutral verb that means an ordinary pace and an adverb pushing for speed, and
the common result is a medium, unconvincing pace that satisfies neither. A
single verb that already means fast, like "bolts," removes the ambiguity
entirely and gives the model one clear physical pattern with a well-defined
speed, stride length, and posture to draw from.

Example: "he bolts for the exit."
Counter-example: "he walks very quickly toward the exit" — an adverb stacked
onto a neutral verb, which renders as a brisk-but-generic walk instead of an
actual run.
