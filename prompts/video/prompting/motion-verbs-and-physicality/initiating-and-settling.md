Name the moment before the main action and the moment after it, not only the
action itself. Real bodies never snap directly into or out of motion.

The recipe:

- Anticipation beat: a breath, a glance, a small shift of weight before the main
  move begins.
- Action beat: the precise verb for the main motion.
- Settle beat: a slight overshoot and correction, or a brief held moment
  afterward.
- Keep all three beats short. This is texture, not slow motion; a half-second of
  wind-up is often enough.

Why: animation's anticipation and follow-through principles exist because
muscles load before they fire and dissipate momentum after they stop; a body
that cuts straight into an action with no lead-in, and stops dead the instant
the action ends, reads as puppeted rather than physically driven. A generated
clip that skips both beats looks like it started mid-motion or was cut off,
which is a strong tell that undermines an otherwise clean shot.

Example: "he rocks back slightly, then pushes off the wall in one motion,
catching himself against the doorframe."
Counter-example: "he pushes off the wall" — an abrupt start with no lead-in and
no settle, which reads as teleported into motion rather than a body building up
and dissipating force.
