Name what a loose element does in response to the primary action, hair, a
jacket hem, a lanyard, a strap, because secondary motion is one of the strongest
unconscious realism cues a viewer registers.

The recipe:

- Pick one element to carry the secondary motion: hair, a hem, a lanyard, loose
  fabric. Do not try to animate everything at once.
- Tie its motion to the primary action's speed: a fast turn makes hair lag and
  swing wide; a slow turn barely moves it at all.
- Let it settle after the primary motion stops, on a slight delay rather than
  stopping in sync.
- One clearly readable secondary element beats five competing ones fighting for
  attention in the frame.

Why: animators call this "drag" or "overlap," and it is physics doing work the
primary action never explicitly stated. When hair, cloth, or a strap moves a
beat behind the body driving it, that lag reads as inertia acting on real mass.
It is a fast, unconscious cue: viewers register whether trailing elements have
their own momentum long before they consciously notice hair or fabric at all.

Example: "she spins toward the camera, her ponytail whipping a half-second
behind her shoulders."
Counter-example: "she spins toward the camera" with no mention of hair or
clothing — the body and every attached element move as one rigid mass with no
lag, an immediate tell of synthetic motion.
