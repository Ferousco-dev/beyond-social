Choose motion verbs that carry a specific implied sound, because audio-visual
coherence sells a shot before an actual audio track ever gets attached to it.

The recipe:

- Impact verbs with a built-in sound: stomp, slam, snap, crack, thud, clink.
- Soft verbs with a built-in sound: pad, brush, whisper past, settle.
- Match the verb to the surface named elsewhere in the prompt; a slam on glass
  implies a different sound than a slam on wood, so keep them consistent.
- Avoid sound-neutral verbs like "moves" or "goes" for any beat that is meant to
  land with weight or emphasis.

Why: viewers cross-reference the sound they expect against what they see even
when watching a silent preview or draft. A verb that implies a specific sound,
footsteps that "stomp" instead of "pad," pre-loads the expected foley in the
viewer's head and makes the visual read as more physically grounded on its own.
It also hands a later sound-design or foley pass a precise, specific cue to
match instead of forcing a guess from a generic action.

Example: "he slams the laptop shut and the room goes quiet."
Counter-example: "he closes the laptop" — a sound-neutral verb that gives no cue
for force or weight, which produces a mismatch when foley is added later, either
too soft or arbitrarily loud.
