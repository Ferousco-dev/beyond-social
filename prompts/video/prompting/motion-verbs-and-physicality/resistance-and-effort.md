Name the force a body or object is working against, wind, water, friction,
another surface, because motion described without an opposing force reads as
motion through a vacuum.

The recipe:

- Name the opposing force explicitly: wind, water, a stuck drawer, a heavy door,
  an uphill grade.
- Show the effort against it: leans into the wind, braces and shoulders the door
  open, wades through knee-deep water.
- Vary speed with the resistance: motion through water or mud is deliberate and
  slowed; motion against wind is jerky and unstable, corrected step to step.
- Give the resistance a payoff: the door finally gives way, the drawer jerks
  free, the last step out of the water is heavier than the ones before it.

Why: real-world motion is never through empty space; every action is shaped by
something pushing back against it, and naming that opposing force is what
separates deliberate, physically grounded movement from the frictionless,
weightless drift that generation models default to when no resistance is
specified. A condition mentioned without an effect on the body, "walks through
the wind," is not the same as a resistance the body visibly responds to.

Example: "she leans hard into the wind, jacket snapping back, each step planted
before the next."
Counter-example: "she walks through the wind" — names the condition but not the
resistance, so the walk animates completely unaffected by it.
