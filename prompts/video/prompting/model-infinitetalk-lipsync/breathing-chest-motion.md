When the mouth is the only moving part of a frame, a viewer's eye drifts to
whatever else is in shot, and a chest that never rises or falls is what a
static photo looks like, not a person holding still. Breathing motion is what
keeps the space around the sync convincing.

- Frame wide enough to include the shoulders or upper chest (a loose medium
  close-up rather than an extreme tight crop) so there is a place for
  breathing motion to actually read on screen.
- Prompt for it explicitly: "subtle rise and fall of the shoulders with
  breathing" since breath motion is easy for the model to omit if it's not
  named, given how little of the frame it occupies.
- Time it loosely to speech: a slightly deeper inhale before a longer sentence
  and a soft exhale at a sentence's end reads as physically motivated rather
  than decorative.
- Keep the amplitude small; visible heaving reads as out of breath or anxious,
  which fights a calm, confident brand voice in most ad-creative contexts.

Why: breathing is the involuntary motion underneath every other movement a
speaking body makes, chest, shoulders, even subtle head bob; when it's absent,
the whole upper body reads as suspended rather than at rest, which is a
different and more visible kind of wrong than a still hand or a locked knee.

Example: "medium close-up including shoulders, subtle natural breathing
motion, slightly deeper breath before the longer second sentence."

Counter-example: a tight, chin-to-hairline crop with breathing unmentioned in
the prompt, the frame has nowhere for breath motion to live even if the model
tried to add it.
