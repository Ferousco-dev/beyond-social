Templates make prompts consistent and fast to adapt. Each slot maps to one of the
structural elements (subject, action, setting, camera, lighting, style). Fill the
brackets; keep the order.

- **Product hero:** "[shot size] of [product] on [surface], [camera move],
  [lighting] light, [background], shallow depth of field, [style], premium."
- **UGC / authentic:** "[person] holding [product] talking to camera, handheld,
  [room] with natural window light, slightly imperfect, phone-camera look, warm."
- **Establishing / mood:** "[wide/aerial] of [place] at [time of day], [camera
  move], [weather], [color palette], cinematic, [emotion]."
- **Explainer beat:** "[close-up] of [object/action demonstrating one step],
  [simple camera move], clean even light, minimal background, crisp, informative."
- **Transformation:** "[before state] then [after state] of [subject], [match cut
  or quick transition], consistent lighting, satisfying."

Why: a slot template forces every prompt to carry the load-bearing information and
makes A/B iteration a matter of swapping one slot, not rewriting from scratch.

Example (product hero filled): "Macro shot of a matte-black watch on wet slate,
slow dolly-in, hard key light from the left, dark background, shallow depth of
field, cinematic, premium." Counter-example: reusing one frozen paragraph for every
product regardless of shape, surface, or mood.
