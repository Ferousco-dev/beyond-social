After the hook, the job is to keep giving the viewer a reason to stay for the next
second. Watch-time and completion are what the algorithms reward, so retention is
distribution.

Tactics:

- Change something every few seconds: a new shot, angle, or piece of information.
  Visual monotony is the main cause of mid-video drop-off.
- Pattern interrupts: a zoom, a sound accent, a text pop, a location change, to
  reset attention right before it would wander.
- Keep forward momentum: each beat should raise a small question the next answers.
  Never resolve everything before the end.
- Cut tight: remove dead air and any second that does not advance the piece. If a
  shot can be shorter, make it shorter.
- Earn the length: only be as long as you have interesting material for. A tight
  12 seconds beats a padded 30.

Why: completion rate drives reach, and it is won or lost in the middle where most
creators coast. Deliberate retention design compounds with a strong hook.

Example: information delivered as a quick escalating list, each item a new shot and
a sound tick. Counter-example: one static talking shot for 25 seconds.
