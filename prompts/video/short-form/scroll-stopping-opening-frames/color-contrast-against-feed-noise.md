The opening frame's dominant color should read as visually distinct from the
desaturated blue-gray-white UI chrome and the muted, blown-out phone-camera
footage that surrounds it in a typical feed. A frame that blends into that
average reads as more of the same and never registers as a discrete object to
stop on.

- Identify one saturated hue to anchor frame one (a red jacket, a yellow bowl,
  a green wall) rather than a full-palette "colorful" scene, which reads as
  busy, not distinct.
- Warm colors (red, orange, coral) against cool ambient light, or a single
  saturated accent against a neutral background, both out-contrast the
  blue-heavy cast of most phone footage and screen glow.
- Avoid opening on skin-tone-dominant, beige, or gray-dominant frames unless a
  deliberate high-contrast element (dark background, bright prop) breaks it up.
- Check the frame in grayscale: if the subject disappears into the background
  at zero saturation, the color contrast isn't doing enough work either.

Why: a scrolling feed is a rapid sequence of visually similar frames; the eye
pre-attentively flags outliers in hue and luminance before any conscious
reading happens. A frame that stands out chromatically gets flagged as
"different" in that pre-attentive pass, which is what actually interrupts the
scroll, independent of subject matter.

Example: "frame 1: single red mug on a matte charcoal counter, everything else
desaturated and out of focus behind it."
Counter-example: a beige kitchen, beige cabinets, beige subject in a cream
sweater, technically well lit but chromatically identical to the feed around
it.
