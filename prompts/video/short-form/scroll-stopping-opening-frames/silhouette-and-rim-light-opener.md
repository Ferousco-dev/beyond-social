Backlighting a subject into partial or full silhouette on frame one, with only
a rim of light tracing their edge, withholds facial and surface detail and
forces the eye to read shape and gesture first. That withheld information is
a second, distinct curiosity gap from color or texture: "who or what is that."

- Key light comes from behind or behind-and-above the subject, at a low
  three-quarter backlight angle, strong enough to blow a bright edge along
  hair, shoulders, and any raised limbs.
- Let the front of the subject fall two-plus stops under the background
  exposure, near-silhouette but with a whisper of shadow detail retained, not
  a flat black cutout, which reads as a graphic, not a photo.
- Choose a recognizable pose or gesture, since shape is doing all the
  storytelling work with detail withheld: an arm raised, a head turned, a
  distinctive garment outline.
- Reserve this for a single subject against a comparatively bright
  background (window, doorway, sunset); it collapses in flat, evenly lit
  scenes with no exposure differential to work with.

Why: withholding a face while giving a clear silhouette activates the same
gap-filling curiosity as a close-up, but through absence rather than
excess of detail, and rim light additionally reads as unmistakably
camera-and-lens-derived rather than synthetic, since it depends on a real
light source occluded by a real edge.

Example: "frame 1: subject silhouetted in a doorway against blown-out
daylight, rim light tracing hair and shoulder, front detail in shadow."
Counter-example: flat, even studio lighting on a subject with a dim
background; no edge separation, no silhouette, no gap to resolve.
