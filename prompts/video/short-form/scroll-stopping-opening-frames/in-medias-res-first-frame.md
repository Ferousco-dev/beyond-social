The literal first frame should show an action already in progress, not a person
about to start one. A body mid-turn, a hand mid-reach, liquid mid-pour, a door
mid-swing reads as "something is happening now" and interrupts the scroll before
the brain finishes parsing the thumbnail; a static ready pose reads as "something
might happen later" and gets skipped.

- Frame the moment 0.3-0.5 seconds after the action begins, not at its start:
  the arm already bent, the object already airborne, the mouth already open.
- Avoid symmetrical, centered "about to speak" poses at frame one; they read as
  a paused video, not a playing one.
- If the shot opens on a person, their weight should be shifted or their
  posture asymmetric, evidence of motion the eye can extrapolate.
- Let the framing feel slightly incomplete, like you cut in a beat late, rather
  than a composed establishing shot.

Why: the human visual system is tuned to detect motion and change over static
scenes. A frame that already implies velocity gives the eye something to
resolve, which holds attention for the extra fraction of a second needed to
stop a scrolling thumb. A composed, static opening gives the eye nothing to
chase.

Example: "frame 1: hand already gripping the jar lid mid-twist, wrist torqued,
lid barely cracked open."
Counter-example: a person standing centered, arms at sides, facing camera,
waiting to begin, first frame looks like a paused menu screen and the thumb
keeps moving.
