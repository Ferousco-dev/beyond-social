A 9:16 frame shot like a cropped 16:9 wide shot wastes most of its own height
on empty ceiling, floor, or background and leaves the subject small in the
middle third; at thumbnail and small-preview sizes that subject reads as
indistinct clutter, not a stoppable image. The opening frame needs the
subject sized for a tall, narrow window, not a letterboxed wide one.

- Compose so the subject occupies roughly 60-80% of the frame's vertical
  span at frame one, not centered in a wide shot with headroom to spare.
- Shoot natively in portrait orientation with the intended framing in mind,
  rather than shooting wide and cropping in post; native framing keeps
  focal length and perspective correct for the tighter composition.
- Push the camera physically closer or use a longer focal length rather than
  digitally zooming into a wide capture, which softens detail and can reveal
  compression artifacts at frame one.
- Leave headroom proportional to a tall frame, tighter than 16:9 habit
  suggests, roughly a fist's width above the head, not the wider margin a
  landscape shot would use.

Why: at the size a phone actually renders it, a small, centered subject in a
sea of dead frame loses almost all its detail and reads as noise before the
brain can identify it; filling the vertical frame keeps the subject's
identifying detail, expression, texture, gesture, legible at the actual
viewing size and distance a feed is watched at.

Example: "frame 1, 9:16: subject framed from mid-chest up, filling most of
the vertical frame, minimal headroom, background a soft blur strip top and
bottom."
Counter-example: a landscape-composed shot center-cropped to vertical,
subject occupying a small box in the middle third with wasted space above
and below.
