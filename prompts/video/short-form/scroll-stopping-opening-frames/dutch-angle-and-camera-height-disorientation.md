Feed footage overwhelmingly sits at eye level and level horizon because
that's how a phone is naturally held; a first frame shot from an unexpected
height or with a deliberate tilt breaks that visual monotony immediately,
before the viewer has processed the subject matter at all.

- Shoot from a low angle, camera near floor or table height, looking up, or a
  high angle looking down, rather than the default chest-height phone
  position.
- If tilting the horizon, commit to a clear 10-20 degree dutch angle, not a
  barely-there 3-degree tilt that reads as an accidental unlevel shot rather
  than an intentional one.
- Pair the unusual angle with a reason the composition supports, looking up
  at something tall, looking down at something small and detailed, so it
  reads as motivated framing, not a gimmick.
- Do not hold the unusual angle for the whole video; use it specifically to
  break the opening frame, then normalize the camera once attention is won.

Why: repeated exposure to the same eye-level, level-horizon framing in every
other video in the feed makes it the visual default the brain stops
registering; any deviation from that default is processed as novel purely
because it violates a learned expectation, independent of what the angle
actually shows.

Example: "frame 1: low angle from near ground level looking up at the
subject stepping over the camera, slight upward tilt."
Counter-example: a barely-tilted, mostly-level shot at standard chest
height, indistinguishable from every other clip in the feed at a glance.
