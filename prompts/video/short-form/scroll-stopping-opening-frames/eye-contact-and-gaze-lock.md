If a human subject is visible in the opening frame, their eyes should already
be locked on the lens before the first frame renders, not mid-glance-up or
mid-blink. Direct gaze is one of the strongest involuntary attention triggers
the visual system has; it fires faster than reading a caption does.

- Eyeline hits the lens dead-on, not the eyeline of a phone held slightly off
  to the side, which reads as "recording myself," not "talking to you."
- Eyebrows and lids should be relaxed and open, not mid-blink; a blink caught
  as frame one reads as a closed-eye photo and undercuts the effect entirely.
- Pair the gaze with a slight forward lean or closer-than-neutral framing,
  intensifying the sense of address rather than passive presence.
- If the subject is not looking at camera by design (POV, candid), commit to
  it fully rather than a half-glance that reads as an accident.

Why: eye contact triggers gaze-following and social-attention circuitry
automatically, before conscious evaluation of the content. A scrolling viewer's
attention gets grabbed by a pair of eyes meeting theirs before they've
processed anything else in frame, which is exactly the fraction of a second
needed to arrest a scroll.

Example: "frame 1: subject's eyes already meeting the lens direct, chin
slightly down, leaning in, eyebrows relaxed, no blink."
Counter-example: subject captured mid-blink or looking just past the lens at
their own screen; the frame reads as unintentional and the connection never
lands.
