Most feed viewing is silent, so on-screen text is not optional; it is how the
message lands. Design it for a phone held at arm's length.

- Captions: burn in subtitles for any spoken words. They lift completion and
  accessibility, and they carry the whole message when sound is off.
- Legibility: large type, high contrast, a subtle background or stroke so text
  survives busy footage. Short lines, a few words at a time, synced to speech.
- Safe zones: keep text clear of the platform UI. On 9:16, avoid the top ~10% and
  bottom ~15-20% where the handle, caption, and buttons sit; keep key text in the
  central band.
- Hierarchy: one headline idea at a time; do not stack competing text.
- Kinetic text: reveal words on the beat to add energy, but keep it readable, not
  frantic.

Why: silent-first viewing means a video with no text often communicates nothing;
well-designed captions are a direct lever on comprehension and completion.

Example: bold two-word captions appearing in the center as each point is spoken.
Counter-example: tiny low-contrast text placed behind the TikTok caption and
buttons where it is unreadable.
