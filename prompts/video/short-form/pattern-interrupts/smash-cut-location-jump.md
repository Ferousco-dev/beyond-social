Cutting directly, with no dissolve or transitional device, from one location
or context to a visually unrelated one forces the viewer to re-orient in the
space of a single frame, which is a stronger jolt than changing the framing
within a scene.

- Cut hard, no cross-dissolve, wipe, or match-on-action bridging the two
  shots; the abruptness is the point.
- Carry one continuity thread across the cut, usually the same speaker
  mid-sentence, so the jump reads as a deliberate structural move rather
  than a mistake or a different video splicing in.
- Land the cut on a sentence or clause boundary in the voiceover, not
  mid-word, so the audio track stays intelligible across the seam.
- Use it once or twice per video; more than that and the video starts to
  feel like a supercut rather than a paced piece.
- Make the two locations genuinely different in light, color, and scale
  (outdoor daylight to a dim interior, wide to tight) so the discontinuity
  reads clearly even to a viewer glancing at a muted phone.

Why: geographic and environmental discontinuity violates a stronger
assumption than framing alone, because viewers track "where am I" as part of
basic scene comprehension, and a scene that changes entirely without warning
forces that tracking to restart, which is what produces the jolt.

Example: "cut from a busy street exterior directly to a quiet bathroom
mirror, same person mid-sentence, no dissolve."

Counter-example: a cross-dissolve between the two locations. The gradual
blend gives the viewer's spatial model time to adjust in transit, which
absorbs the shock the smash cut is meant to deliver.
