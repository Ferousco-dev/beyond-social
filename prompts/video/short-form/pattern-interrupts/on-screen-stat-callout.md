A specific number or data point that snaps onto the frame slightly ahead of
the narrator saying it, rather than appearing in sync with standard word-by-
word captions, breaks the caption-follows-speech rhythm the viewer has
otherwise settled into.

- Bring the number in one to two words before it's spoken, not simultaneously
  with it, so the visual precedes and primes the audio instead of merely
  echoing it.
- Size it noticeably larger than the running caption text, not just bolded.
- Use a fast scale-in (under 150ms) rather than a fade, matching the snap
  quality of other interrupt techniques rather than blending into a soft
  caption style.
- Pair with a light tick or click sound on entry.
- Hold it on screen only as long as it takes to read, roughly 0.05 seconds
  per character as a floor, then let it exit or be replaced rather than
  sitting as a static lower-third for the rest of the video.
- Use a real, specific number from an actual source; a rounded or invented-
  sounding figure undercuts the credibility the technique is trying to
  borrow.

Why: specificity itself is a pattern break against the vague claims most
short-form copy runs on ("a lot", "so much better"), and pairing that
specificity with a caption that violates the established sync-to-speech
rhythm compounds the effect; the number gets attention both for what it says
and for how it appears.

Example: "'73%' snaps onto frame just before the narrator says 'seventy
three percent,' oversized numerals, quick scale-in, light tick on entry."

Counter-example: the stat sitting in a static lower-third graphic for the
entire video. It reads once, on first appearance, then becomes part of the
frame's furniture and stops earning any attention at all.
