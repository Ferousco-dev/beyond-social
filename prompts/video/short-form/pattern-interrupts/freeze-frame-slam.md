Stopping motion dead on a single frame after a run of continuous movement is
a jolt built from absence: the eye has been tracking motion and the motion
simply stops, which is as disruptive as a sudden new movement would be.

- Freeze on the peak of the action, the exact moment of impact, catch, or
  arrival, not a frame just before or after it.
- Hold the freeze for roughly 0.3-0.6s; shorter reads as a stutter, longer
  starts to feel like the video has stalled.
- Add a faint film-grain or noise overlay across the frozen frame so it
  reads as a held photograph rather than a paused video file or a buffering
  glitch.
- Use it once per video; a second freeze immediately trains viewers to
  expect the trick and it stops landing.
- If a caption or graphic is going to stamp onto the frame, bring it in
  during the freeze, not before it, so the freeze itself is what earns the
  attention.

Why: the visual system allocates more processing to change than to static
scenes, so it treats the abrupt cessation of motion as a change in its own
right, the same reason a suddenly silent room draws notice. The grain
overlay is what separates a deliberate freeze from a technical failure; a
perfectly clean, perfectly still frame reads to a modern eye as dropped
playback rather than an edit choice.

Example: "action freezes on the exact impact frame, held 0.4s with a light
grain overlay, caption stamps in during the hold."

Counter-example: freezing on a motion-blurred in-between frame with no grain
added. It looks like the player choked rather than like an intentional beat.
