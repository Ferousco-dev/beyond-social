A caption that snaps onto screen with a sudden scale or rotation change,
rather than fading or sliding in at the same rate as every other word,
functions as a visual interrupt because it breaks the caption track's own
established rhythm.

- Animate the entrance in under 100ms so it reads as a snap, not a tween.
- Overshoot the target scale slightly (grow past 100% then settle back) so
  the motion has a physical, spring-like quality rather than a linear
  digital scale-up.
- Sync the snap exactly to the onset of the spoken word it represents, not
  a beat before or after.
- Limit it to one to three words per use, and use it once or twice in a
  video; if every word snaps in this way, none of them stand out.
- Give it a distinct type treatment (bolder weight, different color, larger
  size) from the baseline running captions so it reads as an exception even
  on a single frame.

Why: standard karaoke-style captions establish a steady rhythm within the
first few words, appearing and disappearing at a predictable rate the
viewer stops attending to. A single word that violates that rhythm, both in
timing and in motion quality, resets attention onto the caption track at
exactly the moment the video needs the claim to land.

Example: "'FREE' snaps in at 130% scale with a slight overshoot, settles to
100%, synced to the exact onset of the spoken word."

Counter-example: every word fading in and out at constant size, including
the "key" word. It's indistinguishable from the baseline caption style, so
there's no exception for the eye to register.
