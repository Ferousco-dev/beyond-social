A crash zoom snaps framing from medium to close in a handful of frames instead
of easing into it, compressing scale so fast it reads as the subject stepping
toward the viewer rather than the camera moving.

- Reserve it for a punchline, reveal, or the word carrying the sentence's
  weight, one or two uses per video at most.
- Keep the push itself to roughly 4-8 frames at 30fps; anything longer reads
  as a fast dolly, not a punch.
- Snap the zoom ratio (e.g. 1.0x to 1.4x) rather than easing it, and let the
  landing frame carry a slight handheld wobble instead of settling dead still.
- Pair with a single percussive sound hit landing on the same frame as the
  snap, not a rising whoosh that telegraphs it early.
- Vary the exact ratio and hold time between uses in the same video so the
  effect doesn't become a recognizable stinger.

Why: an abrupt, large jump in apparent scale mimics the visual signature of
something suddenly entering personal space, which the visual system flags
ahead of conscious attention. The handheld wobble on landing matters as much
as the snap itself: a punch-in that lands perfectly still and perfectly sharp
reads as a digital crop rather than a lens actually moving, which is the
fastest way to make the shot feel synthetic.

Example: "hard punch zoom from medium shot to close-up on 'wait', 6-frame
snap, slight handheld wobble on landing, low bass hit synced to the cut."

Counter-example: a smooth, linear digital zoom over a full second with no
settle. It plays as a template effect layered over static footage, and the
perfectly even ramp is itself a giveaway that nothing physical actually moved.
