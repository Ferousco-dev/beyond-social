When a video uses running captions, the CTA sentence's caption should match
the spoken words exactly, word for word, rather than switching to a
paraphrased or stylized graphic at the one moment it matters most.

- Let the auto-captioned or hand-timed caption track continue verbatim
  through the CTA line, same style as the rest of the captions.
- If a bigger CTA card is used for emphasis, echo the same wording as the
  spoken line, not a shortened or "punchier" rewrite that doesn't match what
  was said.
- Keep caption timing tight to speech through the CTA specifically; this is
  the worst moment for the caption to lag or truncate.
- Don't drop captions right before the CTA to "let the visual card breathe" —
  that's exactly where sound-off viewers need them most.

Why: a large share of short-form is watched muted, and if captions vanish or
diverge from the audio right at the ask, sound-off viewers lose the CTA
entirely at the one point it can't be missed; a mismatch between spoken and
written CTA also creates a half-second of confusion for viewers with sound
on, who notice the two don't line up.

Example: spoken "comment GUIDE and I'll send it to you" paired with an
on-screen caption reading the identical "comment GUIDE and I'll send it to
you," same font and timing as every caption before it.

Counter-example: captions run normally for the whole video, then cut out for
the final CTA line in favor of a stylized graphic that says "LINK BELOW" —
muted viewers, who make up a large share of the audience, never see what to
actually type or do.
