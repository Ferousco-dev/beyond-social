A dedicated end-slate — logo, tagline, static brand card appended after the
content — belongs in broadcast and pre-roll, not short-form; on a feed where
completion rate is a ranking input, a slate is dead runtime that exists
purely to be skipped.

- End the edit on the last frame of actual content or the CTA overlay,
  full stop; don't append a separate card afterward.
- If brand attribution is required, work it into the CTA line or a small
  persistent watermark during the content, not a standalone closing screen.
- Measure any slate against its own completion-rate cost: pull the drop-off
  at the exact timestamp the slate starts before deciding to keep it.
- If a client insists on a slate, keep it under half a second and cut it
  into the CTA beat rather than after it.

Why: platforms treat "watched to completion" as a strong quality signal, and
every second of runtime with no new information is a second where a viewer
who was going to complete the video instead swipes off one beat early — a
slate that adds zero content value directly taxes the metric the rest of the
video worked to earn.

Example: the video ends on the CTA text held over the last live-action frame,
cut hard to nothing — no card, no logo, no fade.

Counter-example: three seconds of a static logo animation with a tagline
after a punchy 20-second video — a completion-rate cost equal to 15% of the
runtime for a beat that carries no information the viewer needed.
