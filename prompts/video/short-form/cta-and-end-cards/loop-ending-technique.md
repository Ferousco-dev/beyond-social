For videos where the goal is watch-time and algorithmic push rather than an
external click, the strongest "CTA" is often no CTA at all — the last frame
is built to cut invisibly back into the first frame so the video rewatches
itself.

- Match the last frame's composition, subject position, and motion vector to
  the first frame within a few pixels and a consistent camera direction.
- End on the same held pose, gesture midpoint, or environmental state the
  video opened on, not a different setup.
- Cut the audio to loop cleanly too — no reverb tail or fade that makes the
  seam audible.
- Avoid ending on static black, a title card, or a logo, which breaks the
  loop and gives the eye a natural stopping point.
- Test it by watching the last two seconds bleed into the first two on
  repeat; a good loop is genuinely hard to spot.

Why: platforms weight rewatches heavily in their ranking signal, often more
than likes or shares, because a rewatch is unambiguous proof of interest with
zero effort from the viewer. A seamless loop converts "that was good" into
multiple views without ever asking the viewer to do anything, which is a
stronger and more scalable signal than a comment-bait CTA.

Example: a clip that opens on a hand reaching for a cup and ends on the same
hand in the same reach position, same framing, same light, so the cut is
invisible on repeat.

Counter-example: ending on a freeze-frame with a logo watermark — technically
a "loop" in the sense that it restarts, but the visible seam and dead frame
kill the rewatch entirely.
