A CTA has to name the exact physical action and the exact object of that
action; "learn more" and "link in bio" describe a category of behavior, not
an instruction anyone can execute in under a second.

- Use a single imperative verb: tap, comment, DM, save, screenshot, swipe up.
- Name the specific object of the action: "comment DEMO," not "comment
  below"; "save this for later," not "don't forget to engage."
- State the reward the action unlocks in the same breath, not as a separate
  sentence: "comment DEMO and I'll send you the template."
- Cut qualifier words that add friction without adding clarity: "feel free
  to," "if you want," "make sure to."

Why: at the point a viewer is deciding whether to act, they are running a
tiny cost-benefit calculation in under a second, and every unit of ambiguity
about what to physically do or what they get for doing it raises the cost
side of that calculation. "Link in bio" requires the viewer to remember the
instruction, leave the video, find the bio, find the right link among
several — each step sheds people. A CTA that names one keyword and one
reward removes every one of those steps.

Example: "Comment 'GUIDE' and I'll send you the checklist right now."

Counter-example: "Don't forget to check out more info in my bio if you're
interested!" — three qualifiers, no named action, no named reward, and an
extra app-switch required to even attempt it.
