When the video's real goal is distribution rather than an immediate external
click, a specific, answerable question aimed at the comment section
outperforms a hard-sell CTA, because it drives the engagement signal the
algorithm rewards without asking the viewer to leave the app.

- Ask a question with a short, easy, opinionated answer: "which one would
  you actually wear, 1 or 2?" not "what do you think?"
- Make it genuinely debatable — a question with one obvious answer gets no
  comments.
- Reference something specific shown in the video so the question requires
  no extra context to answer.
- Reply to a handful of early comments after posting; replies re-surface the
  video and prompt more comments.
- Don't combine this with a hard external CTA in the same video — pick
  whichever goal (distribution vs. direct conversion) matters more for this
  specific post.

Why: comments are one of the heaviest-weighted signals in most short-form
ranking systems because they require more effort than a like and predict
sustained interest, and a low-friction, specific question converts far more
of that latent opinion into actual typed comments than a generic "thoughts?"
does.

Example: a two-outfit try-on ends on "1 or 2 — comment your pick," both
options held on screen together for the question.

Counter-example: "let me know what you think in the comments!" over generic
b-roll with nothing specific to react to — there's no concrete choice to
weigh in on, so the prompt gets ignored.
