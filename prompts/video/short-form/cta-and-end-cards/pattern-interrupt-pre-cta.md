Retention curves on short-form show a predictable cliff in the last few
seconds as viewers who got what they came for start swiping; a small,
genuine pattern interrupt placed right before the CTA can pull a slice of
that group back for one more beat.

- Use one real change, not decoration: a step-in on the frame size (not a
  digital zoom, an actual physical step toward camera), a cut to a new angle
  on the same subject, or a sudden drop in music volume.
- Keep the interrupt under half a second; it's a flag, not a new scene.
- Tie the interrupt to content, not noise — a genuine tonal shift ("but
  here's the part people miss") works, a random sound effect for its own
  sake doesn't.
- Use this once per video, immediately before the CTA, never earlier and
  never twice.

Why: the brain's orienting response fires on sudden change, and a viewer
mid-swipe registers an abrupt shift in framing or audio even in peripheral
attention, which buys a fraction more time for the ask that follows to
register before the thumb commits to scrolling.

Example: the speaker physically leans into the lens on the transition line
"but here's the part that actually matters," music dropping out for that
half-second, then the CTA.

Counter-example: a stock whoosh sound effect and a flash-white transition
before every single CTA regardless of content — it stops functioning as an
interrupt once it's a recognizable template the platform's viewers have seen
a hundred times, and the "produced ad" feel it creates undercuts trust.
