A burned-in CTA has to survive being seen on a 6-inch screen at arm's length
for under two seconds, so treat it like signage, not like a caption.

- Minimum 8-9% of frame height for the CTA line's cap height on a 1080x1920
  canvas (roughly 90-100px).
- Bold or semibold weight only; regular and light weights disappear against
  moving footage.
- A hard stroke (3-4px) or a soft drop shadow at 60-70% opacity, not both
  stacked, to separate text from busy backgrounds without looking like a
  meme template.
- Hold the final CTA card for at least 1.5 seconds of dead time (no new
  motion) so the eye has a still target to land on.
- One line if possible; two lines max, center-weighted, never justified.

Why: short-form is consumed one-handed at variable distances and in bright
outdoor light, so contrast and size failures are the single most common
reason a CTA card gets scrolled past unread rather than rejected — the
viewer simply never registered it as text.

Example: "DM 'GUIDE' for the free template" — one line, bold, high-contrast
white-on-dark-scrim, held static for the last 1.5s of the clip.

Counter-example: thin sans-serif in a decorative color over a busy live-action
background with no scrim or stroke, changing position every half-second to
"look dynamic" — it reads as decoration, not an instruction.
