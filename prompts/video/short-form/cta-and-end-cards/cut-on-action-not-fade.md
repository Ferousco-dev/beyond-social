End short-form videos on a hard cut at the peak of the last action, not a
fade-to-black, dissolve, or slow deceleration into stillness — the ending
should feel like the content ran out of runway, not like a commercial
wrapping up.

- Cut on the frame where motion, gesture, or the punch line lands, within a
  few frames of its peak.
- Avoid audio fades, ducking the music tail, or letting ambient room tone
  trail past the visual cut.
- If a CTA card follows, hard-cut into it too, don't cross-dissolve.
- Reserve fades for genuine tonal breaks (before/after transformations), not
  as a default outro move.

Why: a fade or slow-down is a learned signal from broadcast and ad editing
that tells the viewer "this is over, disengage now" a beat before the video
actually ends, which is exactly the wrong cue in a feed where the algorithm
is measuring whether people watch to the literal last frame. A hard cut on
peak action keeps the viewer's attention live until the frame that matters
most — the CTA — is already on screen.

Example: the line "...and that's how you fix it" cuts hard to black CTA text
on the exact syllable, no audio tail, no dip.

Counter-example: the speaker finishes the sentence, the music swells and
fades over a full second, and the frame slowly dims before the CTA appears —
by the time the ask lands, a meaningful share of viewers have already
mentally exited.
