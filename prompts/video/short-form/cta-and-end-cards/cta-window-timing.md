The call to action belongs in the last 10-15% of runtime, after the video has
delivered the thing it promised in the hook, never stapled on before the payoff
lands.

- On a 20-30s video, hold the ask to the final 2-4 seconds.
- Deliver the payoff first (the tip, the result, the punchline), then the ask.
- If the video has multiple beats, let the last beat resolve completely before
  cutting to or overlaying the CTA; don't interrupt the resolution.
- Never open with the CTA and never repeat it more than once in a sub-30s cut.

Why: viewers grant attention in exchange for value, and the CTA is where that
value gets redeemed into an action. Asking before the payoff reads as an
interruption and triggers a swipe; asking after competes with nothing else for
attention because the content's job is already done. Retention graphs on
short-form consistently show a cliff wherever the video asks for something the
audience hasn't been paid for yet.

Example: "...and that's the exact ratio I use every time. Try it and tag me."
(payoff, then ask, four words, done)

Counter-example: opening on "follow for more tips like this" before a single
tip has been shown. It burns the hook's attention on a request the viewer has
no reason yet to grant, and the drop-off happens before the actual content
starts.
