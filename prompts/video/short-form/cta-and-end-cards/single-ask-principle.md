A short-form video gets one call to action, not "follow, like, comment, and
check the link" chained together, because every added ask dilutes the odds
any single one gets taken.

- Pick the one action that matters most for this specific video's goal
  (follow for a series, comment for a giveaway, tap the link for a sale) and
  cut every other ask.
- If the platform mechanic (follow, save) matters long-term but isn't this
  video's goal, leave it implicit rather than asking for it out loud.
- Sequence asks across a content calendar instead of stacking them in one
  video — this video asks to comment, the next asks to follow.
- Say the one ask once, not three times in three different overlays.

Why: attention at the end of a short-form video is a shrinking window, and a
list of instructions requires the viewer to parse and prioritize under time
pressure they don't have — most people take the path of least effort, which
is none of them. A single, specific, low-friction ask has a real completion
rate; a menu of asks collapses toward zero on all of them.

Example: "Comment 'SEND' and I'll DM you the spreadsheet." (one action, one
clear reward, nothing else requested)

Counter-example: "Like this, follow for part 2, comment your city, and tap
the link in bio" — four competing instructions in the last two seconds, none
of which gets meaningfully more uptake than doing nothing.
