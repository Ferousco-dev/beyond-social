Short-form retention graphs share a recognizable shape: a steep drop in the
first 1-3 seconds (hook filtering), a long shallow decline through the
middle, and a second steeper drop near the end where viewers who got their
answer leave — the CTA needs to sit before that second drop starts, not
after it.

- Pull the platform's own retention/audience-retention graph for the video
  (or for similar past videos before the video is even cut) and find where
  the shallow-decline section starts steepening again.
- Place the CTA at or just before that inflection point, not at the literal
  final frame if the inflection happens earlier.
- If most of the audience is gone by 80% of runtime, a CTA at 95% only
  reaches whoever's left — cut runtime or move the ask earlier.
- Re-check placement per video; the inflection point shifts with topic,
  length, and hook strength, it isn't a fixed percentage.

Why: treating "put the CTA at the end" as a literal instruction ignores that
"the end" of the audience's attention and "the end" of the timeline are
usually different timestamps — the second drop-off is the actual audience
boundary, and a CTA placed past it is being read by a small fraction of who
watched the hook.

Example: a 40-second video whose retention graph shows the steep second drop
starting at second 32 gets its CTA moved to land at second 30, not second 38.

Counter-example: defaulting to "CTA in the last 2 seconds" on every video
regardless of where the actual audience falls off, then wondering why a
video with strong overall views converts poorly.
