The CTA should live in the same location, light, and lens as the content
that preceded it, not cut to a cleaner "studio" setup — a visible register
shift into commercial-looking footage is the fastest way to flag "now the ad
part starts" and trigger disengagement.

- Keep the same white balance, exposure, and practical light sources through
  the CTA; don't switch to flat, even beauty lighting for the ask.
- Keep the same lens/focal length and camera distance the rest of the video
  used; don't punch to a tighter, more "produced" framing.
- Keep any handheld drift, room tone, or background activity present in the
  earlier footage running through the CTA rather than going still and quiet.
- If the CTA is a text overlay, place it over the same continuous shot the
  content ended on rather than cutting to a blank card.

Why: viewers pattern-match "ad register" instantly, and a shift to cleaner
light, a locked-off frame, and dead-quiet audio right as the ask starts reads
as the content ending and a commercial beginning, which is exactly the cue
that trained skepticism responds to by disengaging.

Example: the same window-lit kitchen, same handheld wobble, same ambient
room hum carries straight through from the demonstration into "comment
RECIPE for the full list."

Counter-example: the video cuts from a lived-in, handheld kitchen scene to a
static, evenly lit shot against a seamless backdrop for the CTA line — the
visual register change announces "sponsored moment" before a word is heard.
