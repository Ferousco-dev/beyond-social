If the hook opened a specific question or promise ("the mistake everyone
makes," "what happened next," "the number that changed everything"), the CTA
should sit at the exact moment that loop resolves, not after a separate
wrap-up beat tacked onto the resolution.

- Write the hook and the CTA together, before scripting the middle, so the
  resolution and the ask are structurally the same beat.
- Resolve the loop completely in the CTA line itself where possible: "...and
  that number is in the comments if you want the full breakdown."
- Avoid a resolution beat followed by an unrelated generic sign-off; that
  creates a second ending the viewer has to sit through.
- If the loop can't close without an external step (the link, the DM), say
  so as the resolution: "the full number's in the link, I couldn't fit it
  in a caption."

Why: an open loop is what kept the viewer watching in the first place, and
the moment it resolves is the single highest-attention instant in the whole
video — spending that instant on the CTA rather than on a separate
"thanks for watching, follow for more" wastes the exact leverage the hook was
built to create.

Example: hook is "I tried the $4 grocery store version of this $80 product,"
and the CTA is "comment PART 2 if you want to see how it actually performed"
— the loop (does it work?) and the ask land in the same breath.

Counter-example: the video resolves the loop, then adds a separate ten
second wrap-up ("anyway, thanks for watching, hope that helped, see you next
time") before finally asking for the follow — two endings where there
should be one.
