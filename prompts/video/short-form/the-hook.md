The opening seconds decide everything. On a feed, most viewers leave in the first
three seconds, so the hook is the highest-leverage part of the entire video.

What makes a hook stop the scroll:

- Open on motion or a striking image, never a slow fade-in or a logo. The first
  frame must already be interesting.
- Front-load the payoff or the tension: show the end result, the surprising claim,
  or the "wait, what?" moment first, then earn it.
- Create a curiosity gap: pose the question the rest of the video answers.
- Put the subject and the point in frame immediately; do not warm up.
- Make the first frame legible as a thumbnail; many impressions are silent and
  paused.

For generated content, invest your best shot and your clearest motion in the
opener; a strong hook shot is worth more than three good middle shots.

Why: retention curves are decided at the start; a weak opening means the rest of
the video is never seen, no matter how good it is.

Example: open mid-action on the satisfying result, then cut to "here's how."
Counter-example: a 2-second branded intro sting before anything happens.
