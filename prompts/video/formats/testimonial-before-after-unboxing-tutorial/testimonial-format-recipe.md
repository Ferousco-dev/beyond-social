A testimonial ad works by borrowing the credibility of an ordinary person talking to camera instead of a brand talking at the viewer, and its structure exists to protect that illusion, not to showcase the product directly.

The beat sheet:

- Cold open on a specific claim, first line, no brand intro, no "hi guys": a personal detail tied to the outcome, not a generic superlative.
- Name the prior struggle or the alternative already tried, in one sentence, so the viewer places themselves in the same position.
- The turn: the specific moment or detail that changed their mind, described concretely (a texture, a number, a feeling) rather than restated as praise.
- One brief proof beat, 1-2 seconds, product in hand or a visible result, cut in and back out fast.
- Close on a plain, undersold recommendation, not a jingle-line; the flattest delivery of the video should be the last line.

Why: viewers pattern-match polish to "ad" and imperfection to "person," and every beat in this structure exists to keep that second read intact; a claim without a prior struggle has no stakes, and a recommendation delivered with ad-cadence enthusiasm breaks the illusion right before the CTA lands.

Example: "I tried three of these before this one. [beat] The others left a residue, this one didn't, and that's genuinely it. [cut to product on counter] I'd get this one."

Counter-example: opening with "Hi guys, today I want to talk about this amazing product," then a list of five features, then "you should definitely try it!" delivered with sustained enthusiasm throughout, no struggle, no specific detail, no dip in energy, reads as brand copy with a human face on it.
