A before/after can be told in two different orders, and each order trades a different strength for a different weakness: starting on the after and rewinding sells intrigue, starting on the before and progressing sells rigor.

The two structures:

- Chronological open (before first): starts on the unglamorous starting state, states the timeframe, then reveals the after. Reads as more rigorous and harder to fake, because the viewer watches the "experiment" unfold in order; best when the product category carries real skepticism (health, skin, results-based claims).
- After-first open (reveal, then rewind): opens on the finished result or a striking after-state as the hook, then cuts to "here's where this started," then a quick return to the after. Reads as more attention-grabbing in the first second, because it leads with the most visually interesting frame; best for scroll-stopping on saturated feeds where the claim itself is less contentious.
- Never do both in the same cut, opening on the after, cutting to before, then a second full after reveal; the repetition drains the payoff the second time.
- If opening after-first, keep the rewind segment brief, under 2 seconds, so it reads as context, not a second competing story.
- Match the CTA's confidence to the structure: chronological open supports a stronger evidentiary claim in the CTA; after-first open supports a curiosity-driven CTA ("see how" rather than "proven results").

Why: the opening order is a promise about what kind of claim the video is making, a demonstration or a hook, and a viewer's tolerance for either depends on which promise they were given first; switching structures mid-format breaks the specific expectation the opening frame set.

Example: after-first — "[after shot, 1s] this took two weeks. [before shot] this is where it started. [back to after] here's how."

Counter-example: a video that opens on the after, cuts to before, reveals after again, then a third cutaway to more after footage, the viewer has now seen the payoff three times before the CTA and it has stopped landing as one.
