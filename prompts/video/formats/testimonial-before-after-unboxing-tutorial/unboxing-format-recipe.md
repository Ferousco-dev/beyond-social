An unboxing ad sells anticipation as much as the product itself; its structure is a slow withholding of the object followed by a fast, satisfying release, and most unboxing videos fail by rushing the withholding half.

The beat sheet:

1. Exterior establish (1-2s): the closed package as delivered, on a surface, undisturbed, no hands yet, so the viewer registers it as a real arrived object.
2. Approach (1-2s): hands enter frame, a beat of handling the exterior, a label read, a seal noticed, before any opening action starts.
3. The opening action (2-4s): tape peeled, lid lifted, or seal broken, slow enough that the physical resistance of the material is visible.
4. First reveal (1-2s): the initial glimpse through tissue, foam, or an inner lid, held just long enough to register before the object is fully exposed.
5. Hero reveal (2-3s): the product fully out, in hand or on the surface, the best light and framing of the whole video.
6. Detail inserts (2-4s): one or two close, static beauty shots of texture, finish, or a functional detail.
7. Close: the product in use for one beat, or a plain sign-off, not a second full reveal.

Why: withholding is the mechanism that makes the reveal pay off; a video that opens with the product already visible or rushes steps 1-4 to reach the hero shot faster has skipped the exact tension the format is built to release.

Example: "closed matte box on a wood counter, hands lift it, thumb finds the seal tab, slow peel, tissue paper glimpsed, then lifted fully into frame."

Counter-example: opening directly on the product out of the box in hero lighting, then cutting back to "unbox" a second box for the camera, the anticipation the format depends on never happens.
