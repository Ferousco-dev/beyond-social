An explainer trades a viewer's attention for understanding. Clarity is the whole
product; every choice serves comprehension.

Structure:

1. Hook with the question or payoff ("Here's why your X keeps happening").
2. One concept at a time, in a logical order, each as its own clear shot or beat.
3. Show the mechanism: demonstrate or visualize it; pair any number or term with a
   matching visual so eye and ear reinforce.
4. Recap or takeaway: the one thing to remember, plus a next step.

Craft: clean, uncluttered visuals; strong on-screen text and captions; simple
motion graphics for abstract points; steady pace, fast enough to hold interest but
slow enough to absorb. Cut anything that does not aid understanding.

Why: explainers fail by trying to say too much or by telling without showing; a
one-idea-per-beat structure with matching visuals is what actually teaches.

Example: "3 reasons your plants die" as three clean beats, each with a demo shot and
a two-word caption, ending on the fix. Counter-example: a dense 45s monologue with
five concepts and no supporting visuals.
