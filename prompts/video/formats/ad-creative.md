A direct-response ad has one job: move a specific viewer to a specific action. It
borrows storytelling but stays ruthlessly focused on the outcome.

Beat sheet:

1. Hook (0-2s): call out the audience or the problem, or show the desirable result.
   Brand and hook must land before the scroll instinct fires.
2. Problem / tension (2-5s): the pain the product removes, shown, not told.
3. Solution (5-12s): the product resolving it, with the one benefit that matters
   most, demonstrated.
4. Proof (brief): a reaction, result, or number that makes it credible.
5. CTA (final 2-3s): one clear action ("tap to try", "link in bio"), unambiguous.

Rules: one core message, not five features. Lead with benefit, not brand. Make it
feel native to the platform (UGC style usually beats polished on TikTok/Reels).
Test multiple hooks against the same body; the hook is the biggest conversion lever.

Why: ads that meander or bury the offer waste paid reach; a tight
hook-problem-solution-proof-CTA structure is what performs.

Example: "POV: your desk is a mess" -> the organizer in use -> "everything in
reach" -> "tap to shop." Counter-example: a beautiful brand mood film with no
problem, no benefit, and no CTA.
