A talking presenter (a real-looking avatar delivering a script) is the backbone of
explainers, testimonials, and much UGC-style advertising. It lives or dies on
believability and clarity.

Craft:

- Framing: medium or medium-close, eye level, subject on a third with a little
  lead room. Keep the mouth clearly visible for lip-sync fidelity.
- Lighting: soft key toward the face, gentle rim to separate from the background;
  avoid harsh shadows that fight the mouth movement.
- Delivery: conversational, one idea per sentence, natural pauses. Write for the
  ear. Energy matched to the platform (livelier for TikTok, calmer for explainers).
- Motion: subtle, natural head and hand movement; a static, frozen body reads as
  uncanny, but too much gesture distracts.
- B-roll: cut away to supporting shots over the voice (J/L cuts) so the piece is
  not one locked talking head; this also hides any lip-sync weak spots.

Why: presenters carry trust and information density; small realism failures (dead
eyes, stiff body, off lip-sync) break the spell, so framing and cutaways matter.

Example: medium eye-level shot, soft key, natural gestures, cutaways to product
b-roll over the key claims. Counter-example: a rigid, harshly-lit, unbroken 30s
talking head with robotic delivery.
