Reels rewards a smoother, still energetic pace where cuts land on a completed
action or composition rather than TikTok's mid-gesture jump cut, closer to a
music-video edit than a raw scroll-feed edit.

- Cut on the completion or peak of a movement (a spin's apex, a product's full
  reveal), not mid-motion.
- Favor match cuts and whip-pan transitions that carry visual continuity
  across the cut; continuity reads as intentional craft to a Reels viewer.
- Run a slightly longer average shot length than TikTok, roughly 1.5-3
  seconds, giving each composition a beat to register.
- Sync cuts to a music drop or beat marker rather than dialogue alone; Reels
  leans music-forward even in spoken content.
- Keep camera moves stabilized (gimbal, slider, or controlled handheld) rather
  than TikTok-native raw shake; the identical shake that reads as authentic on
  TikTok reads as under-produced on Reels.

Why: Instagram's broader visual culture, curated grids, aspirational feeds,
sets viewer expectations toward composed, edited-for-camera content. A Reel
cut at TikTok's raw jump-cut rate under-delivers on the polish Reels viewers
are primed to expect from the platform itself.

Example: a product spin that cuts to the next shot exactly as the label
completes its rotation, timed to the music's downbeat.

Counter-example: mid-spin jump cuts every half second with no continuity,
lifted wholesale from a TikTok edit. It reads as unfinished rather than
energetic on Reels.
