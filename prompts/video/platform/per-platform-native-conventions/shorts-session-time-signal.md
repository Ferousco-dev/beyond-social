YouTube's Shorts ranking factors total session time across the app, whether a
Short leads to more watching afterward, not only the Short's own completion,
so a Short that hands off well to more content performs differently than one
built as a pure standalone.

- Reference a series or a "part" structure when the content supports it
  ("part 3 of my kitchen renovation"), which cues both the viewer and the
  system toward continued watching.
- Avoid a hard, fully closed ending with no reason to seek more; a small open
  thread ("full version linked in the next video") supports session
  continuation.
- Keep the Short's own pacing tight regardless; single-video retention still
  gates whether it gets shown at all before session-time effects can matter.
- Connect thematically to the channel's broader catalog so a viewer who
  finishes the Short has an obvious next video in the same topic to land on.

Why: YouTube's core business metric has long been session time across the
platform, not per-video engagement in isolation, and Shorts ranking inherits
that bias. A Short's job is partly to be a good on-ramp into more YouTube
watching, not only to maximize its own isolated performance.

Example: a Short ending on "the full breakdown is the next video in this
Shorts feed," visibly part of a numbered series.

Counter-example: a fully self-contained Short with a definitive "the end"
card and no connection to any other content on the channel, leaving the
viewer nowhere to go next.
