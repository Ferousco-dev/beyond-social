TikTok viewers watch with sound on at meaningfully higher rates than Instagram
or Facebook feeds, a byproduct of the app's music and trend culture and its
autoplay-with-sound default, which changes what the audio track is allowed to
do compared with a silent-first platform.

- Write for the ear first in the opening beat: a distinct diegetic sound or a
  line of dialogue can be the actual hook, not just a captions backup.
- Use trending or recognizable audio as a deliberate genre signal; viewers
  identify a sound and know what kind of content is coming before they see
  anything, which sets expectations fast.
- Mix dialogue and voiceover forward and clean; TikTok's culture tolerates
  less music-bed-buried speech than a cinematic ad mix would use.
- Still caption everything; sound-on is a majority, not a universal, but do
  not design the video as if captions carry all the meaning when the audio
  itself should be doing real narrative work.

Why: because TikTok's feed and creation tools default to sound-on and its
content culture is built around audio trends, songs, sound bites, voice
memes, a video mixed and written as if for a silent feed leaves real
distribution potential unclaimed.

Example: opening on a recognizable trending sound's exact drop point, timed
frame-accurate, before any visual punchline lands.

Counter-example: a near-silent ambient bed with all meaning carried by a text
card, functionally identical whether sound is on or off, wasting the
platform's sound-on advantage entirely.
