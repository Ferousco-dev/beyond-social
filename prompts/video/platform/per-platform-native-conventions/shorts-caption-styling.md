Shorts inherits YouTube's search-and-read culture, so captions can carry more
informational density at a steadier pace than TikTok's word-flash style,
provided they clear the platform's own UI.

- Burn in custom-styled captions rather than relying on YouTube's default gray
  auto-caption bar; a designed caption reads better and holds attention longer.
- Keep text clear of the bottom ~20% of frame, where the channel handle,
  subscribe button, and progress scrubber sit.
- Let a full short sentence hold for 1.5-2 seconds rather than flashing
  word-by-word; Shorts viewers, arriving from a search or informational
  context, read more per beat than TikTok scrollers do.
- Choose a font weight legible at small size on a paused frame; Shorts get
  grabbed as static thumbnails in search and related-video rails more than
  TikTok clips do.
- Skip the rainbow single-word-highlight habit; against YouTube's more neutral
  visual norms it reads as try-hard rather than native.

Why: Shorts sits inside YouTube's search-driven, informational viewing habit
rather than TikTok's entertainment-scroll habit, so captions built like a
headline or lower-third chyron out-perform captions optimized purely for
kinetic energy.

Example: a single steady sentence, "the pan was too hot, here's the fix,"
sitting in the upper-middle third, clear of the subscribe button.

Counter-example: word-by-word flashing captions crammed into the bottom third,
where YouTube's own subscribe button and scrubber overlap and clip half the
text.
