YouTube Shorts inherits YouTube's search index and related-video surfacing,
which gives it a materially longer discovery shelf life than TikTok or Reels'
feed-driven, largely trend-window-bound distribution. Content built around a
durable, searchable topic keeps earning views for months where equivalent
TikTok content mostly dies with its trend cycle.

- Anchor the title, opening line, and captions to a specific searchable phrase
  a person would actually type ("how to fix a squeaky door hinge"), not a
  trend reference that ages out in days.
- Avoid leaning on trending audio or a meme format as the core hook; those
  work on TikTok's feed but do not help Shorts' search-driven long tail and
  can visibly date the content.
- Design the payoff as genuinely reusable information, a fix, a comparison, a
  clear answer, since search-driven viewers arrive with intent rather than for
  entertainment-scroll novelty.
- Keep on-screen text keyword-aligned with the spoken hook, since YouTube's
  systems read captions and transcripts for topical relevance.

Why: TikTok and Reels distribution is dominated by a real-time interest graph
that decays fast once a trend cools, while Shorts sits inside YouTube's search
and recommendation graph, which keeps surfacing evergreen, specific,
well-titled content long after a comparable TikTok would have stopped getting
pushed.

Example: a Short titled and opened around "why your sourdough is too dense,"
still earning search traffic a year after posting.

Counter-example: a Short built entirely around a two-week-old audio trend with
no topical hook; it stops earning views the moment the trend fades and has
nothing evergreen to fall back on.
