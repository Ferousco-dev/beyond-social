Facebook's video feed, including Facebook Reels, skews older and defaults to
silent, smaller-frame autoplay even more strictly than Instagram, and its
audience responds better to directly stated value than to trend-chasing or
in-jokes calibrated for a younger TikTok audience.

- Front-load the concrete value or claim as burned-in text in the first frame,
  not implied through a visual hook alone; Facebook's autoplay is smaller and
  easier to scroll past unread than TikTok's full-screen sound-on default.
- Write captions as complete, plain sentences rather than TikTok's
  fragment-and-emphasis style; this audience responds better to direct
  clarity than youth-coded internet shorthand.
- Avoid assuming familiarity with a TikTok trend, sound, or meme format;
  content built around a trend reference lands as confusing rather than
  native on Facebook.
- Keep the pace closer to Shorts' informational rhythm than TikTok's raw
  jump-cut speed; a slower, clearer edit outperforms high-frequency cutting
  with this audience.

Why: Facebook's viewer base and default playback context, a smaller in-feed
frame, silent by default, often older and less trend-immersed, reward
directness and clarity over the kinetic, in-joke-fluent style that wins on
TikTok, so a straight port of a TikTok edit tends to underperform on Facebook
even with identical subject matter.

Example: opening burned-in text reading "3 signs your AC is about to fail" in
full plain sentences, held two full seconds before any cut.

Counter-example: a TikTok edit with fragment captions, a trending-sound
reference, and rapid meme-paced cuts posted unchanged to Facebook; the
audience finds it illegible rather than energetic.
