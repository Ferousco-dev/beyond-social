Instagram Reels persist as a static cover image on the profile grid, unlike
TikTok's grid, which mostly reuses the video's own frame without the same
curatorial expectation. The chosen cover functions like a thumbnail and needs
to be composed and legible as a still, distinct from the video's live content.

- Choose or design a specific cover frame with clear negative space for the
  caption text Instagram overlays automatically; a busy full-bleed frame gets
  its subject obscured by that overlay.
- Favor a frame with one legible subject and a clear expression or gesture
  that reads at grid-thumbnail size, since most discovery of the cover
  happens on a few hundred pixels in a three-column grid.
- Keep the cover visually consistent with the account's grid palette, so
  profile cohesion holds even though it is a Reel rather than a static post.
- Use a custom cover rather than the auto-selected first frame where the
  platform allows it; the auto-pick often lands mid-motion and reads worse
  than a deliberately composed still.

Why: because the Reels cover doubles as permanent grid real estate on the
profile, unlike TikTok's transient, algorithm-fed browsing, it carries a
curatorial job that a normal in-feed video frame does not have. Treating it
as an afterthought undermines the profile's cohesion even when the Reel
itself performs well.

Example: a custom cover shot of the finished product, centered with clear
headroom, matching the grid's warm-neutral palette.

Counter-example: leaving the auto-selected cover, which lands mid-blink on a
transitional frame with the subject half out of shot.
