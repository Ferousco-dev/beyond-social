Shorts pacing sits between TikTok's raw speed and long-form YouTube's patience.
The platform rewards a clear problem-action-result structure with cuts that
serve clarity over kinetic energy, because much of its audience arrives via
search or a related-video rail expecting to resolve something specific.

- Open with the specific claim or question in the first 1-2 seconds, spoken
  and captioned, so a search-driven viewer confirms this is the right video
  before investing attention.
- Hold demonstration shots 2-4 seconds, long enough to actually see the
  mechanism or result, rather than TikTok's flash-cut pace.
- Structure in three visible beats, problem, action, result, each roughly a
  third of the runtime, mirroring how a longer YouTube video's chapters break
  down.
- Restate the key point as on-screen text at the payoff; a large share of
  Shorts traffic arrives via a muted autoplay swipe on the homepage.

Why: Shorts inherits YouTube's underlying audience behavior of searching for
and evaluating information, so a structure that clearly answers "does this
solve my problem" out-retains a structure optimized purely for scroll-stopping
novelty.

Example: "does this actually remove pet hair?" spoken and captioned in second
one, followed by a clean three-second demo, then the result held for a beat.

Counter-example: seven rapid-fire quarter-second cuts with no single shot
proving the claim; a Shorts viewer bounces because nothing ever confirmed the
premise.
