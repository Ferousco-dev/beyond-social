Instagram's Reels ranking weights sends to DM and saves more heavily relative
to raw watch-time than TikTok's model does, so content built to be sent to one
specific person, not just watched, distributes further on Instagram.

- Build in a genuine "tag a friend who..." beat, a relatable moment the viewer
  recognizes someone specific in, rather than a caption-only gimmick.
- Favor a nameable niche, a specific relationship, hobby, or in-group, over
  broad appeal; specific content is what gets sent to one particular person,
  generic content just gets watched and scrolled past.
- Include one save-worthy element, a recipe step, a checklist frame,
  something the viewer would reference again later, since saves are a
  distinct ranking signal from watch-time.
- End on the shareable beat itself rather than past it; if the punchline lands
  two seconds before the end, trim to it so the send happens at peak impact.

Why: Instagram's graph is built around existing relationships, DMs, close
friends, tagged accounts, and its Reels ranking reflects that by rewarding
content that moves through those private channels, not only content that
holds attention in the public feed.

Example: a Reel built around an oddly specific roommate argument, captioned to
invite a tag, engineered so the exact scenario is the reason to send it to one
person.

Counter-example: a broadly generic "motivational" Reel with no specific enough
scenario for anyone to think of a particular friend to send it to.
