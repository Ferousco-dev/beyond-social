Reels captions read as designed, not app-default: a steady line held for the
sentence, in typography consistent with the account, rather than TikTok's
word-flashing chaos.

- Hold one clean line (two at most) per caption card for the length of the
  sentence; do not flash word by word.
- Keep typography consistent with the account's grid aesthetic: the same
  weight and color family across posts, so captions read as branded.
- Place captions in the lower third or centered with generous margin; avoid the
  cramped, stacked look of dense TikTok text.
- Animate softly: a simple fade or slide-up, not a hard pop-and-scale.
- Limit color to one or two brand tones; skip the rainbow single-word
  emphasis habit that reads as native on TikTok.

Why: Instagram's broader culture (curated grids, cohesive brand feeds) primes
viewers to expect polish and consistency across a profile. Captions that mimic
TikTok's raw karaoke energy read as an unedited repost rather than something
made for Instagram, which undercuts the "considered" positioning that performs
better in-feed and on the profile grid where the Reel also lives.

Example: a single centered line, "3 things nobody tells you about retinol,"
fading in and holding for the full sentence, set in the account's consistent
sans typeface.

Counter-example: porting a TikTok edit's word-by-word yellow-highlight captions
directly into a Reel. It reads as a cross-post rather than native Reels content
and breaks the visual consistency of the surrounding grid.
