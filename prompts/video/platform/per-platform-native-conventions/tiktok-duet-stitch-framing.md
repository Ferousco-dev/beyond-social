TikTok's duet and stitch formats are native remix mechanics with no real
equivalent in Reels or Shorts distribution weight. Content meant to be
duet- or stitch-friendly needs deliberate visual and audio space left for the
reaction format to actually work.

- Frame the subject in roughly the left or center two-thirds of the shot for
  duet's side-by-side layout, rather than filling the entire frame edge to
  edge.
- End on a clear, quotable statement or a visibly reaction-worthy moment
  within the first five seconds specifically, since stitch only pulls that
  opening window into a new video.
- Leave a half-second of audio silence or a natural pause after a punchline so
  a duet reaction has clean room to land without talking over the original.
- Avoid dense on-screen text across the full frame width; duet needs roughly
  half the horizontal space visually clear for the reactor's own feed.

Why: duet and stitch are TikTok-specific distribution multipliers, every
reaction video is a new piece of content pointing back at the original, so a
video that leaves no visual or audio room for that remix format forfeits a
growth channel that has no counterpart on Reels or Shorts.

Example: a five-second opinion clip ending on a clean beat of silence after
the statement, framed in the left two-thirds of the shot.

Counter-example: full-bleed footage with dense captions covering the whole
frame and a punchline buried at second eight, unusable for either duet or
stitch.
