TikTok's native cut rate runs faster than Reels or Shorts, with top-performing
opens often cutting under every 1.5 seconds, and cuts land mid-action rather
than after it resolves.

- Cut every 0.5-1.5 seconds for the first 3-5 seconds to match the scroll speed
  viewers arrive with.
- Cut on action or on an audio beat, never on a static hold; a TikTok cut lands
  mid-gesture, not after the gesture completes.
- Vary shot length after the hook: alternate a 1-second cutaway with a longer
  3-4 second breather shot so pacing has a pulse rather than a flat metronome.
- Let jump cuts show in talking-head footage; on TikTok a visible jump cut
  reads as raw, unproduced, and native rather than as a mistake.

Why: TikTok's own creation tools (in-app auto-cut, the native camera) trained
the audience's eye on rapid, slightly rough cutting. Content edited at
film or TVC pacing, with an average shot length of two to four seconds, reads
as an advertisement from the first frame and gets swiped before the message
lands.

Example: cut on every beat of the opening line, landing the payoff word on the
fourth shot, all inside three seconds.

Counter-example: a single four-second static wide shot to open, held for
"establishing" purposes. On TikTok this reads as dead air and the viewer is
gone before the story starts.
