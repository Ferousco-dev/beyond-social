The same idea should be tuned per platform; audiences and norms differ.

- TikTok: native, authentic, trend-aware. Fast hook, personality, on-trend audio,
  captions. Overly polished ad-like content underperforms; "made for the platform"
  wins. 9:16, roughly 15-34s is a sweet spot.
- Instagram Reels: more aesthetic and aspirational than TikTok; strong visuals and
  clean captions. Reuse TikTok cuts but lift the polish. 9:16.
- YouTube Shorts: often more informational or satisfying; strong hook and a clear
  payoff, tends to reward slightly cleaner structure. 9:16.
- YouTube long-form (16:9): title and thumbnail do the hooking; the video can
  breathe, go deeper, and hold longer shots.
- Facebook: older-skewing, silent autoplay; captions essential, clear value early.

Cross-posting rule: keep the master idea, but re-cut the hook, re-caption, and
match the audio norm per platform. Never post the same file with a competitor's
watermark on it; platforms suppress it and it reads as recycled.

Why: distribution rewards native-feeling content; a platform-tuned cut of the same
footage consistently outperforms a single generic upload sprayed everywhere.

Example: one shoot, three edits: trend-audio TikTok, polished Reel, informative
Short. Counter-example: exporting one 16:9 ad and posting it identically everywhere.
