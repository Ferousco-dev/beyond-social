Point-of-view handheld puts the viewer inside a body, so the shake must match
that operator's literal action, unboxing with their own hands, walking while
filming, rather than a generic external observer's shake.

- Frame at a believable eye or chest height for the implied camera holder, a
  phone at arm's length for a selfie POV, lower and wider for a chest-mounted
  angle.
- Let the subject's own hands enter frame doing the action; a hands-on POV shot
  where the operator's hands never appear reads as detached, not first-person.
- Correlate shake with implied exertion: rummaging in a bag produces faster,
  tighter jitter; walking while glancing at a phone produces a slower bob with
  occasional downward tilt.
- Avoid impossible POV geometry, such as a first-person shot that also shows
  the "operator's" full face, which breaks the subjective conceit outright.

Why: POV only works as an authenticity device when every motion cue is
consistent with one specific embodied viewpoint. Any shot where the movement
doesn't match what that body would actually be doing collapses the illusion
back into "camera on a rig."

Example: "first-person POV, hands opening the box in frame, phone held at chest
height, a natural downward glance."
Counter-example: a "POV" shot floating at head height with no hands ever
visible during an unboxing — reads as a drone, not a person.
