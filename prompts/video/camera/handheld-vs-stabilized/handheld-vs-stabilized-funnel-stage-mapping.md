The handheld-versus-stabilized decision should follow the funnel stage a shot
serves, not a single house style. A top-of-funnel hook earns attention by
looking like a peer's phone video, while a bottom-of-funnel conversion shot
earns trust by looking like a controlled demonstration.

- Hook or scroll-stop, first one to three seconds, cold audience: handheld,
  phone-native framing, a mid-action start; it has to look like organic feed
  content or the thumb keeps scrolling.
- Consideration, feature explanation or comparison: can mix, handheld for a
  presenter talking through it, stabilized inserts for the feature itself.
- Conversion, price, call to action, final beauty shot: stabilized or locked,
  because this is the moment the product's own quality has to carry the message
  without a shaky camera undercutting it.
- Sequence this progression within a single ad, not just across a campaign: a
  spot can legitimately start handheld and end locked, but shouldn't run the
  reverse, since polish that degrades into shake reads as dropping production
  value rather than a deliberate style choice.

Why: viewers unconsciously read camera stability as a proxy for how much a
piece of content was made for them versus made to sell to them, so sequencing
from handheld to stabilized mirrors the natural arc from grabbing attention as
a peer to closing as a credible source.

Example: "opens handheld mid-action for the hook, ends on a locked-off product
beauty shot for the price card."
Counter-example: a spot that opens on a polished locked product shot then cuts
to shaky handheld for the call to action — reverses the trust arc and makes the
close feel less credible, not more real.
