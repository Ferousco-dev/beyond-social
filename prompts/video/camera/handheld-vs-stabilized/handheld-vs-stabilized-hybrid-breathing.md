A stabilized move with a small handheld "breathing" residual layered on top
avoids both the seasick extreme and the sterile, over-locked look that reads as
computer-generated.

- Start from a gimbal-glide or slider base move: a clean line with low vertical
  bob.
- Add a sub-degree, low-frequency roll and pitch oscillation, roughly one cycle
  every two to four seconds, well below true handheld amplitude.
- Never add breathing to a hard product-label hold; reserve it for atmosphere,
  establishing, or b-roll moves where "alive but controlled" is the target.
- Describe the amplitude in words the model can act on, "barely perceptible,"
  "just enough to feel human," rather than numeric jitter values it can't parse.
- Apply breathing to the base move only, not to any secondary element already
  moving in frame (a swinging sign, a spinning fan); stacking residual sway onto
  something that is already animated doubles the noise and reads as unstable
  footage rather than a subtly human camera.

Why: fully locked, mathematically perfect movement is a recognizable synthetic-
video signature, because even real stabilizers, gimbals and Steadicams
included, never fully cancel a body's low-frequency sway. A trace of that sway
is what makes a smooth shot read as operated rather than rendered.

Example: "gimbal glide down the hallway, a faint human breathing sway, nothing
you'd consciously notice."
Counter-example: "perfectly stable, mathematically smooth camera move" — asking
for zero residual is exactly what produces the flat glide viewers now flag as
"AI video."
