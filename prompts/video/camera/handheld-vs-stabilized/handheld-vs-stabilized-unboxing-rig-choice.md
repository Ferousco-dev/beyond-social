Unboxing and premium tabletop content, beauty, tech, jewelry, reads as premium
through mechanical camera smoothness; the hands-in-frame motion of the unboxing
itself is what supplies the human warmth, so the camera should stay locked or
on a controlled slider while the hands do the "handheld" work.

- Camera: locked-off or a slow motorized slider or tabletop dolly, never
  handheld, because the product's own materials, matte box, foil seal, glass,
  need a stable frame to read as high quality on screen.
- Let the hands provide the human, slightly imperfect motion, a natural fumble
  with a seal, a small hesitation opening a lid, that's where "real person"
  comes through, not camera shake.
- Overhead flat-lay unboxing shots specifically require a locked or slow-
  rotating rig; any handheld wobble on a top-down shot is disproportionately
  visible because there is no horizon line to anchor against.
- Reserve one handheld cutaway, the person's reaction after opening, to
  reintroduce the personal register without compromising the product shots.

Why: in unboxing content the authenticity job and the premium job are done by
two different elements, the hands carry authenticity, the camera carries
premium, so collapsing them into one handheld shot for the whole sequence
undersells the product's own craft.

Example: "locked overhead tabletop shot, her hands slowly peeling the seal,
product surfaces sharp and steady throughout."
Counter-example: "handheld unboxing, camera shaking while she opens the box" —
the shake competes with the product's materials for attention and reads as
amateur, not authentic.
