Product hero shots, packshots, and any beat where the viewer must read a label or
a price, should be dead-locked or riding a controlled mechanical move, because
any residual shake reads as a defect in the object, not in the camera.

- Name the rig explicitly: "locked-off tripod," "motorized slider," "turntable
  rotation." Never say "handheld" for a hero beat.
- Reserve movement for the reveal itself (a slow slider push, a controlled orbit)
  and hold fully still during the beat the viewer needs to actually read.
- Keep the product's straight edges parallel to the frame edges; even a one or
  two degree cant on a rigid manufactured object reads as sloppy, not stylish.
- Hold focus steady through the shot; no focus hunting while the label is on
  screen.

Why: the eye judges wobble against what it's attached to. A wobbling human body
still reads as alive and present, but a wobbling rigid product reads as broken
camera work, because rigid objects have no reason to move on their own. For
categories that sell on precision (tech, jewelry, cosmetics), that wobble
directly undercuts the claim the product itself is making.

Example: "locked-off tripod, macro lens, product centered, zero handheld motion,
label fully legible and sharp for two full seconds."
Counter-example: "handheld shot of the phone sitting on the table" — the shake
has no visual justification and reads as amateur footage of a premium object.
