Composition, not just motion, carries an authenticity signal. A frame that sits
slightly off from textbook-perfect, headroom a touch generous, subject a few
percent off-center, reads as operated by a person making a quick judgment call,
while a mathematically balanced frame reads as templated.

- Offset the subject a small amount from dead-center, rough rule-of-thirds
  rather than exact placement, for UGC and testimonial contexts.
- Allow slightly excess or slightly tight headroom rather than the textbook
  ideal; real operators eyeball a frame, they don't measure it.
- For premium or product contexts, invert this instruction: use closer-to-exact
  centering and measured headroom, because precision is the signal there.
- Never combine "imperfect framing" with "professionally composed" in the same
  prompt; the two instructions fight and the model tends to resolve toward the
  safer, centered default.

Why: viewers read framing precision as a proxy for production intent the same
way they read camera stability. Slightly imperfect framing implies a person
pointed this quickly, which is exactly the trust signal UGC and testimonial
content needs, and exactly the wrong signal for a hero product shot.

Example: "casual framing, subject slightly off-center, a little extra headroom,
like a friend just pointed the phone."
Counter-example: "perfectly centered, rule-of-thirds precise" applied to a
testimonial — reads as a template, not a person filming.
