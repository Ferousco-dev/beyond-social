The choice between handheld and tripod for a talking-head shot is a genre
signal: tripod-locked reads as corporate or interview authority, handheld reads
as peer testimonial.

- Corporate explainer, executive message, product demo narration: tripod-locked
  or gimbal-smooth, eye-level; the message's authority depends on visual
  stability matching its claimed source.
- UGC testimonial or "why I switched to X" review: handheld selfie-style, slight
  arm-fatigue drift, held at arm's length or propped imperfectly, mimicking how
  an actual customer would film it.
- Never lock off a shot whose copy claims to be organic; matched-cut precision
  paired with "I just wanted to share..." creates a credibility mismatch a
  viewer feels immediately, even if they can't name it.
- Match production polish to the claimed context; a "quick phone review" shot
  with cinematic color grading is as much a tell as a tripod would be.

Why: viewers use camera stability as a fast, subconscious proxy for who is
holding the camera and why. Mismatching the rig to the claimed context, brand-
produced polish on a supposedly organic testimonial, breaks the frame before a
single word of copy lands.

Example: "handheld, selfie-angle, arm's-length framing, slight drift, natural
room light, talking directly to camera."
Counter-example: tripod-locked, studio-lit "UGC" testimonial with perfectly
symmetric framing — reads as an ad wearing a testimonial costume.
