Handheld shake should trace back to an identifiable physical cause, a footstep,
a breath, a turn of the torso, because that causal link is exactly what a
viewer's motion-perception system checks for, even without naming it.

- Tie vertical bob to footstep cadence when the operator is implied walking,
  roughly one dip per stride, correlated with the implied foot strike.
- Tie roll and pitch drift to a resting breathing rate for a stationary handheld
  hold, a noticeably slower cycle than the walking bob.
- If the operator turns to follow a subject, add a slight overshoot on the pan
  that settles back, matching how a wrist or shoulder rotates past its target.
- Never stack two causes in one shot: don't combine walking bob with static
  breathing drift, it reads as two unrelated noise sources fighting each other.

Why: real camera shake is a byproduct of a body doing something else, so it has
one dominant frequency and a plausible origin. Noise with no discernible period,
or that changes character with nothing on screen to trigger it, is what most
synthetic handheld shake looks like, and audiences flag it as wrong before they
can articulate why.

Example: "handheld follow shot, bob synced to her walking pace, camera catches
up with a small overshoot each time she turns a corner."
Counter-example: "constant random handheld jitter throughout the shot" — no
correlation to any implied motion, so the shake reads as an effect, not a body.
