The slow, ambient gimbal float or orbit with no narrative reason has become its
own AI-video tell, precisely because it is the model's easiest, lowest-artifact
default. Overusing it flags the footage as synthetic even though each shot
looks technically clean.

- Don't default to a slow orbit or push on every shot; vary the movement choice
  per shot the way a real edit does, some locked, some handheld, some dolly.
- If a slow float genuinely fits a beat, ground it with a stated cause, a
  character walking past, a hand entering frame, rather than leaving it as
  ambient, unexplained drift.
- Cut with intention: five consecutive gentle push-ins in a row reads as a
  generated reel, even when each shot is individually well composed.
- Include at least one static or handheld shot per sequence to break the
  rhythm audiences have learned to recognize.

Why: audiences have absorbed enough generative video to recognize the specific
rhythm of unmotivated slow-float camera work as a pattern, independent of any
single shot's visual quality. Breaking that pattern is now a realism lever in
its own right.

Example: "sequence: locked-off shot, then a handheld follow, then a controlled
slider move, one rig choice per beat."
Counter-example: "every shot a slow gentle push-in or orbit" — individually
clean, collectively an unmistakable generated-reel signature.
