Movement is where video earns its existence over a photo. Name the move precisely;
each has a distinct feel, and models respond to the correct term.

- Push-in / dolly-in: camera moves toward subject. Builds focus and tension.
- Pull-out / dolly-out: reveals context; often an ending beat.
- Pan (left/right) and tilt (up/down): camera pivots in place. Scans a scene.
- Truck / track: camera moves laterally with a subject. Follows action.
- Pedestal / crane: camera rises or lowers. Grand reveals.
- Orbit / arc: camera circles the subject. Showcases a product in the round.
- Handheld: subtle shake, human, urgent. The UGC and documentary staple.
- Static / locked-off: no movement. Lets the subject's own action carry the shot.

Keep it to one move per short shot, and keep it slow unless energy is the goal;
compound or fast moves are where generation artifacts appear. "Slow" and "subtle"
are your safest, most premium-looking modifiers.

Why: "cinematic" is vague, but "slow dolly-in" is a directable instruction the
model can execute cleanly, and it is a large part of what separates directed
footage from a moving snapshot.

Example: "Slow orbit around the perfume bottle, locked height, soft studio light."
Counter-example: "dynamic camera, lots of movement, exciting angles."
