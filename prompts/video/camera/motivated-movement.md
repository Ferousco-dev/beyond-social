Every camera move should have a reason: follow an action, reveal information, or
shift emotion. Movement for its own sake feels restless and, in generated video,
multiplies artifacts. When in doubt, hold the shot and let the subject move.

Guidelines:

- Motivate it: push in as a realization lands; pan because the subject walks; pull
  out to reveal where they are. The move answers a question the viewer is forming.
- Match speed to emotion: slow for calm and premium, faster for energy and
  urgency. Speed is a storytelling choice, not a default.
- One idea per shot: a single, legible move reads; a pan-plus-zoom-plus-orbit
  reads as chaos and warps geometry under generation.
- Stillness is powerful: a locked-off frame with strong internal action (steam,
  hands, expression) often outperforms any camera move.

Why: motivated, restrained movement is a hallmark of professional work and, for
generated video specifically, the single biggest lever on clean, artifact-free
output.

Example: "Locked-off macro of coffee being poured; the only motion is the pour and
rising steam." Counter-example: "spinning, zooming, shaking camera around the cup
for excitement."
