In ad creative, an aerial establishing shot only earns its place in the cut if it
answers a question the viewer would otherwise ask, "where is this," rather than
existing to open with an impressive-looking shot.

- Use an aerial establishing shot when the location itself is part of the claim: a
  farm-to-table restaurant showing the actual farm, a resort ad showing the actual
  property.
- Skip it when the product could be anywhere; a phone app or a packaged good gains
  nothing from a drone shot of a generic street it has no connection to.
- Keep the establishing aerial under three seconds inside a :15 or :30 ad. It is
  context for what follows, not the hook itself.
- Cut from the aerial shot directly into the specific, human-scale shot it was
  establishing, without an unrelated shot inserted between them.

Why: every second spent on an aerial shot in a short ad is a second not spent on the
product or the claim, so the aerial has to be doing load-bearing work: proving the
place is real, proving the scale is real, or setting up the exact shot that follows
it. An aerial that doesn't connect to anything specific downstream reads as filler
even when the footage itself is well executed.

Example: real estate ad, "aerial rise over the coastal bluff establishing the
property line, cut directly to the front door opening on the same property."

Counter-example: a phone-app ad opening with a drone shot of a random city skyline
that never reappears or connects to anything else in the ad. It spends the ad's first
three seconds on a location the product has no relationship to.
