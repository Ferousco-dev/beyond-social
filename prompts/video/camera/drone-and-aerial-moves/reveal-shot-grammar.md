A rise-and-reveal shot works by withholding the wide context and then releasing it on
a single, deliberate beat. The move fails the moment it tries to do more than one job
at once.

- Start low and close enough that an obstruction (a ridge, a wall, foreground trees)
  genuinely hides the wider scene, not just partially screens it.
- Rise or push forward at one steady speed. Do not rise, orbit, and zoom in the same
  motion; each added axis dilutes the reveal.
- Time the moment the obstruction clears to land on a beat: a music hit, a VO line, a
  product name, not an arbitrary duration.
- Hold the full wide shot for 1.5-3 seconds after the reveal before cutting. Cutting
  the instant it clears wastes the payoff.

Why: a reveal is an information-asymmetry trick, the audience knows less than the
frame is about to show them, and the payoff is proportional to how cleanly that gap
closes. Multiple simultaneous camera moves blur the moment of disclosure into a vague
sense of motion instead of a single legible "now you see it," and cutting away too
fast denies the audience the beat they were just promised.

Example: "start below tree line beside the cabin porch, ascend steadily to 40 feet as
the trees fall away, revealing the lake and mountains beyond, hold wide for 2 seconds
before cut."

Counter-example: "rise, orbit, and zoom in the same motion while accelerating past the
treeline." This reads as generic drone-reel motion rather than a reveal, because the
audience never gets a clean before/after moment to register.
