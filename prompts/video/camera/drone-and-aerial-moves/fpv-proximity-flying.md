FPV-style proximity flying, diving through gaps and hugging a subject, reads as
spectacle specifically because of the near-miss risk it implies. That risk has to be
visible in the frame or the shot is just a fast, generic move.

- Fly close enough that foreground elements (a doorway frame, tree branches, cables)
  pass within a few feet of the lens, briefly filling the frame edges.
- Keep some roll or bank into turns rather than a flat, gimbal-stabilized turn.
  Real FPV craft genuinely bank into curves the way a plane or a bike does.
- Vary speed within the shot: a beat of near-hover proximity followed by a hard
  acceleration reads as piloted, not as a preset flight path.
- Reserve this move for a single hero beat rather than a default aerial style; it's
  the most attention-grabbing move available and burns out fast with repetition.

Why: the sense of risk in FPV footage comes from legible near-misses, close objects
that appear to threaten the lens and then clear it, combined with visible banking that
shows the craft's own inertia fighting the turn. A path that stays centered and level
through every gap removes the risk cue even if the speed and proximity numbers are
identical, and the shot collapses into a rendered fly-through.

Example: "dive through the barn's open loft door, bank hard right around the support
beam, accelerate down the row of hay bales."

Counter-example: "fly FPV-fast but keep the frame level and the path perfectly
centered through every gap." The numbers say fast and close, but a level, centered
path reads like a rendered corridor fly-through rather than a piloted risk.
