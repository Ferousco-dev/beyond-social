A compound move changes rig type mid-shot, most commonly a crane descent
that lands and hands off into a dolly or gimbal move at ground level, used
for a scene's single opening shot to establish place and then arrive with a
character.

- Plan the handoff as a specific frame: let the vertical travel fully
  resolve, reaching eye level and decelerating to near-zero, before the
  horizontal travel visibly begins, so the two motions don't overlap and
  fight each other.
- Thread one consistent point of interest through both halves of the move;
  the eye needs something to track across the handoff or the shot reads as
  two shots pretending to be one.
- Reserve compound moves for a scene's single most important shot, a cold
  open or hero reveal — using them routinely exhausts their impact.
- Describe the sequencing explicitly rather than naming both moves at once.

Why: the handoff is the hardest part to sell because real rigs require a
literal equipment or operator changeover at that point, a crane arm giving
way to a dolly track, or a stabilizer operator stepping in. Spelling out
resolve-then-begin sequencing is what keeps a generative model from blending
the two motions into one mushy diagonal drift instead of two legible moves.

Example: "crane descends from a wide rooftop view, decelerating to a stop at
street level directly behind him, then the camera begins a lateral dolly to
follow him into the shop."

Counter-example: describing "crane down into a tracking shot" with no
sequencing detail — the model blends both motions into a single continuous
diagonal glide that reads as neither a crane nor a dolly, just an unplaceable
float.
