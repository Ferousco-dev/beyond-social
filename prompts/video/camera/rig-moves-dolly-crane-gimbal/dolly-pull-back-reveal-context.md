A dolly pull-back widens the frame by moving the camera away from a subject
the viewer has already committed to, answering "where are we" or "who else is
here" after a tight opening.

- Start tight on a detail or face before the pull begins; the reveal only
  lands if the viewer was already anchored to something small.
- Keep the horizon and eyeline level through the move unless deliberate
  destabilization is the point.
- A slow continuous pull reads as isolation, the subject visibly shrinking in
  frame; a snap pull-back with a hard stop lands a shock or comedic beat.
- Make sure the wider frame contains genuinely new information: another
  person, a hazard, scale the viewer couldn't have guessed.

Why: the move spends the emotional investment the viewer already made on the
close subject. Recontextualizing something the audience already feels
something about is a stronger beat than showing the same wide information
from the start, because the pull-back forces a re-read of what came before.

Example: "start tight on his hands trembling on the steering wheel, dolly
pulls back slowly to reveal the car surrounded by flashing lights."

Counter-example: pulling back to reveal the same empty room with nothing new
in it — the move spends its narrative currency for no return and reads as
aimless camera drift.
