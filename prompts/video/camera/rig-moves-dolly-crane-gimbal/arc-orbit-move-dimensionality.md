An arc or orbit travels the camera in a curve around a subject, revealing
depth and the side or back planes a static frame can't show, while a dolly
trades subject scale for context instead.

- Partial arcs of 30-90 degrees read as a considered reveal; a full 360-degree
  orbit is a self-conscious hero-shot move, reserved for product turntables
  or a single climactic character beat.
- Hold radius and frame size constant throughout; a wobbling radius reads as
  the operator losing the mark, not a stylistic choice.
- Vary the sweep speed to the content: near-static during a contemplative
  beat, a faster sweep with a hard landing for a reveal.
- Land the arc on a specific angle (three-quarter, profile) rather than
  letting it drift past the intended stopping point.

Why: orbiting is the only move that changes what's visible behind the
subject without changing the camera's distance to them, so it delivers
environmental context while holding subject scale constant, a distinct
read from a dolly, which trades scale for context instead.

Example: "camera arcs 120 degrees around the product on a turntable, radius
and frame size held constant, easing into a three-quarter angle at the end."

Counter-example: a full 360-degree orbit around a person mid-conversation —
nothing in the scene motivates a complete revolution, so it reads as a
demo-reel flourish rather than storytelling.
