A dolly push-in physically moves the camera toward the subject, and that
physical closing of distance reads as growing intimacy or mounting tension in
a way cropping tighter never does.

- Speed: slow and continuous, nearly imperceptible frame to frame; compare the
  first and last frame to check it's actually moving.
- Trigger it on a specific beat: a reaction, a decision, a line landing, not
  an arbitrary mid-shot moment.
- Let the background soften or fall out of focus as the subject fills more of
  the frame, isolating them from their surroundings.
- Reserve it for one beat per scene; a push-in on everything cancels its own
  weight.
- Skip it entirely on shots with no emotional or narrative turn to earn it.

Why: a push-in changes parallax, the shifting relationship between foreground
and background as the camera physically travels, which the eye reads as real
spatial closeness. A zoom only changes magnification and flattens that cue,
which is why a push-in feels like the camera caring about the subject and a
zoom feels like the camera inspecting them.

Example: "slow, continuous dolly push-in on her face over 4 seconds as she
reads the text, background rack-softens, ending on a tight close-up with her
eyes in the upper third."

Counter-example: pushing in fast on an empty desk with no story reason —
without a motivating beat it reads as generic "cinematic" filler, the exact
tell of unmotivated AI camera movement.
