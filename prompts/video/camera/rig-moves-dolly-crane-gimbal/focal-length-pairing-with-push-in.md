The same dolly push-in feels different depending on the paired focal length,
because lens choice determines how much the background changes relative to
the subject as the camera closes distance.

- Wide lens, roughly 24-35mm equivalent: background visibly moves and warps
  as distance closes, producing an immersive, "walking into the moment"
  feel, good for POV-adjacent intimacy.
- Long lens, 85mm equivalent or more, over a short physical travel: the
  background stays compressed and nearly static while the subject's frame
  size grows, producing a more voyeuristic, isolating pull rather than a
  walk-toward feeling.
- State the move and the lens together; naming only "push in" leaves the
  model to default to a mid-range focal length that flattens the intended
  feeling.
- Pick wide for approach and arrival, long for surveillance, distance, or
  dread that closes in without the camera seeming to travel.

Why: perspective compression from focal length and the parallax created by
physical camera travel are two independent variables. Locking one while
varying the other is what actually produces the range of push-in feelings
seen across real cinematography, from an ominous wide push-in to a
compressed long-lens one.

Example: "wide-lens dolly push-in, background hallway visibly warping and
passing at the edges as the camera closes on him."

Counter-example: specifying only "slow push-in" with no lens character — the
result defaults to a generic mid-focal-length look that reads as a stock
cinematic trope rather than a considered choice.
