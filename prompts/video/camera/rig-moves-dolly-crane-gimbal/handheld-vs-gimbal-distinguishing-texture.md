Handheld and gimbal-stabilized footage carry mechanically different motion
signatures, and asking for one while describing the other's physics produces
neither — it lands in an uncanny middle that reads as synthetic.

- True handheld: irregular high-frequency jitter, occasional overcorrections,
  a breathing-rate vertical drift roughly every 3-4 seconds, a frame that
  never fully settles.
- Gimbal: high-frequency jitter is removed but low-frequency movement
  remains, a gentle sway or step-synced bob, with reframing that's smooth
  but not instant when the subject changes direction.
- Choose handheld for urgency, chaos, or documentary and UGC authenticity;
  choose gimbal for polish that still reads as human-operated, like product
  walk-throughs or interviews on the move.
- Name the rig explicitly in the prompt and describe its specific failure
  mode, not just the word "shaky" or "smooth."

Why: each rig is mechanically incapable of certain motions and only capable
of others, so its footage carries a specific fingerprint. Viewers read that
fingerprint, even without naming it, as evidence of what physically carried
the camera.

Example: "handheld, visible jitter and a slight overcorrection as she turns
to face camera, frame never fully locks off."

Counter-example: prompting "handheld" but describing buttery-smooth,
perfectly centered motion — the words and the physics contradict each other,
and the result reads as neither style, just generically synthetic.
