Gimbal-stabilized movement follows a subject through space at walking pace
with a residual soft bob, distinct from both a locked dolly track and true
shaky handheld — its specific signature is what sells "a person carrying a
rig" rather than a render.

- Use it for a subject moving through an environment: walk-and-talk,
  unboxing, a kitchen or storefront tour, weaving around foreground
  obstacles.
- Let the frame drift a few percent off the subject's center as the operator
  navigates rather than locking them dead-center every frame.
- Keep a step-synced vertical bob, roughly one small cycle per stride, and a
  slight lag when the subject changes direction before the frame catches up.
- Match camera speed to the subject's actual walking pace; the camera should
  never appear to set the tempo.

Why: real gimbal footage carries small mechanical signatures — the bob, the
lag on turns, the imperfect centering — that come from a person physically
carrying and correcting the rig. Those signatures are what a viewer's eye
uses, often without naming it, to read the shot as operator-carried rather
than computer-generated.

Example: "gimbal follows her from behind at walking pace through the market
stalls, slight bob on each step, weaving around a produce cart in the
foreground."

Counter-example: a gimbal shot with zero bob and mathematically perfect
subject-centering — the absence of any operator signature is itself a tell
that the footage is synthetic.
