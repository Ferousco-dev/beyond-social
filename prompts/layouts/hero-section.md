A hero earns the scroll in about five seconds, so it carries exactly one idea. Its
job is to answer "what is this, who is it for, and what do I do next" without the
reader working for it.

Anatomy that works: a specific headline (the outcome, not the category), a single
clarifying subhead, one primary action, and at most one secondary action. A
supporting visual should show the product doing the thing, not an abstract
illustration. Keep the headline to one line on desktop where possible; length is
the enemy of a hero.

Why: attention and comprehension both decay fast above the fold. One idea, one
action concentrates both. Two co-equal CTAs split intent and measurably lower
click-through; make the secondary visibly quieter (ghost or text link).

Example: "Turn one product photo into a week of short-form video" > primary
"Start free", secondary text link "See an example". Counter-example: "Welcome to
the future of content" with three buttons of equal weight and a stock illustration
of people pointing at a laptop.
