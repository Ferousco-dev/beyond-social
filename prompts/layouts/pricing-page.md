Three tiers is the sweet spot: too few removes the anchor, too many induces
choice paralysis. Visually recommend one plan (a subtle highlight and a "Most
popular" label), and make that the plan whose economics you actually want.

Order plans left to right by price so the eye anchors on a higher number first,
which makes the recommended plan feel reasonable. Lead each plan with the value
it unlocks, not a feature dump; put the differentiating features at the top and
shared features below a fold or in a comparison table. Show the annual saving as
a concrete amount, and default the toggle to the framing you want without hiding
the alternative.

Why: anchoring and the compromise effect are real and strong. A visible middle
option framed as the smart default captures the majority who do not want the
cheapest or the most expensive. Dark patterns (pre-checked add-ons, hidden
monthly price, fake scarcity) win once and cost trust forever, so we never use
them.

Example: Starter / Pro (Most popular) / Scale, annual toggle showing "Save
$120/yr", Pro card lifted with a 1px accent border. Counter-example: five nearly
identical tiers, a pre-selected annual plan with the monthly price buried, and a
red "Only 2 seats left" banner.
