Hierarchy is the deliberate ordering of attention. Every screen has one primary
element; if everything is emphasized, nothing is. Establish rank with size,
weight, color, and space, in that rough order of strength, and use the minimum
contrast that makes the order unambiguous.

Practical rules: one clear focal point per view. Group related items and separate
unrelated ones with space, not lines, wherever space suffices. Use a single accent
color for the primary action and resist spending it elsewhere. Increase contrast
to promote, reduce it to demote; a muted secondary is more elegant than a second
loud element.

Why: the eye scans by contrast before it reads. Correct hierarchy lets a user act
without consciously parsing the layout, which is the definition of an effortless
interface. Flat, uniform emphasis forces the reader to do the ranking themselves.

Example: a bold outcome headline, a muted subhead, one filled primary button, and
generous whitespace isolating the call to action. Counter-example: three headings
at the same size, two filled buttons competing, and boxes drawn around every group.
