The first viewport does the conversion work; everything below reinforces. It needs
a clear value statement, one primary action, and one trust signal, and nothing
that competes with them.

Practice: make the primary CTA the highest-contrast element in view and state
exactly what happens on click ("Start free, no card"). Reduce friction, fewer
fields, defer sign-up until after a first win where possible. Add one honest proof
point near the CTA (a recognizable logo row, a real number, a short testimonial),
not a wall of badges. Remove secondary navigation that leaks attention off the
page on paid landing pages.

Why: click-through falls as the number of competing actions rises and as
perceived risk rises. Concentrating attention and lowering risk at the decision
point is the highest-leverage conversion move.

Example: high-contrast "Start free" with "no card needed" beneath and one logo
row. Counter-example: two equal CTAs, a full nav bar, and six trust badges of
unknown origin crowding the button.
