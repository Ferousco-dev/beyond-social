Real testimonial edits cut away from the face constantly; an unbroken talking
head for 20+ seconds is a studio-interview convention, not how creators or
brands actually cut social proof.

- Cut to product-in-use b-roll on the clause before the payoff word lands
  (L-cut: audio continues under the new visual), mirroring how documentary and
  UGC editors hide a jump cut and keep pace.
- Keep one verbal imperfection per clip: a false start, a short "so, um," a
  restart — do not smooth every sentence into a clean read.
- Vary shot scale between the talking segments (close-up hands on product, a
  wider shot of the room) so no two consecutive head shots match in scale,
  which is what disguises a mid-sentence jump cut as an intentional edit.
- Hold b-roll only as long as the sentence needs it, roughly 1.5-3 seconds;
  longer reads as filler, shorter reads as a stock-footage montage.

Why: a face on screen for the full duration of a claim gives the viewer time to
scrutinize it as a performance. Cutting to the product moves the credibility
weight onto something the viewer can verify with their own eyes (the object
actually working) while the voice keeps carrying the claim, and the small
verbal imperfection signals an unscripted human, not a read-through.

Example: "customer speaking on camera, cut to close-up of hands opening the
box on the word 'honestly', audio continues uninterrupted, back to customer
mid-sentence."

Counter-example: a single 30-second locked head shot with no cutaways and a
fully polished, pause-free script — technically easy to generate, but it reads
as a scripted ad read rather than a customer talking.
