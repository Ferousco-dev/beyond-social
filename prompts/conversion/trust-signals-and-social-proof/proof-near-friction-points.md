Proof placed only in the hero answers one objection and misses the others; the
right proof needs to sit next to the specific fear it resolves at each step of
the funnel.

- At the pricing table: comparative or ROI proof ("customers save an average
  of $4,200/year") — the fear here is value, not safety.
- At the signup form: low-commitment proof ("no card required," a review
  citing an easy setup) — the fear here is being trapped or oversharing data.
- At the payment field: security and authority proof (processor badge,
  compliance mark) — the fear here is fraud, not value.
- At the first empty-state or onboarding screen: a quick-win testimonial or
  usage stat ("most teams send their first campaign within 10 minutes") — the
  fear here is wasted setup effort.
- Do not repeat the same proof at every step; a logo row that appears in the
  hero, the pricing page, and the checkout footer stops registering after the
  first exposure and wastes the slot.

Why: objections change as the visitor moves through the funnel — trust in the
company, trust in the value, and trust in the transaction are three different
judgments made at three different moments, and generic proof parked only at
the top does nothing for the two that come later. Placing the right proof at
the right friction point resolves the objection at the exact second it's
being formed.

Example: security badge directly under the card field, a "no card required"
line directly under the email field, an ROI stat directly beside the price.

Counter-example: one large testimonial wall on the homepage and no proof
anywhere near the pricing table or checkout — the visitor's last-mile doubts
go unanswered exactly where they're strongest.
