A named, checkable person behind a product transfers more trust than an
anonymous "our team" — but only if the specifics can actually be checked.

- Use a real name, a real title, and a photo of the actual person, linked to a
  profile a visitor can verify (LinkedIn, a personal site, a prior company).
- Lead with one concrete, specific credential relevant to the product ("spent
  six years building payments infrastructure at [prior company]"), not a
  vague "20 years of experience."
- Write the accompanying quote in first person with a specific detail about
  why the product exists, not a mission-statement paraphrase a copywriter
  could have written for any company.
- If citing press or a podcast appearance, link directly to it; an
  unlinked "as featured on [outlet]" claim next to a founder photo is not
  meaningfully different from making it up.
- Keep the photo un-airbrushed and situational (an actual workspace or event),
  not a heavily retouched studio headshot — over-polish on a founder photo
  reads the same way over-polish on a testimonial does: produced, not real.

Why: anonymity is a well-known trust discount online, because there's no
accountability behind an unnamed claim. A specific, checkable person and a
specific, checkable credential let the visitor do their own verification in
seconds, which is what actually converts skepticism into trust, not the
mere presence of a bio section.

Example: "Photo of the actual founder at a real desk, 'I spent 6 years
running payments infra before building this' with a linked LinkedIn profile."

Counter-example: "Our team of industry experts has 50+ years of combined
experience" next to a stock photo of a smiling person in a blazer — nothing
here can be checked, so nothing here is actually proof.
