An actual screenshot of a tweet or post is more credible than a redrawn quote
in brand type, because the platform chrome around it is itself evidence the
words were posted publicly by someone else.

- Capture the real platform UI: handle, avatar, timestamp, and native font,
  not a redesigned card mimicking the platform's look with the brand's own
  typography swapped in.
- Keep the handle and timestamp visible and legible; cropping them out removes
  exactly the parts that make the screenshot checkable.
- Blur or crop other users' replies and any unrelated personal information
  visible in the same screenshot, without altering the original poster's own
  visible text.
- Prefer a genuinely unprompted post over a post you solicited and then
  screenshotted; if it was solicited, that's fine to use but don't caption it
  as spontaneous praise.
- Never retype a quote into a fake tweet-shaped graphic — this is
  functionally a fabricated testimonial wearing a trust format's clothing,
  and it is checkable by searching the quoted text.

Why: the platform chrome is what turns a quote into evidence — it says "this
exists outside our control and you can go verify it," which a redrawn card
cannot claim no matter how faithfully it mimics the platform's visual style.
The credibility comes from the checkability, not from the resemblance.

Example: an actual cropped screenshot of a tweet, handle and timestamp intact,
with the reply thread blurred out below it.

Counter-example: a stylized graphic that imitates Twitter's card layout with
a made-up handle and a quote nobody actually posted — the moment a reader
notices it isn't a real screenshot, the "proof" becomes evidence of
dishonesty instead.
