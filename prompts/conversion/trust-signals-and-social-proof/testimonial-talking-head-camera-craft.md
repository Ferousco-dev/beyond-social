A testimonial only reads as real if the camera behaves like a person actually
pointed one at another person. The most common tell of a fabricated testimonial
is a shot that is too controlled.

- Interview eye-line: subject looks slightly off-lens (5-15 degrees), as if
  talking to an interviewer beside the camera, not dead into the lens.
- Framing: medium close-up, chest-up, with headroom that is not perfectly
  centered; a small, natural crop asymmetry beats a locked-center frame.
- Lens behavior: shallow depth of field with real falloff on the background,
  35-50mm full-frame-equivalent field of view, not a flattened wide angle.
- Camera motion: minute handheld drift or a slow breathing zoom rather than a
  mathematically static lock; a tripod shot with zero micro-movement reads as
  synthetic.
- Light: one motivated source (window, lamp, practical) with visible falloff
  and a soft shadow side, not flat, shadowless front lighting from a ring light.

Why: viewers pattern-match "produced" versus "captured." Perfect symmetry,
dead-center eye contact, and shadowless light are efficient for a model to
generate but are exactly the cues a trained eye (and increasingly an untrained
one) reads as staged or AI-made, which collapses the credibility the
testimonial exists to build.

Example: "medium close-up, subject looking slightly off-camera toward an
interviewer, window light from camera-left with soft falloff, slight handheld
drift, 40mm equivalent, shallow depth of field on a real kitchen background."

Counter-example: "perfectly centered subject, direct eye contact with lens,
flat even studio lighting, locked-off symmetrical frame" — technically clean,
but it reads as a produced ad, not a customer, and undercuts the trust the
format is supposed to earn.
