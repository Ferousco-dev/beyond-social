A struck-through reference price next to the real price changes how the real
price gets judged, because buyers evaluate prices relative to a nearby number,
not in isolation.

- Place the crossed-out price directly beside the live price, same line, so the
  eye compares both in one fixation instead of hunting for context.
- Use a real anchor: a previous list price, an honest annual-total-versus-monthly
  comparison, or a public competitor price. Never invent a number.
- Keep the anchor plausible, roughly 20-40% above the real price. Anchors that
  look manufactured get mentally discounted and drag down trust in the rest of
  the page, not just that one number.
- Anchor once per plan. Repeating a strikethrough on every tier stops reading as
  a genuine discount and starts reading as a template gimmick.
- Pair the anchor with a reason the price changed ("early adopter rate", "annual
  plan"), so the discount has a cause, not just a coincidence.

Why: prospect theory says people weigh gains and losses against a reference
point, not against absolute value. A visible higher anchor reframes the actual
price as a gain (a discount) rather than a cost, which lowers perceived risk at
the moment of decision. The effect collapses, and can reverse, the instant the
anchor looks fabricated, because credibility on one number is read as a signal
about every other number on the page.

Example: "$49/mo ~~$79/mo~~ — early adopter rate, billed annually."
Counter-example: "$19/mo ~~$199/mo~~" with no explanation. A 10x anchor with no
stated reason reads as manipulative and undermines trust in every other claim
on the page.
