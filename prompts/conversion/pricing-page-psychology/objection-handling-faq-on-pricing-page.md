A pricing page that only shows tiers and features leaves the buyer's real
objections unanswered at the exact moment those objections are strongest; a
short FAQ placed below the tiers, not linked off to a help center, closes that
gap before it becomes a lost tab.

- Write each question in the buyer's actual words, pulled from real sales
  calls or support tickets ("Can I switch plans later?", "What happens to my
  data if I cancel?"), not the vendor's internal terminology.
- Order questions by objection frequency, not alphabetically or by internal
  logic; the first two or three questions should be the ones that most often
  precede a lost sale.
- Keep answers to two or three sentences and link out only for genuinely deep
  topics (a security whitepaper, a full SLA document); a long answer on the
  pricing page itself defeats the purpose of keeping the buyer there.
- Include at least one question that addresses cancellation or downgrade
  directly; buyers who cannot find an easy answer to "how do I leave" often
  read that absence as a sign leaving will be made deliberately hard.

Why: unanswered objections do not go away when they are left off the page, they
just get resolved off-page, via a support chat, a competitor's page, or
abandonment, all of which cost more than answering the question where it
arose. Placing the FAQ directly below the pricing table, rather than behind a
help-center link, keeps the buyer in a single continuous decision flow instead
of asking them to leave the page they were about to convert on.

Example: "Can I cancel anytime? Yes, cancel from your account settings with no
retention flow or phone call required. You keep access until the end of your
billing period."
Counter-example: a "Have more questions? Visit our Help Center" link with no
questions shown, sending an about-to-convert buyer away from the page entirely.
