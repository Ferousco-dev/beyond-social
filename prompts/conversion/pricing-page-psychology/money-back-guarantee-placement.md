A money-back guarantee reduces perceived risk at the exact moment of decision
only if it is visible at that moment; buried in a footer or a separate terms
page, it does none of the psychological work it is capable of.

- Put the guarantee within the same eye span as the CTA button, either
  directly beneath it or as a small line adjacent, not scrolled away in a
  policies section.
- State the mechanism, not just the promise: "30-day money-back, no questions
  asked, refunded to your original payment method" beats "satisfaction
  guaranteed," because the specific process reduces the buyer's remaining
  uncertainty about how a refund would actually work.
- Use a small trust icon (shield, checkmark) next to the text, sized to
  support the claim, not to dominate the CTA area; an oversized badge reads as
  compensating for a weak product.
- Keep the guarantee period proportionate to the buying cycle: shorter for
  low-commitment monthly plans, longer for annual commitments where the buyer
  needs more time to judge fit before the window closes.

Why: risk reversal works by moving the financial downside of a wrong decision
from the buyer to the seller, and that only lowers the buyer's felt risk if it
is processed at the same time as the price and the CTA, in the same glance.
Cognitive research on decision-point framing shows that risk-reducing
information loses most of its effect when it is temporally or spatially
separated from the decision itself, because the buyer has already resolved
their risk assessment (often by leaving) before they encounter it.

Example: "Upgrade to Pro — $49/mo" button with "30-day money-back guarantee,
refunded automatically" in smaller text directly below it.
Counter-example: the same CTA with guarantee details only in a "Refund Policy"
link in the site footer, seven scrolls away from the price.
