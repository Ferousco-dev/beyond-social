The plan card with the most visual weight gets evaluated first and remembered
best, regardless of price, because pre-attentive visual processing (size,
contrast, elevation) happens before the buyer reads a single word of the
feature list.

- Scale the recommended card 5-10% larger than its neighbors, or lift it on a
  subtle shadow/elevation so it visually sits above the row rather than in
  it; either cue is enough, stacking three or more starts to look like
  desperation rather than design.
- Give the recommended card's border or top accent a color pulled from the
  brand's primary action color, the same hue used on the CTA button elsewhere
  on the site, so the emphasis reads as consistent with the rest of the
  product, not as a one-off pricing-page trick.
- Keep the non-recommended cards fully legible and equally easy to select;
  visual emphasis should nudge attention, not degrade the other options to the
  point that comparison feels rigged.
- On mobile, where cards stack vertically instead of sitting side by side,
  preserve the emphasis by keeping the recommended card first in scroll order
  or by adding a sticky label, since scale and elevation cues read far weaker
  in a single column.

Why: the visual system (size, contrast, depth, color) is processed in parallel
and faster than reading, so it sets the buyer's starting hypothesis about which
option matters before the content-level argument (features, price) even
begins. That head start compounds with the badge and default-selection effects
rather than replacing them, but only if the emphasis is legible without also
making the other options feel punished, which triggers reactance instead of
preference.

Example: the "Pro" card raised 12px on a soft shadow, 6% wider than Starter and
Team, with a 2px top border in the same blue as the site's primary CTA.
Counter-example: the recommended card scaled up, shadowed, badged, and
color-inverted all at once while the other two cards are rendered in flat gray
with no border, making the comparison feel pre-decided rather than presented.
