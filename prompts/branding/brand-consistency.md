A brand is a system, not a logo. Consistency across color, type, spacing, imagery,
and voice is what makes separate outputs feel like one product. When a brand is
supplied, treat its tokens as constraints, not suggestions.

Practice: use the brand's exact color roles, type families, and radius/spacing
scale. Keep voice consistent (formal vs. playful, terse vs. warm) across headline,
body, and microcopy. Reuse the brand's component patterns rather than inventing
new ones per screen. When the brand is silent on something, choose the most
neutral option that fits the existing system rather than introducing a new
direction.

Why: recognition and trust are built by repetition. Inconsistency, even when each
piece is attractive, reads as unpolished and erodes the brand faster than a plainer
but coherent system.

Example: every surface uses the brand's neutral ramp, single accent, and one
type family, with the same button shape throughout. Counter-example: each screen
picks its own accent, radius, and tone because it looked good in isolation.
