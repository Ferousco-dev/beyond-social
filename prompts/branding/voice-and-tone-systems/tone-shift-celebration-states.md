Success moments are where a brand is allowed to spend its personality budget,
but the spend should be proportional to what the user actually accomplished,
not a fixed maximum every time.

- Scale energy to the size of the win: a first successful action gets warm
  encouragement, a major milestone (first paid conversion, a big export) earns
  the brand's biggest tonal move — save the confetti-equivalent language for
  genuinely rare moments so it doesn't get diluted by daily use.
- Credit the user's action, not the product's cleverness — "you shipped your
  first campaign," not "we made it easy for you to ship."
- Keep celebration copy short; a long paragraph after a win reads as the
  brand making the moment about itself.
- Allow contractions, exclamation points, and one well-placed piece of
  personality per message — but cap it at one, stacking multiple flourishes
  reads as forced enthusiasm.
- Avoid generic superlatives ("amazing," "awesome," "incredible") — name the
  specific thing that happened instead of grading it.

Why: over-celebrating routine actions trains users to tune out the brand's
enthusiasm entirely, so the tone has no reserve left for moments that
actually deserve it. Naming the specific accomplishment instead of praising
it in the abstract also reads as the brand paying attention, which is itself
a form of warmth that generic praise can't deliver.

Example: "Your first campaign is live. 12 shots rendered, ready to publish."
— specific, credits the user, one clean beat of energy.

Counter-example: "AMAZING JOB!! You're absolutely CRUSHING it! 🎉🎉🎉" after a
routine save — disproportionate energy for a low-stakes action burns the
brand's celebratory register, so it has nothing left for a real milestone.
