Money moments — checkout, plan changes, failed charges, refunds — need the
brand's most literal, least stylized tone in the entire product, because
ambiguity here has a real financial consequence, not just a UX one.

- State amounts, currencies, billing dates, and recurrence explicitly in
  every message that touches money — never rely on the user inferring a
  number from context.
- Use plain declarative sentences, not questions or exclamations: "your card
  was charged $29.00 on Aug 21" not "all set — you're charged $29!"
- Do not use humor, wordplay, or brand slang anywhere near a price, a charge,
  or a cancellation — even mild levity next to a dollar figure reads as the
  brand not being serious about the user's money.
- Front-load the number and the consequence before any explanatory or
  reassuring text — a user scanning for the amount shouldn't have to read a
  full paragraph to find it.
- For failed payments, separate the fact (charge failed) from the fix (update
  card) into two short, sequential sentences rather than one blended one —
  this keeps the failure legible even to a skimming, stressed user.

Why: users read financial copy differently than any other copy in a
product — they check it for exact numbers and consequences, not for
personality, so any stylistic flourish becomes noise that has to be parsed
around, and any ambiguity reads as either a mistake or something being
hidden. Literalness here isn't a lack of brand voice; it's the register the
brand's directness trait was defined for.

Example: "Payment failed: your card ending 4412 was declined for the $49.00
renewal. Update your card to keep your plan active."

Counter-example: "Uh-oh, your card said no! 💳 Let's get that sorted." —
the informality obscures the specific amount and account impact, and reads
as glib about something the user may be financially anxious about.
