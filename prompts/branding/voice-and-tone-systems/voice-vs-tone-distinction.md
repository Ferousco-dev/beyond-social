A brand has one voice (fixed personality and vocabulary) but many tones (how that
voice modulates for the emotional weight of a moment). Treating them as the same
thing either freezes the brand into one flat register or lets it drift into a
different persona per screen.

- Define voice as traits with stated opposites — "direct, not blunt," "warm, not
  cute" — so writers know the boundary, not just a vague target adjective.
- Define tone as independent dials: formality, energy, warmth. These move per
  context; voice traits do not move.
- Never let tone swap the vocabulary the brand has claimed — product names, verb
  choices for core actions ("send," never "fire off" in one place and "launch"
  in another) stay fixed in every state.
- Write tone rules as if/then pairs: "if the user just lost work, energy drops
  near zero and warmth rises," not "be empathetic sometimes."

Why: users experience a product as one continuous relationship across an
emotional journey — signup, a slow load, an error, a win. If voice mutates
between those moments it reads as impersonation, a different writer each
screen. If tone never adapts, the brand reads as tone-deaf exactly when the
user is most attentive to how it's talking to them. Separating the two lets a
team of writers stay consistent without a style guide turning into a
script for every possible screen.

Example: "Payment failed. Check your card details and try again." — same
direct, plain-verb voice as the rest of the product, tone pulled down to
low energy and no embellishment because money and failure are in the room.

Counter-example: "Oopsie! Something broke on our end lol, mind trying again?"
— voice itself changed into a cuter persona just to soften the moment,
which reads as evasive precisely when the user needs the brand to be
straight with them.
