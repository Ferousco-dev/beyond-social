Onboarding tone should move deliberately across the sequence — from
orientation to instruction to encouragement — rather than holding one
register from the welcome screen to the first real task.

- Screen one (orientation): warmest, most brand-personality-forward point in
  the whole product — this is a first impression, spend some of the budget
  here.
- Middle screens (setup, permissions, configuration): tone flattens toward
  neutral-clear; these are instructional moments where cleverness competes
  with comprehension and comprehension should win.
- Final screen (first real action available): tone lifts again, action-
  oriented and specific about what happens next, not just congratulatory
  about having finished setup.
- Track tone as a deliberate curve across the whole flow (a simple high-
  medium-high shape works for most onboarding), and review it as one
  document, not screen by screen — a reviewer catching only individual
  screens will approve a flow that reads as flat or erratic end to end.
- Never let permission-request screens (camera, notifications, payment)
  carry celebratory tone — these are trust asks, not wins, and treating them
  as delight moments manipulates rather than informs.

Why: users experience onboarding as one continuous sequence, so a tone that's
locally fine on each screen can still read as directionless or manic in
aggregate if it isn't authored with the arc in mind. A deliberate curve gives
the sequence narrative shape instead of a string of independently-approved
screens.

Example: "Welcome — let's get your first video made." (warm) → "Connect your
brand assets so renders match your look." (neutral, instructional) → "You're
set. Generate your first video." (lifted, action-forward).

Counter-example: maximum enthusiasm on every screen including the payment-
details step — sustained peak energy reads as pressure exactly where the
user most needs calm, unhurried clarity.
